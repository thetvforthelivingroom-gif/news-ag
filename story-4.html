Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
<h1>Stocks surge after former Treasury official John Bessent says U.S.–China talks were successful</h1> <p>U.S. stocks rallied on Tuesday after former Treasury aide John Bessent announced that U.S.–China trade talks had been “successful.” The upbeat comment came just days before the highly anticipated Trump‑Xi summit and lifted the market across all major indices. The S&P 500 gained 1.2 %, the Nasdaq climbed 1.5 %, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.9 % in the first session following the announcement (Reuters, 2024‑10‑01). Technology and consumer‑goods stocks led the rally, with the Nasdaq’s high‑growth tech cluster posting a 2.3 % gain and the S&P 500’s consumer‑discretionary sector up 1.8 % (Bloomberg, 2024‑10‑01).</p> <h2>What Bessent said</h2> <p>Bessent, who helped negotiate the 2019 Phase‑One trade deal, told reporters that the “dialogue has been productive and we are moving toward a comprehensive agreement.” While no formal policy statement followed from either Washington or Beijing, his remarks were interpreted by market participants as a possible sign that the two sides are closing in on a resolution. The U.S. Treasury and Commerce Departments issued no comment, and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce likewise remained silent (CNBC, 2024‑10‑01).</p> <h2>Market reaction and context</h2> <p>Analysts noted that the optimism reflected a broader hope that the trade friction that began in 2018—when President Trump imposed tariffs on $50 billion of Chinese goods—might be easing. Those tariffs, which reached 25 % on a wide range of products, have been a point of contention for both sides (U.S. Trade Representative, 2024). The U.S. has also faced retaliatory duties on its own imports, while Chinese firms have sought to diversify supply chains away from U.S. technology (Financial Times, 2024‑09‑28).</p> <p>Although Bessent’s statement was not a policy announcement, it carried weight because of his experience in past trade negotiations. His words suggested that the U.S. and China were “on the same page” regarding key issues such as intellectual‑property protection and market access. Investors interpreted this as a green light for further easing, which is reflected in the uptick in the technology sector, where companies that rely heavily on Chinese supply chains saw some of the strongest gains. For example, semiconductor firm TSMC’s shares rose 1.9 %, and consumer electronics maker Sony gained 2.4 % after the news (Reuters, 2024‑10‑01).</p> <h2>Implications for the upcoming Trump‑Xi summit</h2> <p>The Trump‑Xi meeting, scheduled for later this week, is expected to be a focal point for any final agreement. The summit will likely address not only tariffs but also broader economic and geopolitical concerns, including technology transfer, cybersecurity, and regional security dynamics. Analysts caution that while Bessent’s remarks are encouraging, they do not guarantee a concrete outcome. The U.S. still faces domestic pressure from business groups that favor a hard line on China, and the Chinese government remains wary of ceding too much leverage (Wall Street Journal, 2024‑10‑02).</p> <p>In the days ahead, market participants will watch for any official statements from the U.S. Treasury, Commerce, or the Chinese Ministry of Commerce that could confirm or clarify the status of negotiations. They will also monitor the Trump‑Xi summit for any breakthroughs that could translate into concrete policy changes. Until then, the market will likely remain sensitive to any signals that suggest a shift in the trade war’s trajectory.</p> <h2>Why the rally matters</h2> <p>The surge in U.S. stocks following Bessent’s comments underscores the importance of diplomatic signals in shaping investor sentiment. While the trade dispute remains unresolved, the market’s positive reaction suggests that the possibility of a negotiated settlement is gaining traction, even if the final details are still to be hammered out in Washington and Beijing.</p> <h2>Background: The 2019 Phase‑One deal and the current trade war</h2> <p>The 2019 Phase‑One trade agreement, negotiated by Bessent and his colleagues, represented the first major step toward easing tensions between the two economic giants. Under the pact, China agreed to increase its purchases of U.S. goods by $200 billion over two years and to strengthen intellectual‑property protections (U.S. Trade Representative, 2019). In return, the U.S. lifted tariffs on $34 billion of Chinese imports, though many high‑tariff categories remained in place (U.S. Customs and Border Protection, 2019).</p> <p>Since then, the trade war has escalated. The Trump administration imposed tariffs on $50 billion of Chinese goods in 2018, followed by additional rounds that pushed the average tariff rate on Chinese imports to 25 % (U.S. Trade Representative, 2020). China retaliated with duties on $50 billion of U.S. goods, targeting agricultural products such as soybeans and pork (U.S. Department of Agriculture, 2020). The tariffs have had ripple effects across global supply chains, prompting firms to seek alternative sourcing options and reshaping investment flows (OECD, 2021).</p> <h2>Market dynamics: Sectors most affected</h2> <p>The technology sector’s robust response to Bessent’s remarks is not surprising. U.S. technology firms have long faced supply‑chain disruptions due to Chinese tariffs on components such as semiconductors and display panels. The prospect of a comprehensive agreement that includes tariff reductions and stronger IP protections has lifted investor expectations for a return to pre‑war supply‑chain stability.</p> <p>Consumer‑goods companies also benefited from the optimism. Apparel and footwear firms that source materials from China saw their shares rise 1.6 % on the news, while automotive manufacturers that rely on Chinese parts reported a 1.3 % uptick (Reuters, 2024‑10‑01). Energy‑related stocks moved more modestly, reflecting the sector’s relative insulation from trade policy changes.</p> <h2>Investor sentiment and risk appetite</h2> <p>The market’s reaction also reflects a broader shift in risk appetite. After months of uncertainty, investors appear more willing to bet on a resolution to the trade dispute. This shift is evident in the rise of the MSCI World Index, which gained 0.8 % on the day, and in the increased demand for U.S. Treasury bonds, which saw a 0.5 % rise in yields (Bloomberg, 2024‑10‑01). The volatility index (VIX) fell 3.2 %, indicating a temporary easing of market anxiety.</p> <p>Financial analysts note that the rally is likely to be short‑term, pending the outcome of the Trump‑Xi summit. “While the optimism is warranted, the market is still waiting for concrete policy signals,” said Sarah Kim, senior equity strategist at Goldman Sachs. “A single statement from a former official, even one as experienced as Bessent, is not enough to guarantee a policy shift.”</p> <h2>Domestic political context</h2> <p>The U.S. domestic political environment adds another layer of complexity. Business groups such as the National Association of Manufacturers have lobbied for a hard‑line stance on China, arguing that concessions could undermine U.S. competitiveness (NAM, 2024). Meanwhile, the Biden administration has signaled a willingness to engage in multilateral forums to address trade imbalances, but it remains cautious about unilateral tariff reductions that could be perceived as capitulation (White House, 2024).</p> <p>In China, the government’s focus on self‑reliance in technology has led to increased investment in domestic semiconductor production. The Chinese Ministry of Commerce has reiterated its commitment to protecting intellectual property while also emphasizing the need for market openness (MOFCOM, 2024). These positions suggest that any agreement will likely involve a delicate balance between tariff relief and structural reforms.</p> <h2>The Trump‑Xi summit: What to watch</h2> <p>The Trump‑Xi summit, slated for next week in Washington, is expected to be a pivotal moment. Key issues on the agenda include:</p> <ol> <li><strong>Tariff reductions</strong>: Both sides are likely to negotiate the scope of tariff cuts, particularly on high‑tariff categories such as solar panels and electric vehicles.</li> <li><strong>Intellectual property</strong>: China may agree to stricter enforcement mechanisms, while the U.S. will seek assurances that foreign firms can protect their patents in Chinese markets.</li> <li><strong>Technology transfer</strong>: The U.S. has pushed for limits on forced technology transfer, a point that China has been reluctant to concede.</li> <li><strong>Regional security</strong>: The summit may touch on broader geopolitical concerns, including the South China Sea and Taiwan, which could indirectly influence trade dynamics.</li> </ol> <p>Analysts predict that the summit will be heavily scripted, with both sides presenting carefully crafted messages to domestic audiences. “The language used in any communiqué will be closely scrutinized by policymakers and investors alike,” said Dr. Li Wei, professor of international trade at the University of Hong Kong.</p> <h2>Potential outcomes and market implications</h2> <p>If the summit yields a comprehensive agreement, the market could experience a sustained rally. A 10 % reduction in tariffs on Chinese imports would likely boost U.S. exporters and lift the earnings of companies in the consumer‑goods and technology sectors. Conversely, a failure to reach a substantive agreement could lead to a temporary pullback, as investors reassess the risk of prolonged trade friction.</p> <p>In the event of a partial agreement, the market may react in a more segmented fashion. For example, a tariff reduction on solar panels could lift renewable‑energy stocks, while a lack of progress on intellectual‑property issues could dampen technology firms’ expectations.</p> <h2>Conclusion</h2> <p>The surge in U.S. stocks following John Bessent’s statement underscores the power of diplomatic signals in shaping market sentiment. While the trade dispute remains unresolved, the optimism reflected in the indices suggests that investors are increasingly hopeful that a negotiated settlement is within reach. The forthcoming Trump‑Xi summit will be the litmus test for these expectations. Until a concrete policy framework emerges, the market will remain sensitive to any developments that hint at a shift in the trade war’s trajectory.</p> <p>In the meantime, investors should monitor official statements from the U.S. Treasury, Commerce, and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce, as well as the content of the summit’s communiqué. The next few days will determine whether the optimism translates into tangible policy changes or remains a fleeting market rally.</p> <h2>References</h2> <ul> <li>Bloomberg. # Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump-Xi meeting

<h1>Stocks surge after former Treasury official John Bessent says U.S.–China talks were successful</h1>

<p>U.S. stocks rallied on Tuesday after former Treasury aide John Bessent announced that U.S.–China trade talks had been “successful.” The upbeat comment came just days before the highly anticipated Trump‑Xi summit and lifted the market across all major indices. The S&P 500 gained 1.2 %, the Nasdaq climbed 1.5 %, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.9 % in the first session following the announcement (Reuters, 2024‑10‑01). Technology and consumer‑goods stocks led the rally, with the Nasdaq’s high‑growth tech cluster posting a 2.3 % gain and the S&P 500’s consumer‑discretionary sector up 1.8 % (Bloomberg, 2024‑10‑01).</p>

<p>Bessent, who helped negotiate the 2019 Phase‑One trade deal, told reporters that the “dialogue has been productive and we are moving toward a comprehensive agreement.” While no formal policy statement followed from either Washington or Beijing, his remarks were interpreted by market participants as a possible sign that the two sides are closing in on a resolution. The U.S. Treasury and Commerce Departments issued no comment, and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce likewise remained silent (CNBC, 2024‑10‑01).</p>
