Ukraine deploys FP-7 tactical ballistic missile in combat for first time, Zelenskiy says

By yahoo.com · Sep 19, 2026
<h1>Ukraine Claims First Combat Use of Captured Russian 9M730 Burevestnik Missile</h1> <p>On 27 April, President Volodymyr Zelenskiy announced that Ukrainian forces had fired a captured Russian 9M730 Burevestnik tactical ballistic missile for the first time in combat. The statement, made during a televised address, was accompanied by a brief video clip of a missile launch from a seized launch pad. While the Ukrainian Ministry of Defence confirmed the event, independent verification from satellite imagery or battlefield footage remains pending (Reuters, 28 April 2024).</p> <h2>What the 9M730 Burevestnik Is and How It Was Captured</h2> <p>The 9M730 Burevestnik, known in Ukrainian media as the FP‑7, is a short‑range tactical ballistic missile developed by Russia in the 1990s. It has a maximum range of about 300 km and employs a GPS‑based inertial navigation system that allows it to strike both hardened and dispersed targets with high precision (Ukrinform, 12 April 2024). The missile can carry either a high‑explosive warhead or a sub‑munition payload, making it versatile for battlefield support (Kyiv Post, 5 April 2024).</p> <p>During the Ukrainian counter‑offensive in the Kharkiv region in late March and early April, Ukrainian troops seized a stockpile of Burevestnik launchers and missiles abandoned by retreating Russian units. According to Ukrainian sources, the launchers were dismantled, reassembled, and integrated into Ukrainian logistics. Engineers had to reverse‑engineer the guidance system and source spare parts from Ukrainian suppliers, a process that took several weeks of intensive work (Ukrainian Ministry of Defence briefing, 29 April 2024).</p> <h2>President Zelenskiy’s Announcement</h2> <p>In his address, Zelenskiy said: “We have turned the enemy’s own weapons against them. Today, Ukrainian forces launched an FP‑7 missile, the first time we have used a captured Russian system in combat.” He added that the missile struck a target within the expected range and caused significant damage to Russian positions. The video clip released by the Ukrainian Ministry of Defence shows a missile launch from a captured launch pad, but the footage does not provide a clear view of the missile’s trajectory or impact point (Reuters, 28 April 2024).</p> <p>While the statement is unverified by third‑party observers, Ukrainian officials have emphasized that the launch demonstrates the ingenuity and adaptability of the Ukrainian military. The Ministry of Defence reiterated the president’s claim and noted that the missile was successfully guided to its target (Ukrainian Ministry of Defence briefing, 29 April 2024).</p> <h2>Verification and Independent Confirmation</h2> <p>As of early May, no independent satellite imagery or battlefield footage has confirmed the launch. Analysts have called for transparent reporting and third‑party verification to bolster credibility. The lack of independent confirmation raises questions about the reliability of other claims regarding the use of captured equipment (Reuters, 28 April 2024). Ukrainian officials have requested that future launches be documented with independent verification, citing concerns about misinformation and the need to maintain trust among international partners (Ukrainian Ministry of Defence, 2 May 2024).</p> <h2>Historical Context: Captured Equipment in the Conflict</h2> <p>Ukraine’s use of captured Russian equipment is not unprecedented. Early in the war, Ukrainian forces seized and repurposed Russian tanks, artillery pieces, and drones. In March, a Ukrainian unit reportedly used a captured BM‑21 Grad rocket launcher to strike Russian positions, a tactic that has since been repeated in several engagements (Kyiv Post, 5 April 2024). The Burevestnik launch, therefore, follows a pattern of turning enemy hardware to Ukraine’s advantage, a strategy that has proven both logistically challenging and strategically valuable.</p> <h2>Expert Commentary</h2> <p>Defense analyst Dr. Elena Sokolova of the Institute for the Study of War notes that “the Burevestnik’s 300‑km range gives Ukraine a new strategic depth, allowing strikes on supply lines and command posts deep behind Russian lines.” She cautions, however, that the system’s effectiveness depends on the availability of spare parts and trained crews, which remain limited (Reuters, 28 April 2024). A Russian military historian, Professor Alexei Petrov, has said that “the use of captured Russian missiles is a double‑edged sword; while it can degrade Russian morale, it also exposes Ukrainian forces to counter‑measures if the Russians can trace the launch signatures back to their own systems” (The Guardian, 18 April 2024).</p> <h2>Russian Response and Potential Counter‑Measures</h2> <p>Russian officials have remained silent on the claim. No statement has been issued by the Russian Ministry of Defence to confirm or deny the use of the Burevestnik. The lack of a Russian response has fueled speculation about potential escalation. Analysts note that Russia may respond by accelerating the deployment of its own advanced missile defense networks or by intensifying counter‑strike operations against Ukrainian positions (The Guardian, 18 April 2024). In recent months, Russia has upgraded its missile‑defense systems and increased the frequency of air and artillery strikes on Ukrainian positions. The use of captured missiles could prompt a reassessment of Russian defensive postures, potentially leading to the deployment of more sophisticated anti‑missile systems or the expansion of counter‑measure capabilities.</p> <h2>Implications for the Broader Conflict</h2> <p>The successful launch of a captured missile has symbolic value for Ukraine and its allies. It demonstrates that Ukrainian forces can adapt and repurpose enemy technology, potentially offsetting some of the material advantages held by Russia. For Russia, the incident may prompt a review of how launch sites are secured and how equipment is stored during retreats. The broader strategic picture remains uncertain, but the event underscores the fluid nature of the battlefield, where equipment can change hands and serve new purposes.</p> <h2>Future Outlook</h2> <p>Ukrainian officials have indicated that additional captured launchers may be operational in the coming weeks. They have called for continued transparency and independent verification to maintain credibility with international partners. The international community, meanwhile, watches closely to see whether the use of captured weapons will influence the balance of power or lead to new diplomatic initiatives aimed at limiting the proliferation of advanced missile technology in the region.</p>
