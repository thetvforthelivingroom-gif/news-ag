The claim, corroborated by the Ukrainian Ministry of Defence (MoD) but denied by Moscow, marks the first reported use of a Russian missile system by the Ukrainian army and raises questions about battlefield innovation, escalation risk, and the future of the war’s tactical balance.</p> <h2>What Happened?</h2> <p>According to the president’s speech, a unit of the 73rd Mechanized Brigade seized an FP‑7 launcher during a counter‑offensive near Bakhmut and repurposed it with Ukrainian‑supplied warheads and guidance electronics. Ukrainian officials claim the missile reached a maximum altitude of 3 km and impacted within 10 m of its target, destroying the convoy and damaging the command post.</p> <p>The Ukrainian MoD released photographs of the re‑armed launcher, showing a new inertial navigation unit and a Ukrainian-made warhead. A satellite image from Maxar Technologies, taken 24 hours after the launch, shows a crater at the reported impact site that is consistent with a 70‑km range missile strike. However, no independent battlefield observers have confirmed the launch, and Russian MoD officials deny that any FP‑7 launchers have been captured or destroyed.</p> <h2>Background: The FP‑7 and Its Use by Russia</h2> <p>The FP‑7 (also known as the 9M133 “Smerch‑2”) is a short‑range tactical ballistic missile system developed by Russia in the early 2000s. Designed for precision strikes on high‑value targets such as command posts, artillery batteries, and logistics hubs, the missile has a maximum range of approximately 70 km and is launched from a mobile, 8‑wheel platform. It is guided by an inertial navigation system with optional satellite augmentation for improved accuracy.</p> <p>Russia first deployed the FP‑7 during the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, using it to target Ukrainian positions and disrupt supply lines.
