Ukraine deploys FP-7 tactical ballistic missile in combat for first time, Zelenskiy says

By yahoo.com · Sep 19, 2026
<article> Ukraine’s deployment of the FP‑7 tactical ballistic missile in combat for the first time marks a significant shift in the country’s military strategy, according to President Volodymyr Zelenskiy. The announcement, made during a televised briefing, follows a series of reports that the missile—an indigenous system with a range of roughly 150 km—was fired at a Russian‑occupied target near Kharkiv. The move signals Ukraine’s growing confidence in its domestic defense industry amid a war that has stretched both its resources and its international support.

What happened

President Zelenskiy revealed that Ukrainian forces had successfully launched an FP‑7 missile during an operation in the Kharkiv region, the first combat use of the system since its development. The missile struck a Russian‑controlled logistics hub, reportedly destroying a supply convoy and disrupting enemy communications. Zelenskiy described the deployment as a “military milestone” that demonstrates the effectiveness of Ukraine’s home‑grown weapons program [2]. The launch was confirmed by the Ukrainian Ministry of Defence, which said the FP‑7 was fired from a mobile launch platform positioned within 20 km of the target zone. The operation was carried out under the cover of darkness to minimize detection by Russian air defenses.

The FP‑7 is a tactical ballistic missile developed by Ukrainian defense contractors, featuring a solid‑fuel engine and GPS‑based guidance. While the system was originally intended for use in training exercises, the recent combat deployment underscores its operational readiness. According to the Ministry of Defence, the missile’s 150‑km range allows Ukrainian units to strike deep into Russian‑occupied territory, providing a strategic advantage in targeting supply lines and command posts. The successful strike also validates the missile’s accuracy, which is reported to be within a 10‑meter circular error probable (CEP) under optimal conditions [2].

The Ukrainian Armed Forces have been integrating the FP‑7 into their broader missile arsenal, which includes Western‑supplied systems such as the HIMARS and ATACMS. The decision to field the FP‑7 reflects a dual objective: to diversify Ukraine’s offensive capabilities and to reduce dependence on foreign aid for critical weaponry. The missile’s deployment also serves as a morale booster for Ukrainian troops, who have endured prolonged combat operations against a numerically superior adversary. The Ministry of Defence stated that the FP‑7 will be used in future operations to target high‑value enemy assets, thereby enhancing Ukraine’s deterrence posture [3].

Background and context

The conflict that began in February 2022 has exposed gaps in Ukraine’s defense infrastructure, prompting a rapid expansion of domestic weapons production. Prior to the war, Ukraine’s missile industry was limited to a handful of systems, many of which were outdated or lacked the range needed to counter Russian advances. The FP‑7 emerged from a national initiative to develop a self‑sufficient missile capability, with funding from the Ukrainian government and support from international partners. The project, which began in 2018, aimed to produce a missile that could match the performance of Western systems while remaining affordable for Ukraine’s defense budget [3].

The FP‑7’s development was accelerated by the urgent need to counter Russian missile strikes on Ukrainian cities. Ukrainian officials have highlighted the missile’s role in providing a counter‑offensive option that can be deployed quickly and with minimal logistical support. The system’s solid‑fuel propulsion allows for rapid launch, while its GPS guidance ensures precision strikes against moving targets. The Ministry of Defence has emphasized that the FP‑7 complements existing Western‑supplied systems, creating a layered missile strategy that can adapt to evolving battlefield conditions [2].

Internationally, the FP‑7 deployment has been viewed as a demonstration of Ukraine’s resilience and ingenuity. Western allies, particularly the United States and members of the European Union, have praised Ukraine’s efforts to build a robust domestic defense industry. In a statement, the U.S. Department of Defense noted that Ukraine’s indigenous missile programs “enhance the overall security architecture of the region” and “reduce the burden on allied supply chains” [2]. The European Union has also expressed support for Ukraine’s missile development, citing the importance of self‑sufficiency in the face of geopolitical uncertainty.
