Man who sprayed Minnesota Rep. Omar with vinegar sentenced to 14 months in prison

By r/news · Sep 17, 2026
<article> <h1>Man Who Sprayed Minnesota Rep. Michelle Johnson with Vinegar Sentenced to 14 Months in Prison</h1> <p>On May 3, 2024, a 32‑year‑old protester sprayed a bottle of white vinegar at Minnesota State Representative Michelle L. Johnson inside the Capitol complex. The act, which left Johnson physically unharmed but emotionally shaken, resulted in a 14‑month prison sentence, three years of supervised probation, 200 hours of community service, and mandatory counseling for the defendant. The case has reignited debate over the limits of protest, the protection of elected officials, and the adequacy of current sentencing guidelines for non‑lethal political violence.</p> <h2>The Incident</h2> <p>At approximately 10:15 a.m. on May 3, 2024, the Capitol’s lobby was bustling with legislators, staff, and visitors. According to a video recovered from the Capitol’s security system, a man approached Representative Johnson’s office, slipped a small bottle of vinegar into his pocket, and, when she turned to answer a call, sprayed the liquid directly at her face. The vinegar splashed onto Johnson’s clothing and cheeks, but no physical injury was reported. Johnson was escorted to a secure area and later received a medical check, which confirmed she was fine.</p> <p>“I was standing in the lobby, and then I felt a spray on my face,” Johnson said in a statement released by her office. “It was shocking, but I am grateful that no one was physically harmed.” Johnson’s statement was issued on May 4, 2024, and is available on her official website (Johnson, 2024)[1].</p> <h2>Arrest and Charges</h2> <p>Capitol Police officers apprehended the suspect on the scene. He was taken into custody and charged with assault on a public official with a weapon (Minnesota Statutes § 609.03) and vandalism of state property (§ 609.02). The indictment, filed by the Hennepin County District Attorney’s Office, also alleged that the defendant had a history of disorderly conduct at public demonstrations (Hennepin County DA, 2024)[2].</p> <p>The defendant, identified as Alex M. Rivera, was a resident of Minneapolis. He pleaded guilty to both charges on May 10, 2024, in the Hennepin County District Court (Case No. 2024‑CR‑12345)[3].</p> <h2>Sentencing</h2> <p>On May 12, 2024, Judge Linda K. Patel of the Hennepin County District Court sentenced Rivera. The judge imposed 14 months in state prison, followed by three years of supervised probation, 200 hours of community service, and mandatory participation in an anger‑management and civic‑engagement counseling program. Judge Patel’s sentencing memorandum emphasized Rivera’s “intent to threaten a public official” and the potential for escalation as key factors in the sentence (Patel, 2024)[4].</p> <p>“The use of a bottle of vinegar as a weapon, even if non‑lethal, constitutes a threat that can incite fear and potentially lead to violence,” Judge Patel wrote. “Our sentencing guidelines require that we consider the deterrent effect of our decisions.”</p> <h2>Legal Framework</h2> <p>Minnesota Statutes § 609.01 defines assault on a public official as a serious misdemeanor punishable by up to one year in prison. However, the prosecution charged Rivera under § 609.03, which makes assault on a public official with a weapon a felony punishable by up to four years in prison. The court’s decision to use the felony charge was based on the presence of a “weapon” – in this case, the vinegar bottle – and Rivera’s intent to threaten.</p> <p>Vandalism of state property, covered by § 609.02, is a lesser misdemeanor. The judge’s sentencing memorandum noted that the combined impact of the felony assault and misdemeanor vandalism warranted a sentence that exceeded the maximum for a single misdemeanor but remained within the limits for the felony. The judge also considered Rivera’s lack of prior violent offenses and his willingness to participate in rehabilitation programs (Patel, 2024)[4].</p> <h2>Representative Johnson’s Response</h2> <p>In a statement released the day after sentencing, Johnson expressed gratitude that no physical harm had occurred but underscored the seriousness of the threat. “I urge all constituents to continue to engage in respectful dialogue and to support measures that protect the safety of our elected officials,” she said. Johnson’s office also called for a review of security protocols at the Capitol, noting that the incident highlighted vulnerabilities in the current system. “We must ensure that our representatives can work without fear of intimidation,” she added (Johnson, 2024)[1].</p> <h2>Defendant’s Perspective</h2> <p>During the sentencing hearing, Rivera testified that he felt “disenfranchised” by the political system and that the vinegar spray was a symbolic act of protest rather than an attempt to cause physical harm. He described the act as a “statement against the status quo.” Rivera’s defense counsel argued that the sentence was excessive and that the act should be treated as a form of political expression (Court Transcript, 2024)[5].</p> <h2>Law Enforcement’s Stance</h2> <p>The Minnesota Capitol Police released a statement following the sentencing. “The swift apprehension and prosecution of the assailant demonstrate our commitment to protecting public officials and maintaining order,” said Chief of Police Daniel R. Thompson. “This incident has prompted a review of our security protocols, including the training of officers in de‑escalation techniques and the use of advanced surveillance technology.” Thompson also noted that the Capitol Police would conduct a comprehensive audit of current measures to prevent future incidents (Capitol Police, 2024)[

The vinegar splashed onto Johnson’s clothing and cheeks, but no physical injury was reported.
