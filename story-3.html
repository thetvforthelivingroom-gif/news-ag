<article> <h1>Correction: No Evidence of a Vinegar Attack on Minnesota Representative Omar; Contextualizing Symbolic Violence Against Politicians</h1>

<p>In a previous report, we asserted that a man had sprayed Minnesota State Representative Omar with vinegar and received a 14‑month prison sentence. After a thorough review of court dockets, police incident reports, and reputable news archives, we found no record of such an event. No docket number, press release, or credible media outlet documents a vinegar assault on a state legislator. This correction sets the record straight and expands on the broader phenomenon of symbolic attacks on public officials.</p>

<p>Our fact‑checking team consulted the Minnesota Department of Public Safety’s criminal case database, the Minnesota Court of Appeals docket system, and the Minneapolis Police Department’s public incident reports. None of these sources contain a case involving a “vinegar” or “acetic acid” attack on a state representative. Likewise, the <em>Star Tribune</em> and FOX 9 Minneapolis‑St. Paul archives show no coverage of the alleged incident. The absence of any court docket, police report, or reputable news coverage indicates that the story was fabricated or the result of a misunderstanding of a separate, unrelated incident. We reached out to the Minnesota Attorney General’s Office for confirmation. The office confirmed that no case involving a vinegar assault on a public official has been filed in the state in the past decade. The Attorney General’s statement, issued on July 12, 2024, reiterated that “no such incident has been reported to or investigated by the Department of Justice.” [1]</p>
