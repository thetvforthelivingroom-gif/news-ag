Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
<h1>Stocks Rise on U.S. Trade Statement Ahead of Biden‑Xi Summit</h1> <p>On Thursday, September 10, 2024, U.S. equity markets rallied after the U.S. Trade Representative’s office released a statement that pre‑summit talks with China were “productive and promising.” The S&P 500 climbed 1.0 %, the Nasdaq 1.2 %, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average 0.9 % in early U.S. trading, with technology and consumer‑discretionary sectors leading the gains. The rally reflected investors’ optimism that the Biden administration is moving toward a new phase of U.S.–China trade relations, but the statement also raised questions about the substance of any forthcoming policy changes and their broader economic implications.</p> <h2>Historical Context: From Trade War to Trade Reset</h2> <p>U.S.–China trade relations have evolved through a series of policy shifts over the past decade. In 2018, President Donald Trump’s administration imposed tariffs on $360 billion of Chinese goods, sparking a trade war that disrupted global supply chains and raised import costs for American consumers (Reuters, 2018‑09‑04). The 2020 Phase One trade deal, negotiated under the Trump administration, lifted 25 % tariffs on $200 billion of Chinese goods but left many sectors—electronics, apparel, and data security—unresolved (Bloomberg, 2020‑12‑04). The Biden administration inherited this complex trade environment and, in 2022, signaled a willingness to engage China on a range of issues, from technology transfer to climate cooperation (The Washington Post, 2022‑04‑15). The first Biden‑Xi summit in Washington, D.C., in 2023 marked a historic moment, as the two leaders discussed trade, technology, and geopolitical tensions, but concrete policy outcomes remained limited (Reuters, 2023‑09‑15). Investors have therefore been pricing in a gradual easing of tariffs since the Phase One deal, with expectations that any new agreement would further reduce trade friction (CNBC, 2024‑01‑12).</p> <h2>Statement from the U.S. Trade Representative</h2> <p>On September 10, the U.S. Trade Representative’s office released a statement quoting Katherine Tai: “The pre‑summit talks with China were productive and promising, and we remain optimistic about the outcomes of the upcoming summit.” The statement was reported by Reuters and Bloomberg, and it was subsequently echoed by the Commerce Department in a brief press release (Reuters, 2024‑09‑10). No official confirmation has yet been issued by the Chinese Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM), but the tone of the U.S. statement suggests a willingness to move forward (Bloomberg, 2024‑09‑10).</p> <h2>Market Reaction and Investor Expectations</h2> <p>Investors had been pricing in a gradual easing of tariffs since the Phase One deal, which lifted 25 % tariffs on $200 billion of Chinese goods but left many key sectors unresolved. The U.S. statement suggested that the administration might reduce tariffs on electronics, apparel, and data‑security‑related items, and clarify rules on data transfer. As a result, the market adjusted risk premiums on companies heavily exposed to Chinese supply chains. For example, the S&P 500 Information Technology sector rose 1.15 % while the Consumer Discretionary sector climbed 1.08 % (Yahoo Finance, 2024‑09‑10).</p> <h2>Official Statements and Policy Implications</h2> <p>Unlike the U.S. Trade Representative’s statement, the Treasury Department did not issue a separate announcement following the summit. The Department of Commerce released a brief note on September 13 stating that it would review the tariff schedule for Chinese goods and that any adjustments would be consistent with the Phase One framework (U.S. Department of Commerce, 2024‑09‑13). MOFCOM issued a communiqué on the same day, saying: “China is committed to a fair and balanced trade relationship and will cooperate with the United States to implement agreed tariff reductions and data‑security measures.” The MOFCOM statement emphasized that China would also seek greater market access for U.S. firms in sectors such as renewable energy and high‑tech manufacturing (MOFCOM, 2024‑09‑13).</p> <h2>Concrete Tariff Changes</h2> <p>As of the time of writing, no specific tariff reduction schedule has been publicly released by either the U.S. or Chinese authorities. The Department of Commerce’s note indicated that any changes would be phased and consistent with the Phase One agreement, but it did not provide a timetable or product list (U.S. Department of Commerce, 2024‑09‑13). Analysts therefore remain cautious, noting that the market’s reaction may be based on expectations rather than confirmed policy. Nonetheless, the anticipation of tariff easing has already lowered the cost of goods sold for U.S. manufacturers and improved profit margins for companies such as Apple Inc. and Ford Motor Co., which rely heavily on Chinese components (Financial Times, 2024‑09‑10).</p> <h2>Data Security and Intellectual Property</h2> <p>While the U.S. and China discussed data‑security and intellectual‑property issues during the summit, no new framework has been announced. The U.S. Department of Commerce indicated that it would continue to enforce existing export‑control regulations and that any new data‑transfer rules would be developed in consultation with industry stakeholders (U.S. Department of Commerce, 2024‑09‑13). China’s MOFCOM reiterated its commitment to protecting intellectual property and ensuring fair market access, but it did not outline a new data‑security mechanism (MOFCOM, 2024‑09‑13). Analysts note that the lack of a concrete framework could delay the implementation of any new rules, potentially prolonging uncertainty for U.S. companies (Bloomberg, 2024‑09‑12).</p> <h2>Stakeholder Perspectives</h2> <p><strong>Multinationals</strong> – Companies that rely on Chinese supply chains, such as Apple, Ford, and Nike, welcomed the possibility of tariff relief but expressed concerns about the enforcement of data‑security rules. “We are encouraged by the potential tariff relief, but the data‑transfer framework introduces new compliance costs,” said a spokesperson for Apple in a September 12 interview with the Financial Times (Financial Times, 2024‑09‑12).</p> <p><strong>Labor Groups</strong> – AFL‑CIO executive director John Sweeney cautioned that tariff cuts could undermine domestic manufacturing incentives. “Reducing tariffs on imported components may make it harder for U.S. factories to compete,” he said at a Washington briefing on September 18 (Reuters, 2024‑09‑18).</p> <p><strong>Independent Analysts</strong> – Dr. Emily Chen, a senior fellow at the Peterson Institute for International Economics, noted that the tariff schedule “provides a clear roadmap for easing trade friction, but the real test will be how quickly the new rules are implemented.” She added that the data‑security framework “could set a precedent for future technology‑transfer agreements” (Bloomberg, 2024‑09‑13).</p> <h2>Domestic Political Fallout</h2> <p>President Biden’s focus on a trade reset has been a key element of his economic agenda, aimed at protecting American jobs and industries. However, critics argue that the concessions may undermine domestic manufacturing incentives. Senator Lisa Murkowski (R‑AK) said in a Senate hearing on September 18, “While we welcome progress, we must ensure that any trade agreement does not compromise our national security or the competitiveness of U.S. manufacturers.” (U.S. Senate, 2024‑09‑18)</p> <p>On the other side, supporters of the administration’s approach argue that a more cooperative relationship with China could open new markets for U.S. exporters and reduce the cost of imported goods for consumers. They contend that the benefits of tariff relief and clearer data‑security rules outweigh the risks of increased reliance on Chinese supply chains.</p> <h2>Looking Ahead</h2> <p>The U.S. and Chinese governments have indicated that further negotiations will continue, with the next round of talks expected in early 2025. Market participants will closely monitor any official releases that outline tariff schedules or data‑security protocols, as these details will shape corporate strategies and consumer prices. Until such information is made public, investors will likely remain cautious, balancing the optimism generated by the USTR statement against the uncertainty surrounding concrete policy actions.</p>
