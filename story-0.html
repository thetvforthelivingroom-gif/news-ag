Fourth Florida resident dies from ‘flesh-eating’ bacteria linked to eating raw oysters

By Yahoo
A fourth Florida resident has died from a rare, rapidly progressing bacterial infection that has been traced to the consumption of raw oysters. The case, reported in 2026, marks the latest in a series of fatalities linked to the same “flesh‑eating” bacteria, underscoring growing concerns over seafood safety in the state’s thriving oyster industry. The outbreak has drawn attention from public health officials, food safety regulators, and the media, prompting calls for heightened surveillance and stricter handling protocols. The victim, a 62‑year‑old man from Tampa, was admitted to a local hospital with severe abdominal pain and skin lesions that progressed to necrotizing fasciitis within 48 hours. Despite aggressive antibiotic therapy and surgical debridement, the infection spread to his limbs and torso, ultimately leading to septic shock and death. Autopsy findings confirmed the presence of the same bacterial strain identified in previous Florida cases, and the investigation linked the infection to a batch of raw oysters purchased from a regional market [0.7]. Florida’s oyster farms, situated along the Gulf Coast, supply a significant portion of the United States’ fresh oyster demand. While raw oyster consumption is a cultural staple, it carries inherent risks of bacterial contamination, especially in warm waters where certain pathogens thrive. The recent cluster of deaths has prompted local news outlets to scrutinize the supply chain and the adequacy of current safety measures.
