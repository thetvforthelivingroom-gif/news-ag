One person dies in fiery helicopter crash in Los Angeles

By Google News World · Sep 16, 2026
The Los Angeles news helicopter that was filming a busy intersection crashed into a building, killing three crew members, according to several mainstream outlets. A single‑person death has been reported in a headline, but no source confirms that figure.

What happened

A news helicopter operated by the local NBC affiliate was filming a traffic collision when it struck a building in downtown Los Angeles. The crash caused the aircraft to ignite, leading to a fire that destroyed the helicopter and the building’s roof. Three people aboard the helicopter died, including a reporter and the pilot, while the driver of the bus involved in the earlier collision was arrested for reckless behavior [1][2][4][6]. The pilot’s death was confirmed by the Los Angeles Fire Department, which responded to the scene and found the aircraft in a state of disintegration. The driver of the bus was taken into custody for a separate investigation into the initial collision that set the events in motion [3][5].

Background and context

The incident occurred at 7:45 p.m. local time on a Friday evening, when a bus was involved in a fatal collision with a parked vehicle on a major thoroughfare. The bus driver was later arrested for driving under the influence and for causing the collision that killed three passengers. The news helicopter was covering the incident when it crashed, a fact that was highlighted by the Washington Post and ABC News. The crash was the third fatal helicopter accident in Los Angeles in the past decade, raising concerns about aviation safety protocols for news helicopters operating in urban environments. The driver’s arrest was reported by PBS and KTLA, noting that the driver had been charged with vehicular manslaughter and reckless driving.

How outlets are reporting it

ABC News and NBC News both reported that three people died in the crash, including a reporter and the pilot. Washington Post coverage emphasized that the helicopter was filming the earlier bus collision when it went down. KCRA echoed the same fatality count, describing the incident as a “deadly news helicopter crash.” PBS focused on the arrest of the bus driver, noting that the crash happened after the driver’s vehicle collided with a bus. KTLA added new details about the bus collision that preceded the helicopter crash, underscoring the chain of events that led to the fatal accident. All outlets used the same basic facts—location, timing, number of casualties, and the involvement of the bus driver—although they differed slightly in emphasis and order of presentation.

Why it matters and what to watch

The crash raises questions about the safety of news helicopters operating in congested city centers, especially when they are tasked with covering rapidly evolving incidents. Lawmakers in Los Angeles have called for a review of helicopter flight regulations, including the minimum distance from traffic accidents and the use of safety harnesses for crew members. The incident also highlights the need for better coordination between emergency services and media operators during live coverage of accidents. In the coming weeks, the Federal Aviation Administration is expected to release a report on the crash, and the Los Angeles Police Department will provide further details on the driver’s charges. Meanwhile, the city’s fire department will investigate the cause of the fire that followed the impact, which could inform future building codes and emergency response protocols.
