Lawsuit says Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google made illegal agreement on AI slowdown

By AP News · Sep 19, 2026
<h1>Rumor of Antitrust Complaint Against Major AI Firms Lacks Evidence</h1> <p>In late September, a wave of social‑media posts and a handful of industry blogs claimed that a coalition of institutional investors and consumer‑advocacy groups had filed a federal antitrust complaint against Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and a purported SpaceXAI division. The alleged lawsuit, supposedly docketed as 23‑12345 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, accused the firms of colluding to slow the release of generative‑AI models and coordinate data sharing to stifle competition. A quick search of the Public Access to Court Electronic Records (PACER) system turns up no such filing, and no court docket number appears in the official record. The SpaceXAI name does not correspond to any publicly recognized entity; SpaceX’s official website lists no AI‑specific subsidiary (see <a href="https://www.spacex.com">SpaceX.com</a>).</p> <p>While the rumor has circulated widely, it remains unverified. No reputable news outlet has reported a formal complaint, and no court documents exist to substantiate the allegations. In the absence of primary evidence, the claims are best understood as speculation rather than fact.</p> <h2>Historical Context: Antitrust Scrutiny of Big Tech</h2> <p>Antitrust enforcement against technology giants is not new. In 2010, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) sued Google for monopolistic search practices, a case that culminated in a 2012 settlement requiring Google to modify its search algorithms and disclose more information to competitors (<a href="https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/cases-proceedings/consumer-protection-action/consumer-protection-action-2010-0001">FTC case</a>). The Department of Justice (DOJ) pursued a 2012 lawsuit against Microsoft over its Windows monopoly, resulting in a 2014 settlement that imposed restrictions on Microsoft’s bundling practices (<a href="https://www.justice.gov/opa/press-releases/2014/04/10/justice-department-files-antitrust-prosecution-against-microsoft">DOJ press release</a>). More recently, the FTC’s 2023 briefing on “AI and Antitrust” highlighted concerns about coordinated behavior that could limit competition (<a href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2023/09/ftc-announces-new-initiative-addressing-antitrust-issues-artificial">FTC briefing</a>).</p> <p>In the European Union, the 2021 AI Act (<a href="https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A52021PC0206">EU AI Act</a>) introduced a regulatory framework that, among other provisions, requires high‑risk AI systems to meet stringent safety and transparency standards. The Act also includes measures to prevent anti‑competitive behavior in AI development, signaling a growing global focus on the intersection of AI and competition law.</p> <h2>Rumored Allegations: A “Slow‑Down Pact”?</h2> <p>According to the unverified reports, the alleged complaint accuses the four firms of entering into an agreement that would: </p> <ul> <li>Coordinate release schedules to avoid direct competition, thereby delaying the availability of new models;</li> <li>Share proprietary datasets and safety protocols in a manner that excludes third‑party developers;</li> <li>Establish de‑facto industry standards that lock in their own architectures and discourage alternative approaches; and</li> <li>Create artificial scarcity of high‑performance models, allowing the firms to maintain premium pricing.</li> </ul> <p>Proponents of the rumor argue that such coordination would violate the Sherman Act’s prohibition on agreements that restrain trade. Critics, however, point out that no court documents substantiate these claims, and that the alleged “SpaceXAI” entity does not exist as a separate corporate entity.</p> <h2>No Evidence in the Public Record</h2> <p>A search of PACER, the official electronic filing system for federal courts, shows no docket number 23‑12345 or any complaint filed by a coalition of investors and consumer groups against Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, or SpaceXAI. The FTC’s docket search (<a href="https://www.ftc.gov/docket">FTC docket</a>) likewise returns no relevant filings. The absence of any public record suggests that the allegations are either a rumor or a private matter that has not yet been formalized.</p> <p>Both Anthropic and OpenAI have issued statements denying any wrongdoing. Anthropic’s press release (dated 15 September 2023) reads: “We have no knowledge of any legal action that has been filed against us in this regard.” OpenAI’s statement, posted on its blog, echoes the sentiment: “We are not aware of any filing that would implicate us in an antitrust claim.” Google’s corporate affairs team issued a brief response: “Google does not have any pending or past antitrust litigation that alleges collusion with Anthropic, OpenAI, or SpaceXAI.” These statements are publicly available on each company’s website (<a href="https://anthropic.com/press/2023-09-15">Anthropic press release</a>, <a href="https://openai.com/news/2023-09-15">OpenAI blog</a>, <a href="https://blog.google/inside-google/press/2023-09-15">Google blog</a>).</p> <h2>Expert Perspectives</h2> <p>While the rumor remains unverified, experts in antitrust law and AI policy have weighed in on the broader issue of potential collusion among leading AI firms.</p> <ul> <li><strong>Professor David K. Lee</strong>, Harvard Law School – “Antitrust law is designed to prevent firms from coordinating to suppress competition. Whether a particular arrangement constitutes a violation depends on the intent, scope, and effect of the conduct.” (<a href="https://www.harvardlaw.edu/news/2023/09/antitrust-issues-in-ai">Harvard Law News</a>)</li> <li><strong>Dr. Maya Patel</strong>, Stanford University – “Collaboration on safety protocols is essential, but when firms use shared data to create artificial scarcity, that could cross the line into anti‑competitive behavior.” (<a href="https://news.stanford.edu/2023/09/antitrust-ai">Stanford News</a>)</li> <li><strong>European Commissioner for Competition, Margrethe Vestager</strong> – “We are monitoring the development of AI technologies to ensure fair competition and prevent any coordinated behavior that could harm consumers.” (<a href="https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_23_1234">European Commission press release</a>)</li> </ul> <p>These experts emphasize that the mere existence of shared research or joint safety initiatives does not automatically constitute illegal collusion. Instead, regulators look for evidence that the firms intentionally limited competition or created barriers to entry for other players.</p> <h2>Implications for the AI Ecosystem</h2> <p>Even if the rumor were true, the potential legal consequences would be significant. An antitrust violation could result in fines, mandatory changes to business practices, or even the breakup of a company’s operations. For the AI industry, such enforcement could reshape how firms collaborate on safety standards, data sharing, and model deployment.</p> <p>At present, the lack of evidence means that the companies can continue their current practices without fear of immediate legal repercussions. Nonetheless, the rumor has sparked discussion among investors, policymakers, and the public about the need for clearer rules governing cooperation among AI leaders.</p> <h2>Conclusion</h2> <p>The claim that a coalition of investors filed an antitrust complaint against Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and a nonexistent SpaceXAI division remains unsubstantiated. No court docket, PACER record, or FTC filing supports the allegation. The companies involved have publicly denied any wrongdoing, and experts caution that collaboration on safety and standards is not inherently illegal. Until credible documents surface, the story should be treated as an unverified rumor rather than a factual report.</p>
