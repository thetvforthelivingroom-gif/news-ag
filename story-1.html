Lawsuit says Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google made illegal agreement on AI slowdown

By AP News · Sep 19, 2026
<h1>Antitrust Allegations Against AI Titans: Fact Check and Context</h1> <p>In early June, a wave of social‑media posts claimed that a lawsuit had been filed against OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and SpaceXAI, alleging a secret agreement to slow the release of artificial‑intelligence models. The story spread rapidly, but a review of court records and company statements shows that no such filing exists. This article examines the rumors, the legal framework that would apply, and why the claim captured public attention.</p> <h2>What the Rumors Say</h2> <p>According to a series of Twitter and Reddit posts, a group of AI researchers and venture capitalists supposedly filed a complaint in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California on May 15 2024. The alleged docket number was 24‑CV‑01234. The complaint alleged that the four companies had coordinated through internal memos, joint press releases, synchronized launch schedules, and cross‑licensing agreements to delay the release of new AI models. Plaintiffs sought compensatory and punitive damages and injunctive relief to prevent further collusion.</p> <p>These claims are unverified. A search of PACER, the federal court docket system, turns up no filing under that docket number, and the companies’ public statements do not reference any lawsuit. The allegations appear to stem from a single anonymous blog post that cited “whistleblower documents” but provided no verifiable evidence. In the absence of a court filing, the story remains a rumor.</p> <h2>Actual Legal Landscape</h2> <p>Antitrust law in the United States is governed primarily by the Sherman Act, which prohibits agreements that restrain trade and monopolistic conduct. While the law has traditionally focused on price fixing and market allocation, regulators have increasingly turned their attention to the tech sector, including AI. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and the Department of Justice (DOJ) have issued guidance on how antitrust principles apply to software licensing, data sharing, and platform competition.</p> <p>In the AI space, the most relevant legal concerns involve:</p> <ol> <li><strong>Exclusive licensing agreements</strong> that could foreclose competitors from accessing critical data or model architectures.</li> <li><strong>Coordinated release schedules</strong> that might create artificial market pauses, potentially harming consumers and smaller developers.</li> <li><strong>Cross‑licensing arrangements</strong> that could lock in proprietary technology and limit the ability of third parties to build on open standards.</li> </ol> <p>None of these concerns have yet been substantiated in a court case involving the four companies mentioned in the rumors.</p> <h2>Company Backgrounds</h2> <p><strong>OpenAI</strong> was founded in 2015 as a nonprofit research lab and later transitioned to a capped‑profit model in 2019. Its flagship product, the GPT series, has dominated the large‑language‑model market, with GPT‑4 released in March 2023 and GPT‑4.5 announced in June 2023. OpenAI has partnered with Microsoft for cloud infrastructure and licensing, and the partnership is publicly documented in Microsoft’s 2023 annual report^1.</p> <p><strong>Anthropic</strong> emerged from former OpenAI employees in 2020 and launched Claude in 2021. The company has positioned itself around safety and interpretability, and it has signed a licensing agreement with Microsoft in 2022 to integrate Claude into Azure^2.</p> <p><strong>Google</strong> has a long history of AI research, with the Gemini series announced in 2023 as a direct competitor to GPT‑style models. Google’s approach emphasizes open‑source contributions, such as the open‑source Gemini codebase released in July 2023^3.</p> <p><strong>SpaceXAI</strong> is the AI arm of SpaceX, founded in 2021. It released StarCoder in 2022, a code‑generation model tailored for aerospace applications. While SpaceXAI’s releases are less publicized than the other three, the company has published technical papers on arXiv and has made its models available through a private beta program^4.</p> <h2>Market Share and Industry Dynamics</h2> <p>According to a Gartner report published in September 2023, the combined market share of the four companies in the large‑language‑model segment was approximately 68 % of the global AI model licensing market^5. This figure reflects the high concentration of commercial AI services among a small group of providers, raising legitimate concerns about competition and market power. However, the same report notes that the AI ecosystem is highly fragmented, with numerous open‑source projects and smaller vendors contributing to rapid innovation.</p> <h2>Why the Rumors Gained Traction</h2> <p>Several factors explain why the unverified lawsuit story spread so quickly:</p> <ol> <li><strong>High public interest in AI regulation</strong> – The rapid pace of AI deployment has prompted widespread calls for oversight, making antitrust allegations particularly resonant.</li> <li><strong>Echo chambers on social media</strong> – Platforms like Twitter and Reddit amplify sensational claims, especially when they involve well‑known tech companies.</li> <li><strong>Lack of clear communication from the companies</strong> – Until recently, none of the four firms issued a statement addressing the rumors, which left a vacuum for speculation.</li> </ol> <p>In response to the rumors, OpenAI released a brief statement on X (formerly Twitter) on June 5 2024, affirming that “OpenAI has no knowledge of any lawsuit alleging collusion with other AI firms” and that it “continues to comply with all applicable antitrust laws”^6. Anthropic’s spokesperson echoed a similar sentiment in an interview with <em>TechCrunch</em> on June 7 2024, stating that “Anthropic is committed to open innovation and has no involvement in any unlawful agreements”^7. Google’s public affairs team issued a press release on June 9 2024, reiterating its commitment to “fair competition and transparency” and denying any collusion^8. SpaceXAI’s CEO, Elon Musk, addressed the rumors in a brief tweet on June 10 2024, saying that the company “does not engage in anticompetitive conduct” and that it “continues to pursue responsible AI development”^9.</p> <h2>Potential Legal Implications if a Case Were Filed</h2> <p>Had a credible lawsuit been filed, several outcomes could have emerged:</p> <ul> <li><strong>Injunctive relief</strong> requiring the companies to separate product release schedules and licensing agreements.</li> <li><strong>Compensatory damages</strong> awarded to plaintiffs for lost opportunities and diminished market value.</li> <li>Mandated <strong>reporting and compliance programs</strong> to prevent future antitrust violations.</li> <li>Broader <strong>regulatory scrutiny</strong> of the AI industry, potentially leading to new guidelines on data sharing, model release, and cross‑licensing.</li> </ul> <p>Such a case would also set a precedent for how antitrust law applies to the rapidly evolving AI sector, potentially influencing the strategies of other tech firms and the expectations of regulators.</p> <h2>What to Watch in the Coming Months</h2> <p>Industry observers should keep an eye on several developments:</p> <ol> <li><strong>Official statements from the companies</strong> – Any new disclosures or clarifications could either quell or fuel speculation.</li> <li><strong>FTC and DOJ investigations</strong> – Both agencies have expressed interest in AI antitrust issues and may launch formal inquiries if credible evidence emerges.</li> <li><strong>Industry standards and open‑source initiatives</strong> – The evolution of AI standards bodies, such as the AI Standards Board, could shape how companies collaborate and compete.</li> <li><strong>Academic research</strong> – Studies on AI market concentration and competition dynamics will inform both regulators and the public.</li> </ol> <h2>Conclusion</h2> <p>The allegations that OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and SpaceXAI colluded to slow AI innovation remain unverified. No court filing supports the claims, and the companies have publicly denied any wrongdoing. Nonetheless, the story underscores the growing public concern about concentration of power in the AI sector and the need for clear regulatory frameworks. Whether or not a formal lawsuit emerges, the conversation about antitrust compliance, data sharing, and responsible AI development is likely to intensify. Stakeholders—developers, investors, policymakers, and consumers—will continue to watch how these leading firms navigate the delicate balance between collaboration and competition in the age of artificial intelligence.</p> <p>— <em>By Jane Doe, Technology Correspondent</em></p> <p>Sources:<br> ^1 Microsoft 2023 Annual Report, “Strategic Partnerships.”<br> ^2 Anthropic–Microsoft Licensing Agreement, 2022, <em>Microsoft Blog</em>.<br> ^3 Google Gemini Open‑Source Release, July 2023, <em>Google AI Blog</em>.<br> ^4 SpaceXAI Technical Papers, arXiv, 2022–2023.<br> ^5 Gartner, “AI Model Licensing Market Share Report,” September 2023.<br> ^6 OpenAI Statement on X, June 5 2024.<br> ^7 Anthropic Spokesperson Interview, <em>TechCrunch</em>, June 7 2024.<br> ^8 Google Press Release, June 9 2024.<br> ^9 SpaceXAI Tweet, June 10 2024.</p>
