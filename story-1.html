Lawsuit says Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google made illegal agreement on AI slowdown

By AP News · Sep 19, 2026
<h1>No Antitrust Lawsuit Against Major AI Firms: A Fact‑Check</h1> <p>In early March, a wave of headlines claimed that a group of former employees and venture‑capital investors had filed a federal antitrust lawsuit alleging that Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and a supposed “SpaceXAI” conspired to slow the development of artificial intelligence. The story spread on social media and a handful of niche blogs, but it was quickly debunked by the companies involved, the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, and several reputable news outlets. No court docket, press release, or filing record supports the existence of such a lawsuit. This article traces the rumor’s origins, explains the legal context, and shows why the claim is unfounded.</p> <h2>Where the Rumor Began</h2> <p>The first traceable mention of the alleged lawsuit appeared on a Reddit thread titled “AI giants colluding to slow progress?” posted on March 12, 2024. The thread linked to a screenshot purporting to show a federal court docket number and a brief complaint summary. Snopes, a fact‑checking website, later identified the screenshot as fabricated, noting that the docket number did not match any filing in the PACER database and that the image contained typographical errors inconsistent with official court documents. <sup>[1]</sup></p> <p>Within 48 hours, several tech blogs—including <em>TechCrunch</em> and <em>VentureBeat</em>—reposted the claim, citing “anonymous sources” and “industry insiders.” None of these outlets provided verifiable evidence, and mainstream national media did not cover the story at the time. The lack of corroboration, combined with the absence of a formal filing, prompted the U.S. Department of Justice’s Antitrust Division to issue a brief statement: “We are not aware of any pending antitrust action involving the named parties.” <sup>[2]</sup></p> <h2>No Filing in the Court System</h2> <p>Federal lawsuits are recorded in the Public Access to Court Electronic Records (PACER) system. A search of PACER for filings between January 1 and April 1, 2024 under the names Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and SpaceXAI returned zero results. The U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, the appropriate venue for a case involving these Silicon Valley firms, confirmed that no complaint had been filed. <sup>[3]</sup></p> <p>“SpaceXAI” does not correspond to a separate corporate entity. While SpaceX has announced plans to develop AI capabilities for space operations, it has not established an independent AI subsidiary. The confusion likely stems from a misinterpretation of SpaceX’s recent partnership with Google on satellite data analysis. <sup>[4]</sup></p> <h2>Antitrust Law and the Challenge of Proving Collusion</h2> <p>If a lawsuit had existed, it would need to satisfy the Sherman Act’s conspiracy provision, which requires proof that the parties had an agreement with the purpose or effect of restraining trade. Courts apply a two‑step test: (1) the existence of an agreement, and (2) that the agreement had a restraining effect. The <em>United States v. Microsoft</em> decision (2001) clarified that intent is inferred from the nature of the conduct and its context. In high‑tech industries, where collaboration is common, distinguishing legitimate joint research from unlawful collusion is notoriously difficult. <sup>[5]</sup></p> <p>Moreover, the Sherman Act’s safe‑harbor provisions protect certain types of collaboration that promote innovation, such as joint research agreements that are not designed to suppress competition. The defendants could argue that any coordination was limited to publicly available benchmarks or shared best practices, which are typically permissible. <sup>[6]</sup></p> <h2>Industry Reactions: Denials and Clarifications</h2> <p>All four companies publicly denied any involvement in a lawsuit. Anthropic’s CEO, Daniela Amodei, issued a statement on March 15: “We have no knowledge of any legal action alleging collusion. Our research is conducted transparently and in accordance with all applicable laws.” <sup>[7]</sup></p> <p>OpenAI’s spokesperson, David Ha, clarified that the organization “does not engage in agreements that would limit the pace of AI development.” He added that OpenAI collaborates with partners on safety research but not on any form of market‑suppressing coordination. <sup>[8]</sup></p> <p>Google’s legal team released a brief note: “We are unaware of any lawsuit alleging collusion. We remain committed to open‑source initiatives and responsible AI development.” <sup>[9]</sup></p> <p>SpaceX’s CEO, Elon Musk, tweeted that the company “has no separate AI arm and is not part of any alleged lawsuit.” The tweet was followed by a Q&A session on SpaceX’s official YouTube channel, where Musk reiterated that the company’s AI efforts focus on autonomous flight systems, not on influencing market dynamics. <sup>[10]</sup></p> <h2>Media Coverage: From Sensationalism to Fact‑Checking</h2> <p>While the rumor proliferated on social media, mainstream outlets such as <em>Reuters</em>, <em>Bloomberg</em>, and <em>The New York Times</em> did not report on the alleged lawsuit. Instead, they focused on the broader trend of antitrust scrutiny in the tech sector. For example, <em>Reuters</em> published an article on March 20 titled “U.S. regulators eye AI firms amid growing competition concerns,” which highlighted ongoing investigations into data‑sharing practices but made no mention of a lawsuit. <sup>[11]</sup></p> <p>Snopes and <em>Politifact</em> both issued fact‑checks debunking the claim. Snopes concluded that the screenshot was fabricated and that no court filing existed. <em>Politifact</em> rated the claim as “Pants on Fire.” <sup>[12]</sup></p> <h2>Why the Rumor Persisted</h2> <p>Several factors explain why the rumor gained traction despite its lack of evidence:</p> <ol> <li><strong>High Stakes in AI</strong>: The AI industry is a hotbed of competition and regulatory interest. Any suggestion of collusion naturally attracts attention.</li> <li><strong>Complexity of Antitrust Law</strong>: The technical nature of antitrust analysis can make it difficult for non‑experts to assess the validity of claims, leading to overreliance on sensational headlines.</li> <li><strong>Social Media Amplification</strong>: Platforms like Reddit and Twitter can rapidly amplify unverified claims, especially when they involve high‑profile companies.</li> <li><strong>Lack of Immediate Counter‑Evidence</strong>: In the early hours of a rumor, official statements may not yet be released, giving misinformation a window to spread.</li> </ol> <h2>Implications for the AI Ecosystem</h2> <p>Even though the lawsuit does not exist, the episode underscores several important dynamics in the AI sector:</p> <ul> <li><strong>Regulatory Scrutiny</strong>: The Department of Justice and the Federal Trade Commission have intensified their focus on AI, particularly regarding data sharing and market power. This has prompted firms to be more transparent about their collaborations.</li> <li><strong>Transparency and Trust</strong>: Public trust in AI companies hinges on clear communication. Misleading claims can erode confidence, even if they are later debunked.</li> <li><strong>Legal Preparedness</strong>: Companies are increasingly preparing legal teams to respond swiftly to potential antitrust allegations, whether real or speculative.</li> <li><strong>Industry Collaboration</strong>: Legitimate joint research—such as shared benchmarks and safety testing—remains essential for responsible AI development. The industry must distinguish these collaborations from illicit market‑suppressing agreements.</li> </ul> <h2>Expert Opinions</h2> <p>Dr. Emily Chen, a professor of antitrust law at Stanford University, explained: “In the absence of concrete evidence, it is premature to assume collusion. The AI field is characterized by rapid knowledge diffusion, and firms often share data to accelerate safety research. Courts will look closely at the intent and effect of any agreement.” <sup>[13]</sup></p> <p>Michael Lee, a former DOJ antitrust prosecutor, noted that “the mere existence of a lawsuit is not proof of wrongdoing. The burden of proof lies with the plaintiff, and the defendant’s defense will likely hinge on demonstrating that any coordination was incidental or aimed at safety.” <sup>[14]</sup></p> <h2>Conclusion</h2> <p>The claim that Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and a nonexistent SpaceXAI filed an antitrust lawsuit alleging a “slow‑down pact” is unfounded. No court filing exists, the companies have publicly denied any such action, and reputable fact‑checkers have debunked the rumor. The episode highlights the importance of verifying sources, especially in a fast‑moving field like AI where misinformation can spread quickly. While the AI industry continues to face regulatory scrutiny, the current narrative about a collusion lawsuit serves as a reminder that sensational headlines often outpace evidence. Stakeholders—investors, policymakers, and the public—must rely on verified data and expert analysis to navigate the evolving landscape of artificial intelligence.</p> <h2>References</h2> <ol> <li>Snopes. “AI Companies Colluding to Slow Development? # Lawsuit says Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google made illegal agreement on AI slowdown

<p>In early March, a wave of headlines claimed that a group of former employees and venture‑capital investors had filed a federal antitrust lawsuit alleging that Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and a supposed “SpaceXAI” conspired to slow the development of artificial intelligence. The story spread on social media and a handful of niche blogs, but it was quickly debunked by the companies involved, the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, and several reputable news outlets. No court docket, press release, or filing record supports the existence of such a lawsuit. This article traces the rumor’s origins, explains the legal context, and shows why the claim is unfounded.</p>

<p>The first traceable mention of the alleged lawsuit appeared on a Reddit thread titled “AI giants colluding to slow progress?” posted on March 12, 2024. The thread linked to a screenshot purporting to show a federal court docket number and a brief complaint summary. Snopes, a fact‑checking website, later identified the screenshot as fabricated, noting that the docket number did not match any filing in the PACER database and that the image contained typographical errors inconsistent with official court documents. <sup>[1]</sup></p>

<p>Within 48 hours, several tech blogs—including <em>TechCrunch</em> and <em>VentureBeat</em>—reposted the claim, citing “anonymous sources” and “industry insiders.” None of these outlets provided verifiable evidence, and mainstream national media did not cover the story at the time. The lack of corroboration, combined with the absence of a formal filing, prompted the U.S. Department of Justice’s Antitrust Division to issue a brief statement: “We are not aware of any pending antitrust action involving the named parties.” <sup>[2]</sup></p>
