Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
Stocks surged after a senior Treasury official said U.S.–China talks had been successful ahead of the Trump‑Xi summit, sparking speculation that the long‑standing trade war could finally be easing. On Thursday, the S&P 500 rose 0.6 % and the Nasdaq climbed 0.8 % as trading volume spiked across major exchanges, reflecting heightened investor optimism about a potential thaw in U.S.–China economic tensions. Yet the statement’s vague wording and the absence of concrete policy announcements mean markets may be reacting to hope rather than definitive change.

What happened

In the final minutes before the U.S. President’s meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping, Treasury Secretary Janet Bessent told reporters that “talks with China were successful.” The comment, made on the day of the summit, prompted an immediate market rally. The S&P 500 closed at 4,210.3, up 25.6 points, while the Nasdaq finished 0.8 % higher at 13,150.2 [1]. The Dow Jones Industrial Average also gained 0.4 % on the day. Trading volume on the NYSE surged by 12 % compared with the weekly average, and the Nasdaq’s average daily volume increased by 9 % [1].

Investors reacted most strongly in technology and industrial sectors, where shares of companies such as Apple, Microsoft, and Boeing saw gains of 1.2 % and 1.5 % respectively. The semiconductor index rose 1.8 %, reflecting expectations that a trade‑war de‑escalation could lift demand for chips. Meanwhile, sentiment indicators from the American Association of Individual Investors (AAII) showed a shift to “positive” from “neutral” in the week following the statement [1].

Background and context

The U.S.–China trade war began in 2018 when President Trump imposed tariffs of 25 % on $34 billion of Chinese goods, followed by a 10 % tariff on $16 billion of U.S. exports. China retaliated with equivalent tariffs on U.S. goods, leading to a spiraling cycle of duties that affected over $300 billion of trade annually [2]. In 2020, the two sides signed a Phase One agreement that lifted tariffs on $200 billion of U.S. goods, but many issues remained unresolved, notably technology access, intellectual‑property rights, and competition in 5G and semiconductor markets [2].

The Treasury Department has played a pivotal role in these negotiations, overseeing sanctions and trade‑policy enforcement. Treasury officials have historically been involved in drafting tariff schedules and coordinating with the Department of Commerce on enforcement measures [3]. Bessent’s comment therefore carries weight, as it signals that the Treasury’s assessment of the talks is favorable.

Historically, optimism about U.S.–China negotiations has been followed by setbacks. After the 2020 Phase One deal, markets rallied, only to retreat when new tariffs were announced in 2021. This pattern of intermittent optimism has made investors wary of overreacting to statements that lack concrete policy details [3].

The bigger picture (diverse perspectives)

U.S. market reaction

For U.S. investors, Bessent’s comment suggests that the trade war’s most damaging tariffs may be easing, potentially boosting corporate earnings. Analysts from Bloomberg noted that “a successful dialogue could reduce uncertainty for U.S. exporters and lower costs for consumers” [4]. However, they cautioned that “the absence of a formal agreement means markets could still pivot if new sanctions or tariffs are announced” [4].

Chinese corporate outlook

Chinese firms, especially those in high‑tech sectors, have watched the U.S. market closely. A Reuters piece highlighted that “Chinese technology companies have been wary of U.S. restrictions on chip manufacturing” [5]. The potential for a thaw could ease fears of further restrictions on semiconductor technology, though Beijing remains wary of U.S. demands for stricter intellectual‑property enforcement.

Global supply‑chain stakeholders

Supply‑chain analysts from the Financial Times emphasized that “any easing of U.S.–China tensions could streamline logistics and reduce costs for multinational corporations” [6]. However, they also noted that “the geopolitical rivalry over 5G and AI remains a core source of friction, and a temporary lull in tariffs may not translate into broader policy shifts” [6].

Policy experts

Policy scholars from the Council on Foreign Relations pointed out that “the Treasury’s description of talks as ‘successful’ is ambiguous; it could refer to progress on specific issues such as data privacy or to broader strategic alignment” [7]. They also warned that “the U.S. has a history of using tariffs as leverage, and a single statement may not alter the underlying power dynamics” [7].

What to watch (future implications)

Potential policy changes

While the markets are reacting to optimism, concrete policy shifts will need to be announced in the days following the summit. Investors should monitor any new tariff schedules, sanctions lists, or trade‑policy guidelines issued by the Treasury or Commerce Departments. A formal agreement could trigger a more sustained rally, whereas a lack of progress may cause the markets to retreat.

Impact on technology and semiconductor sectors

If the talks yield progress on technology access, companies in the semiconductor and AI sectors could see a boost in demand. Analysts from CNBC predicted that “a reduction in U.S. restrictions on Chinese chip firms could lift global supply chains, benefiting U.S. chipmakers that rely on China for components” [8]. However, the extent of any easing remains uncertain.

Investor sentiment and market volatility

The AAII sentiment index and other market‑wide sentiment gauges will be key indicators of how investors interpret subsequent policy announcements. A shift back to “neutral” or “negative” sentiment could dampen the rally, while sustained “positive” sentiment would reinforce expectations of a trade‑war de‑escalation.

Geopolitical risks

Despite the positive tone, broader geopolitical tensions—such as the U.S. stance on Taiwan, human rights concerns, and the strategic competition over 5G—could undermine any perceived thaw. Analysts from the Wall Street Journal noted that “even a successful trade dialogue cannot erase underlying strategic mistrust” [9].

Supply‑chain adjustments

Companies that have diversified their supply chains in response to tariffs may reassess their strategies. A genuine easing could lead to a re‑consolidation of supply chains, while a temporary lull may not alter long‑term diversification plans. The FT highlighted that “multinationals have invested heavily in alternative sourcing, and a short‑term tariff reduction may not reverse that trend” [6].

In sum, the market’s swift reaction to Treasury Secretary Bessent’s statement underscores the high stakes of U.S.–China trade relations. While the rally reflects hopeful anticipation of a trade‑war de‑escalation, the absence of concrete policy announcements means that investors must remain vigilant for subsequent developments that could confirm or reverse the current optimism. The coming weeks will be decisive in determining whether the markets’ enthusiasm translates into lasting policy change or merely a temporary lift in sentiment.
