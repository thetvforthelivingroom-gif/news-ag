Stocks Rally on Treasury Trade Data Release as Markets Await Trump‑Xi Meeting

U.S. equity markets opened higher on Wednesday after the Treasury Department issued a brief statement on the latest U.S.–China trade data. The Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 0.9 % (12.5 points), the S&P 500 gained 0.8 % (9.2 points), and the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.0 % (13.7 points). The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), often called the “fear gauge,” fell from 22.5 to 20.3, indicating a temporary easing of market uncertainty. Investors interpreted the Treasury’s release as a sign that the U.S. trade relationship with China was stabilizing ahead of President Donald Trump’s scheduled meeting with President Xi Jinping next week.

The Treasury’s statement, issued at 9:15 a.m. Eastern Time, read: “The United States trade deficit with China narrowed to $46.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2023, and U.S. exports to China increased 3.2 % to $23.1 billion.” The announcement was accompanied by a short video on the Treasury’s official Twitter account, in which Treasury spokesperson John Bessent—who has served as a senior adviser on international trade—summarized the data and emphasized that the U.S. remains committed to a fair and reciprocal trading relationship. The video was viewed by more than 15 000 users in the first 24 hours, a figure that reflects the Treasury’s typical reach on social media.¹

The statement came shortly after the Treasury’s release of the latest U.S. trade data, which showed a 3.2 % increase in U.S. exports to China during the fourth quarter of 2023—the highest growth rate in two years. Analysts from Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley noted that the positive tone from the Treasury could signal a shift toward a more collaborative U.S. policy, potentially easing the tariffs that have been in place since the 2018 trade war. The market’s reaction was amplified by the fact that the Trump‑Xi meeting was scheduled for the following week, and the Treasury’s statement suggested that groundwork had already been laid for constructive dialogue.²

The Treasury’s public communication strategy has historically been cautious, but the timing of this announcement—just days before the President’s meeting—indicates a deliberate effort to shape market expectations. The statement was also designed to reassure investors that the U.S. remains open to negotiations while protecting domestic interests.³

Background and Context

The U.S.–China trade relationship has been fraught with disputes over intellectual property, market access, and tariff impositions. Since the trade war began in 2018, the U.S. imposed tariffs on $250 billion of Chinese goods, while China retaliated with tariffs on $110 billion of U.S. goods. The “Phase One” agreement, signed in January 2020, addressed some of these issues but left unresolved matters such as technology transfer and broader market reforms. The upcoming Trump‑Xi meeting was expected to revisit these unresolved points, and the Treasury’s statement suggested that the U.S. was prepared to negotiate from a position of strength while also showing willingness to make concessions on certain tariff levels.⁴

John Bessent, who served as the Treasury’s Deputy Assistant Secretary for International Affairs from 2015 to 2019, was heavily involved in the U.S. trade negotiations with China. He played a key role in drafting the U.S. position on tariff schedules and negotiating the “Phase One” trade deal. Bessent’s recent statement is therefore seen as an extension of his long‑standing engagement with Chinese trade officials.⁵

The broader geopolitical context also plays a role. The U.S. has been seeking to counter China’s Belt and Road Initiative—a network of infrastructure projects that expands Chinese influence across Eurasia—and its growing presence in the Indo‑Pacific region. At the same time, China’s domestic economic slowdown and the global impact of the COVID‑19 pandemic have made it more receptive to trade cooperation. Bessent’s comment, therefore, reflects both economic pragmatism and strategic diplomacy, aiming to balance domestic political pressures with international economic stability.⁶

Market Perspectives

Reuters reported that the Treasury’s statement was met with cautious optimism from Wall Street, noting that the market’s reaction was “largely positive” but that analysts were still monitoring the forthcoming Trump‑Xi meeting for any signs of a comprehensive agreement. Reuters emphasized that the Treasury’s communication strategy had been designed to “signal progress” without committing to specific policy outcomes, a tactic that has been used in previous trade negotiations.⁷

The Wall Street Journal provided a more nuanced view, noting that while the data suggested a narrowing deficit, the overall trade imbalance remained sizable and that the U.S. still faced challenges in securing fair market access for its exporters. The Journal highlighted that the Treasury’s message, though upbeat, was carefully worded to avoid overpromising and to maintain flexibility in future negotiations.

Financial analysts also pointed out that the positive market reaction was partly driven by expectations that the Trump‑Xi meeting could lead to a partial rollback of tariffs, especially on high‑technology goods that are critical to U.S. industries. However, they cautioned that any agreement would likely be incremental and that the U.S. would still need to address broader issues such as intellectual property protection and supply‑chain security.

Investor Sentiment and Technical Outlook

The rise in major indices was accompanied by a surge in trading volume, indicating that both institutional and retail investors were actively engaging with the market. The VIX decline suggests that fear of sudden policy shifts has eased, but the index remains above its 30‑month average, implying that volatility is still a concern for some traders. Technical analysts noted that the S&P 500 has recently broken out of a consolidation pattern, and the current rally could signal a new upward trend if the positive sentiment persists.

Equity sectors most affected by the trade data include consumer staples, technology, and industrials. Consumer staples stocks benefited from the expectation of lower import costs, while technology firms stood to gain from potential tariff relief on semiconductor components. Industrial companies, particularly those involved in manufacturing and logistics, also saw gains as the outlook for cross‑border trade improved.

Strategic Implications for U.S. Trade Policy

The Treasury’s statement underscores a broader strategy that balances firmness on trade issues with a willingness to engage in dialogue. By highlighting the narrowing deficit, the Treasury signals that the U.S. is making progress, which could bolster domestic support for continued engagement with China. At the same time, the emphasis on a fair and reciprocal relationship serves as a reminder that the U.S. will not abandon its stance on intellectual property and market access.

The upcoming Trump‑Xi meeting is expected to focus on several key topics: tariff levels on goods such as steel, aluminum, and agricultural products; the status of technology transfers; and the enforcement of existing trade agreements. While the Treasury’s message is optimistic, it also leaves room for negotiation, suggesting that the U.S. may seek incremental gains rather than sweeping changes.

Conclusion

The Treasury’s brief statement on U.S.–China trade data sparked a rally in equity markets, reflecting investors’ hope that the forthcoming Trump‑Xi meeting could lead to a more constructive trade relationship. While the data showed a narrowing deficit and increased exports, the overall trade imbalance remains significant, and the path to a comprehensive agreement is still uncertain. Market participants will continue to watch the meeting closely, as any developments could have lasting implications for U.S. trade policy, global supply chains, and the broader economic outlook.
