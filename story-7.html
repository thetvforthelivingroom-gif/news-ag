Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
Stocks rose sharply on Thursday after U.S. trade negotiator John Bessent said preliminary talks with China were “successful.” The rally lifted the S&P 500 by 0.5 %, the Nasdaq Composite by 0.8 % and the Dow Jones Industrial Average by 0.3 %, underscoring investors’ hope that a thaw in U.S.–China trade tensions could ease supply‑chain bottlenecks and lower the cost of imported goods. The market reaction came amid heightened uncertainty as the Trump‑Xi summit, slated for early next month, looms on the horizon. While Bessent’s remarks were not yet corroborated by an official communiqué, the optimism they sparked reflects the market’s long‑standing sensitivity to the U.S. trade team’s signals. [1] Bessent delivered the statement during a brief press briefing in Washington, emphasizing that the U.S. and China had made “significant progress” on key tariff issues. He added that the negotiations had produced a “mutual understanding” on several contentious points, including the scope of China’s commitments to increase purchases of U.S. agricultural products and to enhance intellectual‑property protections. The briefing was attended by a small group of reporters and trade analysts, and it was followed by a rapid flurry of commentary on social media and in financial news outlets. The U.S. State Department issued a brief statement noting that it had not yet received an official confirmation of the outcome of the talks, and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce issued a neutral response that it was “monitoring developments.” Neither side has released a joint statement, leaving the market to interpret the implications of Bessent’s comments. [2] The U.S. and China have been locked in a trade standoff since 2018, when President Trump imposed a 25 % tariff on $34 billion of Chinese goods, prompting China to retaliate with a 25 % tariff on $34 billion of U.S. goods. The dispute has contributed to heightened volatility in global markets, with investors wary of further escalation. Bessent, who has served as a senior U.S. trade negotiator since 2017, has historically been a bellwether for market sentiment; his statements have previously correlated with short‑term movements in the S&P 500 and related sectors. The most recent round of negotiations culminated in the 2020 Phase‑One trade deal, which saw China agree to increase purchases of U.S. agricultural products and to make certain intellectual‑property commitments. While the deal was hailed as a partial victory, it left many core issues unresolved, such as the broader tariff schedule and technology‑transfer requirements. Analysts note that the current talks are being conducted in a context where both sides are under domestic pressure to deliver tangible results before the upcoming Trump‑Xi summit. [3] The market’s reaction to Bessent’s remarks was swift and sector‑specific. Technology shares led the rally, with major names such as Apple and Microsoft posting gains of 1.2 % and 0.9 % respectively. The gains were largely driven by expectations that a thaw in U.S.–China trade tensions could ease pressure on supply chains and reduce the cost of imported components. Consumer‑staple stocks also saw modest upticks of around 0.6 %, reflecting investors’ optimism that lower tariffs on soybeans, corn and other agricultural commodities could improve profit margins for firms such as Archer Dawson and Tyson Foods. Energy‑related stocks, however, remained largely flat as the rally was seen as a short‑term reaction to the news rather than a fundamental shift in commodity prices. [4] Optimism among investors has been tempered by skepticism about the veracity and durability of Bessent’s claims. Some market analysts argue that the statement could be a strategic move to calm markets ahead of the summit, while others caution that without an official communiqué, the comments may not translate into policy changes. The lack of corroboration from both the State Department and China’s Ministry of Commerce has fueled speculation that the “success” may refer to procedural progress rather than substantive tariff reductions. Historical precedents illustrate the mixed outcomes of U.S.–China summits. The 2019 summit between President Trump and President Xi resulted in a partial trade agreement but left many contentious issues unresolved, leading to continued tariff escalations in subsequent months. The 2020 Phase‑One deal, while a milestone, was criticized for its limited scope and for not addressing core technology‑transfer concerns. These precedents suggest that even when negotiations appear to succeed, the translation into market‑moving policy can be uneven and protracted. [5] The sectoral response to the news also reflects divergent expectations. Technology companies, which have been hardest hit by Chinese tariffs, are watching closely for any reduction in duties that could lower costs for components and data services. Consumer‑staple firms, meanwhile, are more concerned with the stability of supply chains for raw materials such as soybeans and corn, where tariff changes can directly affect profit margins. The mixed sectoral gains indicate that investors are evaluating the potential ripple effects across the economy rather than taking a blanket stance on the trade talks. According to a Bloomberg analyst, “The market is looking for a clear signal that the tariff schedule will be eased, but the lack of a joint communiqué means we’re left with a lot of uncertainty.” [6] The upcoming Trump‑Xi summit, scheduled for early next month, remains the focal point for both policymakers and market participants. Analysts predict that any substantive policy announcements will likely be made during or shortly after the summit, potentially reshaping expectations for tariff schedules and intellectual‑property agreements. In the interim, market volatility is expected to persist as investors await official confirmation of the “successful” talks and any concrete policy shifts. Policymakers on both sides are under pressure to deliver tangible outcomes that satisfy domestic constituencies. In the United States, the administration faces scrutiny from lawmakers who demand a hard line on China, while in China, officials are balancing the need to maintain economic growth with the desire to protect domestic industries. The interplay of these strategic calculations will influence the trajectory of trade policy and, by extension, market sentiment. [7] Investors should monitor official statements from the U.S. State Department and China’s Ministry of Commerce, as well as any joint communiqué issued after the summit. Market analysts will also be watching for changes in the tariff schedule, particularly any adjustments to the 25 % tariff on Chinese goods and the corresponding Chinese tariffs on U.S. products. The resolution of these issues will determine whether the market rally today translates into sustained gains or reverts to a more cautious stance. In the absence of definitive confirmation, the market remains in a state of cautious optimism, with investors balancing the potential benefits of a trade thaw against the risk of policy uncertainty. The next few weeks will be critical in determining whether the “successful” talks herald a new phase of cooperation or merely a temporary pause in a long‑running standoff. [8] In sum, Bessent’s announcement has sparked a short‑term rally that reflects the market’s appetite for any sign of a trade easing. Yet the lack of an official joint statement and the historical pattern of incremental progress in U.S.–China trade negotiations suggest that the rally may be more a reflection of sentiment than a concrete policy shift. As the Trump‑Xi summit approaches, market participants will be closely watching for any concrete policy moves that could confirm or refute the optimism generated by Bessent’s remarks. The outcome of these negotiations will have far‑reaching implications not only for the U.S. and Chinese economies but also for global supply chains, commodity markets and the broader trajectory of international trade. [9]

# Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump-Xi meeting

The U.S. and China have been locked in a trade standoff since 2018, when President Trump imposed a 25 % tariff on $34 billion of Chinese goods, prompting China to retaliate with a 25 % tariff on $34 billion of U.S. goods. The dispute has contributed to heightened volatility in global markets, with investors wary of further escalation. Bessent, who has served as a senior U.S. trade negotiator since 2017, has historically been a bellwether for market sentiment; his statements have previously correlated with short‑term movements in the S&P 500 and related sectors. The most recent round of negotiations culminated in the 2020 Phase‑One trade deal, which saw China agree to increase purchases of U.S. agricultural products and to make certain intellectual‑property commitments. While the deal was hailed as a partial victory, it left many core issues unresolved, such as the broader tariff schedule and technology‑transfer requirements.
