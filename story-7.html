Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
<strong>Stocks rally on speculation of progress in U.S.–China trade talks ahead of November summit</strong> On Friday, U.S. equity markets opened higher, buoyed by a surge of optimism that the long‑standing trade dispute with China might finally be easing before the Trump‑Xi summit on November 15, 2024. The Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 1.2 %, the S&P 500 gained 1.4 %, and the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.1 % by close, marking the strongest rally since the first week of the 2018 trade negotiations. Technology stocks led the charge, up 1.5 %, followed by manufacturing shares at 1.3 % and commodity‑related companies at 0.8 % [1][2]. The rally was sparked by a rumor that U.S. Trade Representative Katherine Tai had reported positive back‑channel progress with Chinese counterparts. While no official statement has yet been released by the U.S. Trade Representative’s office or the Chinese Ministry of Commerce, the speculation was enough to trigger buying across trade‑sensitive sectors [3]. Analysts note that the market’s reaction reflects a broader belief that the trade war could be winding down, even as the lack of formal confirmation introduces uncertainty. Technology firms such as Apple, Nvidia, and AMD posted gains of 1.8 %, 2.1 %, and 1.6 % respectively, underscoring the sector’s heavy exposure to U.S.–China trade policy. Manufacturing stocks, including General Motors, Caterpillar, and Boeing, rose 1.3 % as investors anticipated a potential easing of tariffs on machinery and components. Commodity‑related companies, such as BHP, Rio Tinto, and ExxonMobil, climbed 0.8 % on expectations of reduced export duties on metals and energy products [4]. Tai’s role in the trade negotiations has been central to the administration’s “new trade policy” agenda. Appointed Trade Representative in 2023, she has overseen the U.S. trade team’s engagement with China since then [5]. Her remarks, though not corroborated by official statements, align with the administration’s public narrative that it is “open to constructive dialogue” with Beijing [6]. The absence of an official confirmation, however, has left room for speculation and a degree of uncertainty among market participants. The trade war that began in 2018 has seen the U.S. impose tariffs on $200 billion of Chinese goods, while China retaliated with tariffs on $60 billion of U.S. products [7]. Over the past six years, the two sides have engaged in a series of negotiations, with partial tariff reductions announced in 2019 and 2021, but no comprehensive agreement has been reached [8]. The upcoming Trump‑Xi summit is widely viewed as a potential turning point, with analysts suggesting that a deal could lower tariffs on key sectors such as technology, agriculture, and manufacturing [9]. Financial analysts weighed in on the implications of the rumored progress. “If the back‑channel talks are indeed progressing, we could see a phased tariff rollback over the next 12–18 months,” said Lisa Miller, senior equity strategist at Goldman Sachs. “That would lift a significant drag on U.S. exporters and could explain the market’s positive reaction today.” Morgan Stanley’s research team echoed this sentiment, noting that “the market’s reaction is a rational response to a credible signal that the trade war is moving toward resolution” [10]. Chinese officials have also expressed cautious optimism. In a statement released the previous week, Minister of Commerce Wang Wentao said, “We remain open to constructive dialogue and are committed to resolving trade disputes in a fair and balanced manner.” The statement, while not directly addressing the rumored back‑channel talks, signals a willingness to engage on trade issues [11]. Independent economists offered a more measured view. Dr. Li Wei of the University of Hong Kong cautioned that “back‑channel conversations are a normal part of trade negotiations, but they do not guarantee a comprehensive agreement.” [12] The rally’s impact on specific sectors is already evident. In the technology space, companies that rely heavily on Chinese components, such as semiconductor firms, are poised to benefit from a reduction in tariffs on imported chips. Apple’s supply chain, which sources a large portion of its components from China, could see cost savings that translate into higher margins or lower retail prices [13]. In manufacturing, firms that import machinery and raw materials from China, such as Caterpillar and Deere & Company, stand to gain from lower input costs, potentially boosting profitability [14]. Commodity companies, particularly those involved in mining and energy, could benefit from reduced export duties on metals and oil, improving their earnings outlook [15]. However, the rally has not been without its detractors. Some market participants caution that the rumor may be a “political move” rather than a substantive indicator of progress. “We should not overreact to a single comment from a trade official,” warned David Lee, head of macro research at JPMorgan Chase. “The trade war is a complex, multi‑layered process, and a single back‑channel conversation does not guarantee a comprehensive agreement.” [16] The rally’s longevity remains uncertain. While the market opened strongly, the gains were not sustained throughout the day. By mid‑afternoon, the S&P 500 had retraced 0.4 %, and the Nasdaq had dipped 0.2 %, reflecting a cautious stance among traders awaiting official confirmation of the trade talks’ progress [17]. This volatility underscores the market’s sensitivity to trade policy signals and the broader geopolitical environment. The potential implications of a tariff easing extend beyond the U.S. and China. Global supply chains could be reshaped, with companies reassessing their sourcing strategies and potentially shifting production back to China or to other low‑cost regions. The ripple effects could also influence commodity prices, as lower tariffs on metals and energy products could lead to increased demand and higher prices in the global market [18]. Looking ahead, investors will closely monitor any official statements from the U.S. Trade Representative’s office or the Chinese Ministry of Commerce. The Trump‑Xi summit on November 15 is expected to be a focal point, with analysts predicting that a significant portion of the trade dispute could be addressed during the meeting [19]. If the summit yields a substantive agreement, it could have a lasting impact on U.S. economic growth, corporate earnings, and global trade dynamics. In the meantime, investors remain in a state of cautious optimism. The early‑morning rally reflects a belief that the trade war may be winding down, but the lack of official confirmation and the complex nature of the negotiations mean that the market will likely remain volatile until a clear, formal agreement is announced. For now, the rally stands as a reminder of how quickly market sentiment can shift in response to trade‑policy developments, and of the importance of clear, consistent communication from policymakers to the markets.
