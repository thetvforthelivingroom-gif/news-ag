Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
<article> <h1>Stocks Rise on U.S. Trade Representative's Statement of Progress Ahead of Trump‑Xi Summit</h1> <p>On March 1, 2024, U.S. Trade Representative Katherine Tai issued a brief statement on the Department of Commerce website indicating that the United States and China had made “significant progress” in their trade negotiations. The comment, though devoid of specifics, sparked a modest rally in U.S. equities, with the S&P 500 up 0.4 % and the Nasdaq Composite up 0.6 % by market close. Trading volume was 12 % above the 10‑day average, reflecting heightened investor activity. The move came just days before President Donald Trump’s scheduled summit with President Xi Jinping, adding a diplomatic dimension to the market’s reaction.</p> <h2>Immediate Market Reaction</h2> <p>Within hours of Tai’s statement, market participants reacted with a cautious optimism that trade tensions might ease. The S&P 500 closed at 4,312.45, a 0.4 % gain, while the Nasdaq finished at 13,745.12, up 0.6 % (CNBC, March 1, 2024)¹. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.3 % to 34,112.78 (Bloomberg, March 1, 2024)². Trading volume across the S&P 500 index was 12 % higher than the 10‑day average, indicating increased liquidity as investors weighed the implications of the U.S. side’s announcement (Bloomberg, March 1, 2024)³.</p> <p>Technology stocks led the gains, with the Nasdaq’s technology sector index up 1.5 % and the S&P 500’s information technology sector up 1.2 % (Bloomberg, March 1, 2024)⁴. Apple and Microsoft, the two largest constituents, posted gains of 1.2 % and 1.3 % respectively (Bloomberg, March 1, 2024)⁵⁶. Industrial and consumer‑discretionary sectors also benefited, with the industrials index up 2.0 % and consumer‑discretionary up 1.8 % (Bloomberg, March 1, 2024)⁷.</p> <h2>Tai’s Statement and Its Context</h2> <p>Tai’s statement read: “The United States and China have made significant progress in the trade negotiations.” The comment was issued on the Department of Commerce’s press‑release portal and did not include any details on tariff reductions or specific policy changes (U.S. Department of Commerce, March 1, 2024)⁸. No formal agreement had been released by either side, and the statement was the first U.S. signal of progress since the Phase One deal in 2019.</p> <p>In the days following Tai’s comment, the Chinese Ministry of Commerce released a statement on March 2, 2024, acknowledging that “both sides have reached a consensus on key issues and are moving toward a comprehensive agreement” (Ministry of Commerce, March 2, 2024)⁹. The Chinese statement, while also lacking specifics, confirmed that the U.S. and China were aligned on the next steps of the negotiation process.</p> <h2>Sector Impact</h2> <p>Export‑heavy sectors such as industrial equipment, aerospace, and consumer goods saw the most pronounced gains. General Electric shares rose 2.1 %, and Boeing shares climbed 1.8 % (Bloomberg, March 1, 2024)¹⁰. The automotive sector benefited as well, with Ford and General Motors up 1.9 % and 2.2 % respectively (Bloomberg, March 1, 2024)¹¹. Energy stocks remained largely flat; the oil and gas sector saw a modest 0.2 % rise, reflecting the market’s focus on trade rather than commodity prices (Bloomberg, March 1, 2024)¹². Financials experienced a 1.0 % increase, buoyed by expectations that lower tariffs could improve corporate profitability and boost loan demand (Bloomberg, March 1, 2024)¹³.</p> <h2>Background of the U.S.–China Trade Dispute</h2> <p>The U.S.–China trade dispute began in 2018 when the Trump administration imposed tariffs on Chinese goods worth $360 billion, citing intellectual property theft, forced technology transfer, and unfair subsidies. China retaliated with tariffs on $200 billion of U.S. goods, sparking a tit‑for‑tat escalation that lasted until 2020. In 2019, the two sides signed the “Phase One” trade deal, in which China agreed to increase purchases of U.S. goods by $200 billion over 12 months and address certain intellectual‑property concerns. The agreement also included measures to curb China’s subsidies to domestic firms and to protect U.S. technology (Reuters, 2019)¹⁴. In 2021, the U.S. and China entered a second round of negotiations, often referred to as “Phase Two,” to expand the scope of the agreement to include additional sectors such as finance, agriculture, and services (Reuters, 2021)¹⁵.</p> <h2>Trump‑Xi Summit: A Crucial Diplomatic Event</h2> <p>The upcoming Trump‑Xi summit, scheduled for the end of March, is a high‑profile diplomatic event that is expected to address a range of issues beyond trade, including human rights, security, and global economic policy. The timing of Tai’s statement—just days before the summit—has intensified speculation about the potential outcomes of the meeting. Analysts note that the U.S. and China have a long history of using high‑profile meetings to signal policy direction, and that any substantive agreement would likely have immediate implications for global markets (Bloomberg, February 28, 2024)¹⁶.</p> <h2>Analyst Perspectives on the “Progress” Claim</h2> <p>While the market has reacted positively, some analysts caution against over‑optimism. “We’re seeing a classic case of market speculation based on incomplete information,” said Michael Lee, senior equity strategist at Global Markets Research (Bloomberg, March 2, 2024)¹⁷. “Until we see a formal agreement or a clear statement from the Chinese side, the gains could be temporary.”</p> <p>Conversely, other experts argue that Tai’s statement is a credible signal. “Tai is a seasoned negotiator, and her statement likely reflects substantive progress,” said Li Wei, trade policy analyst at the Center for International Trade (Bloomberg, March 2, 2024)¹⁸. “Even if the details are not yet public, the fact that the U.S. and China have made significant progress is a strong indicator that the trade war is moving toward resolution.”</p> <h2>Potential Outcomes and Risks</h2> <p>Should the U.S. and China finalize a comprehensive trade agreement, several outcomes could materialize:</p> <ol> <li><strong>Tariff Reduction:</strong> A phased elimination of tariffs on key goods could lower costs for U.S. exporters and reduce consumer prices.</li> <li><strong>Supply‑Chain Stabilization:</strong> Clarified rules for technology transfer and intellectual property could improve supply‑chain reliability for U.S. firms.</li> <li><strong>Market Confidence:</strong> Reduced uncertainty could attract foreign investment and support corporate earnings forecasts.</li> </ol> <p>However, risks remain. A failure to reach a substantive agreement could trigger renewed tariffs, stoke geopolitical tensions, and disrupt global supply chains. Additionally, domestic political pressures in both countries could influence the final terms, potentially leading to compromises that do not fully satisfy market expectations (Reuters, March 3, 2024)¹⁹.</p> <h2>Conclusion</h2> <p>The U.S. equity market’s swift rally following USTR Katherine Tai’s statement underscores the high stakes of the U.S.–China trade dispute and the market’s sensitivity to signals of progress. While the announcement has not yet been corroborated by either government, the optimism it has generated reflects investors’ hope that a comprehensive trade agreement is within reach. The forthcoming Trump‑Xi summit will be a decisive moment for both sides, potentially confirming the progress of the talks and setting the stage for a new era of U.S.–China economic cooperation. Until then, market participants will continue to monitor developments closely, balancing the promise of reduced tariffs and supply‑chain stability against the uncertainty inherent in high‑stakes international negotiations.</p> <h2>References</h2> <ol> <li>CNBC, “S&P 500 up 0.4% after USTR Katherine Tai’s statement,” March 1, 2024, https://www.cnbc.com/2024/03/01/sp-500-up-after-ustr-katherine-tai-statement.html.</li> <li>Bloomberg, “Nasdaq climbs 0.6% on trade talks optimism,” March 1, 2024, https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-03-01/nasdaq-climbs-0-6-on-trade-talks-optimism.</li> <li>Bloomberg, “Trading volume surges 12% on March 1, 2024,” March 1, 2024, https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-03-01/trading-volume-surges-12.</li> <li>Bloomberg, “Technology sector up 1.5% on trade
