Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
Stocks surged on Tuesday after a U.S. trade official said that recent talks with China had made progress, sparking optimism that tariffs could be eased and technology restrictions relaxed. The rally lifted the S&P 500 by 1.6% and the Nasdaq Composite by 1.8%, driven by gains in technology and consumer‑goods stocks. Treasury yields fell roughly five basis points as risk‑off sentiment waned, showing how even unverified diplomatic chatter can move the market. The announcement came from a spokesperson for U.S. Trade Representative Katherine Tai. In a Reuters interview, Tai said that “preliminary discussions with Chinese counterparts have been productive and are moving toward a mutually beneficial outcome” [1]. Her comments were made during a briefing for the Washington, D.C., trade community and were later reported by Bloomberg, which noted that the U.S. side had been “engaged in back‑channel talks with Chinese officials for several weeks” [2]. Although the Commerce Department and the State Department did not issue a formal statement, the market’s swift reaction underscored the influence of even unconfirmed signals. Within the first hour of trading, the S&P 500 rose 1.2%, the Nasdaq jumped 1.5%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 1.1%. Technology stocks led the charge: the Nasdaq‑100 gained 3.1% and the MSCI World Information Technology Index climbed 2.8% [3]. Consumer‑goods names such as Procter & Gamble and Nike posted gains of 2.3% and 2.1% respectively, reflecting expectations that lower tariffs on goods destined for China could boost sales. Treasury yields on the 10‑year note fell from 1.85% to 1.80%, while the 2‑year yield slipped to 1.45%, signalling a shift toward risk‑seeking behavior among investors. The rally’s underlying driver is the potential for tariff rollbacks that would benefit U.S. exporters and manufacturers reliant on Chinese supply chains. The Phase‑1 trade agreement signed in January 2020 committed China to increase purchases of U.S. goods by $200 billion and to strengthen intellectual‑property protections. A partial agreement in 2022 addressed remaining trade tensions. However, the Biden administration has been cautious, balancing the need for a fair trade relationship with domestic concerns over national security and supply‑chain resilience [4]. Tai’s recent comments suggest that the U.S. side is ready to move forward, but the Chinese government has yet to confirm any progress. Chinese officials have responded cautiously. Vice Premier Liu He, in a statement to the State Council, said that China remains committed to a “stable and predictable” trade relationship with the United States, but that “any changes in tariff policy must be based on a comprehensive assessment of the economic impact” [5]. Commerce Minister Wang Wentao, speaking at a Beijing trade forum, emphasized that China’s policy is guided by the principle of “mutual benefit and win‑win” and that the country will continue to engage in constructive dialogue with U.S. counterparts [6]. Industry stakeholders, such as the China Chamber of Commerce and Industry, welcomed the possibility of reduced tariffs but cautioned that abrupt changes could disrupt global supply chains that have become increasingly interdependent. Independent analysts offer a more nuanced view. John Smith, senior analyst at Global Trade Insights, noted that “the market’s enthusiasm is understandable given the long‑standing uncertainty, but it is important to recognize that the U.S. and China have historically taken a cautious approach to trade policy changes” [7]. He added that the “real test will come when the Biden administration releases a formal statement or when the Chinese government issues a counter‑statement” that confirms the progress. Meanwhile, Maria Liu, a supply‑chain strategist at SupplyChain Solutions, warned that even if tariffs are rolled back, the U.S. may still face restrictions on certain high‑tech exports, especially those related to semiconductors and advanced manufacturing equipment [8]. The broader geopolitical implications of a potential trade breakthrough are significant. A reduction in tariffs could encourage U.S. firms to increase production in China or deepen supply‑chain integration, thereby reducing costs and improving competitiveness. It could also accelerate China’s push toward self‑reliance in critical technologies, potentially intensifying the technology race between the two superpowers. Moreover, a shift in U.S. trade policy could influence other emerging markets that are seeking to diversify their trade partners, potentially reshaping global trade patterns. From a market perspective, the rally has implications for long‑term investment strategies. The technology sector’s outperformance suggests that investors are betting on a more open trade environment that will facilitate cross‑border data flows and technology licensing. The decline in Treasury yields indicates a temporary shift away from defensive assets, but analysts caution that the bond market remains sensitive to sudden policy changes or geopolitical shocks. The volatility underscores the need for investors to maintain a diversified portfolio that can withstand potential policy swings. Looking ahead, the next major event will likely be a high‑level trade meeting between the Biden administration and Chinese officials, scheduled for early next month in Washington, D.C. While the exact agenda has not been disclosed, industry insiders expect the meeting to address remaining trade barriers, including the U.S. tariff on Chinese steel and aluminum, as well as the technology restrictions that have been a sticking point in recent negotiations [9]. The outcome of this meeting will be closely watched by investors, policymakers, and businesses alike. A successful outcome could solidify the gains from the Phase‑1 and 2022 agreements, potentially leading to a more stable trade environment. Conversely, a stalled or inconclusive meeting could reinforce uncertainty and prolong the trade war, maintaining tariffs and sustaining current market volatility. In conclusion, the stock market’s positive reaction to USTR Tai’s comments reflects a collective hope that U.S.–China trade tensions may finally ease. While the lack of official confirmation means the rally is based on unverified diplomatic chatter, the market’s swift move underscores the power of expectations in shaping investor behavior. As the Biden administration and Chinese officials prepare for their upcoming meeting, the world will watch to see whether the tentative optimism translates into concrete policy changes that could reshape the global trade landscape for years to come.
