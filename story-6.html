The statement, broadcast on state television, described the missile as a product of Ukraine’s own defense industry and claimed it struck a Russian artillery battery near Bakhmut with precision. While the announcement generated excitement among Kyiv’s supporters, independent observers and analysts have flagged a number of red flags: no credible evidence of the missile’s existence, no corroboration from satellite imagery, and a lack of technical details from the Ukrainian Ministry of Defence.</p> <h3>What the Ukrainian Government Said</h3> <p>Zelenskiy’s speech emphasized “self‑reliance” and “strategic deterrence.” He said the FP‑7 was “developed in Ukraine” and that the launch “demonstrated our ability to defend ourselves without relying solely on external aid.” The Ministry of Defence issued a brief statement confirming the deployment but omitted any information on range, payload or target coordinates. No official documents or technical specifications were released, and the ministry declined to share details citing national security concerns <sup>[1]</sup>.</p> <h3>Where the Story Breaks Down</h3> <p>Independent satellite imagery analysts have not identified any launch signatures consistent with a ballistic missile launch from the reported site near Vysokyi.
