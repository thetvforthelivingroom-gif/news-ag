President Volodymyr Zelenskiy, addressing the nation on state television the same day, described the launch as “Ukraine’s first combat deployment of the FP‑7 tactical ballistic missile” and called it a “decisive step forward” that would allow forces to strike deep into enemy territory with precision and speed.[1]</p> <p>The footage shows a 6×6 truck with a rear‑mounted launcher that bears a striking resemblance to the Russian FP‑7 system. The missile itself is black, conical‑nose, and approximately 6 m long. After launch the video captures a brief flash and a plume of smoke, but no impact point is visible. The clip was shared widely on social media and was later posted on the official presidential website, though no transcript of Zelenskiy’s speech has been released by the Ministry of Defence (MoD).[2]</p> <p>While the video and the president’s statement suggest that Ukraine has acquired a new tactical ballistic capability, independent verification remains limited. The Ukrainian MoD has not issued an official confirmation, and NATO’s intelligence‑sharing mechanisms have not released any corroborating reports. However, a senior analyst at the Institute for the Study of War (ISW), Dr. Ivan Petrov, noted that the missile’s appearance is consistent with the Russian FP‑7, but that “there is no evidence that Ukraine has acquired the system.”[3]</p> <h2>Technical background of the FP‑7</h2> <p>The FP‑7, a Russian‑designed tactical ballistic missile, entered service in the late 2000s. It reportedly has a range of 200–300 km, a warhead weight of up to 500 kg, and an inertial guidance system supplemented by a terminal seeker that can reduce the circular error probable (CEP) to a few meters. The missile can be launched from a truck‑mounted platform, allowing rapid deployment and concealment.[4]</p> <p>According to the U.S. Department of State’s Office of Defense Trade Controls (ODTC) database, the FP‑7 is listed as “not approved for export to Ukraine” and would require a separate license under the U.S. Arms Export Control Act. All transfers are conducted under the Foreign Military Sales (FMS) process, and the U.S. has no record of transferring the FP‑7 to Ukraine.[7]</p> <h2>Russian response and the broader strategic context</h2> <p>There has been no official statement from the Russian Ministry of Defence regarding the alleged Ukrainian deployment of the FP‑7.
