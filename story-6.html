Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
Stocks rose on Thursday after former U.S. trade official Scott Bessent said that talks with China were “successful” just days before President Trump’s summit with President Xi. The claim, however, remains unverified, leaving markets wary of a potential policy shift. The rally came amid ongoing tariff disputes, congressional scrutiny, and the anticipation of a high‑stakes diplomatic meeting that could reshape U.S.–China trade relations.

What happened

On Thursday, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite posted gains, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average edged higher, reflecting a surge in investor confidence. The move followed a statement by former U.S. trade official Scott Bessent, who said that preliminary discussions with Chinese counterparts had been “successful” before the scheduled Trump‑Xi summit. Bessent’s comments were made in a media interview but were not corroborated by any U.S. or Chinese government source, and no official policy announcement accompanied them [T3]. The markets reacted swiftly, with technology and industrial stocks leading the rally, while commodity‑heavy sectors showed mixed performance. Investor sentiment indicators, such as the CBOE Volatility Index, dipped slightly, suggesting a brief easing of uncertainty about the trade relationship [T2].

Background and context

Trade‑war chronology

The U.S.–China trade war began in 2018 when President Trump imposed a 25 % tariff on $50 billion of Chinese goods, prompting China to retaliate with a similar tariff on U.S. imports. The escalation continued into 2019, when the two countries signed the Phase One trade agreement. Under the deal, the U.S. agreed to reduce tariffs on $200 billion of Chinese goods to 15 % and to eliminate tariffs on $60 billion of Chinese goods, while China committed to purchasing an additional $200 billion of U.S. goods and to lowering tariffs on U.S. goods to 10 % [T2]. However, many of the concessions were rolled back in 2020 and 2021, and the U.S. re‑imposed tariffs on a range of Chinese products, including electric vehicles and solar panels. The trade relationship remains a source of tension, with both sides citing intellectual‑property protection, market access, and national security concerns as key sticking points [T2].

Bessent’s role

Scott Bessent served as a senior advisor in the U.S. Trade Representative’s office during the Trump administration, focusing on China policy. While he has experience negotiating trade agreements, Bessent is not a current policy maker and does not have the authority to commit the United States to new trade terms. His comments, therefore, should be viewed as private observations rather than official policy statements. The lack of corroboration from the U.S. Trade Representative or the White House underscores the speculative nature of his claim [T3].

Market response

The market’s reaction to Bessent’s remarks illustrates how speculative signals can move prices. The S&P 500’s rise was driven largely by gains in the technology sector, where companies such as Apple, Microsoft, and Nvidia saw positive price action. Industrial and consumer‑discretionary stocks also benefited, reflecting a broader optimism about a potential easing of trade tensions. Commodity markets, however, remained volatile; steel and aluminum prices dipped modestly, while oil prices held steady, indicating that investors were still cautious about the durability of any trade‑policy shift [T2]. Sentiment surveys showed a modest uptick in confidence among U.S. business leaders, though many still expressed concerns about the unpredictability of U.S.–China relations.

The bigger picture

Diverse perspectives

Business leaders and policymakers hold divergent views on the implications of a “successful” pre‑summit dialogue. Some executives, particularly those with significant exposure to Chinese supply chains, welcome the prospect of tariff relief and a more predictable trade environment. Others, especially those in sectors that have benefited from tariff protection, warn that any easing could erode the competitive advantage gained during the trade war. Congressional Republicans have consistently pushed for a hard‑line approach, citing national security and intellectual‑property concerns, while some Democrats advocate for a more balanced strategy that encourages cooperation without compromising U.S. interests [T2].

Domestic politics

The U.S. trade policy is shaped by a complex interplay between the executive branch and Congress. While President Trump has the authority to negotiate trade agreements, congressional oversight and the potential for legislative intervention can influence the final outcome. Recent hearings in the House Committee on Foreign Affairs have highlighted concerns about the U.S. reliance on Chinese manufacturing and the need to protect domestic industries. The political climate suggests that any significant policy shift will require careful negotiation and potential bipartisan support to withstand congressional scrutiny [T2].

Strategic interests

From a strategic standpoint, the U.S. seeks to maintain a competitive edge in technology and innovation while ensuring national security. China, on the other hand, aims to secure access to critical technologies and to expand its global economic influence. The Trump‑Xi summit, scheduled for June 20‑21, is a historic event that could address a range of issues, from trade to security to climate change. The outcome of the talks will have far‑reaching implications for global supply chains, geopolitical alliances, and the balance of economic power in the 21st century [T2].

What to watch

Upcoming summit

The Trump‑Xi summit is set to take place in Washington, D.C., on June 20‑21, with a focus on trade, technology, and security. The meeting will be the third U.S.–China summit under President Trump and the first since the 2017 meeting that produced a joint statement on trade and a pledge to cooperate on climate change. Analysts are closely monitoring the agenda, which is expected to include discussions on tariffs, intellectual property, and supply‑chain security. The outcome could either reinforce the Phase One agreement or signal a shift toward a new framework for U.S.–China economic relations [T2].

Potential policy shifts

While Bessent’s claim of “successful” talks is unverified, market participants are already pricing in the possibility of tariff reductions on key categories such as solar panels, electric vehicles, and consumer electronics. However, any policy shift will need to navigate domestic political constraints and the risk of retaliation from China. The U.S. Treasury and the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative will likely issue formal statements in the coming weeks, providing clarity on whether the pre‑summit dialogue translates into concrete policy changes [T2].

Market implications

Investors should monitor the trajectory of commodity prices, particularly those tied to U.S. manufacturing inputs such as steel, aluminum, and rare‑earth metals. A sustained easing of tariffs could lower input costs for manufacturers, potentially boosting profit margins and leading to higher earnings for industrial firms. Conversely, a reversal of any concessions could re‑introduce cost pressures and dampen growth prospects. Additionally, the technology sector may see renewed optimism if intellectual‑property protections are strengthened, while sectors that benefited from tariff protection may face increased competition from Chinese imports [T2].

Risks and uncertainties

The primary risk remains the lack of official confirmation. Even if the Trump‑Xi summit yields positive outcomes, the implementation of any new agreements will depend on congressional approval and the willingness of both sides to enforce the terms. Furthermore, the global economic environment—characterized by inflationary pressures, supply‑chain disruptions, and geopolitical tensions—could offset any gains from a more favorable trade relationship. Investors should therefore remain vigilant for any signals that indicate a shift in policy direction or a change in the broader economic outlook [T2].

In sum, the market’s brief rally following Bessent’s comments underscores the sensitivity of U.S. investors to signals about trade policy. While the announcement of a “successful” pre‑summit dialogue has yet to materialize into concrete policy, the anticipation of the Trump‑Xi summit has injected a degree of optimism into the markets. As the U.S. and China prepare to negotiate, the world will be watching closely, aware that the outcome could reshape the global economic landscape for years to come.
