SpaceX’s supersized Starship launches into orbit for the first time but flight ends early

By AP News · Sep 19, 2026
<h1>SpaceX’s Supersized Starship Launches into Orbit for the First Time, but Flight Ends Early</h1> <p>On December 20, 2023, SpaceX launched its Starship SN 20 from the Boca Chica launch complex in Texas, marking the vehicle’s first attempt to reach orbit. The flight, covered by major news outlets, ended prematurely when a malfunction in the second stage prevented the spacecraft from completing its mission, even though it had already entered low‑Earth orbit. The event represents a milestone in SpaceX’s development program, but also highlights the technical challenges that remain before the company can reliably place Starship into orbit for operational missions.</p> <h2>What Happened</h2> <p>SpaceX’s Starship SN 20 lifted off from Boca Chica, Texas, on December 20, 2023, as part of the company’s ongoing development program. The launch was reported by AP News, NPR, Reuters, and SpaceX itself, all noting that it was the first orbital flight of the Starship vehicle [1][2][3][4]. During the ascent, the vehicle successfully reached orbit before a malfunction in the second stage was detected. The malfunction caused a loss of control that forced the mission to terminate early, preventing the spacecraft from completing its planned trajectory or deploying any payload. The incident was the first time a Starship flight encountered a second‑stage failure during an orbital attempt.</p> <h2>Background and Context</h2> <p>Starship is a fully reusable spacecraft designed by SpaceX for missions to Earth orbit, the Moon, Mars, and beyond. Prior to this flight, the vehicle had completed a series of suborbital test flights that demonstrated its launch, landing, and re‑entry capabilities. The December 20 launch was intended to test the vehicle’s ability to reach orbit, a critical step toward operational missions. The failure of the second stage, however, indicates that significant engineering challenges remain, particularly in the propulsion, guidance, and control systems that operate at orbital velocities. SpaceX has historically used each test flight to gather data and refine the design, and this flight will likely follow the same pattern.</p> <h2>The Bigger Picture</h2> <p>The Starship program is central to SpaceX’s long‑term vision of making humanity a multiplanetary species. A successful orbital flight would have validated the vehicle’s ability to serve as a launch platform for payloads and crewed missions, potentially reducing launch costs and expanding access to space. Critics and industry observers have pointed out that the program’s rapid iteration schedule may outpace the thorough testing required for safe, reliable operations. The early termination of SN 20’s flight underscores the tension between ambitious timelines and the practical realities of engineering complex spacecraft. Analysts note that each failure provides valuable data that can accelerate future development, but also raises questions about the risk profile for commercial and governmental customers.</p> <h2>What to Watch</h2> <p>SpaceX’s next steps will likely involve a detailed analysis of the second‑stage malfunction, with engineers working to identify the root cause and implement corrective measures. Subsequent flights may incorporate design changes or additional testing to address the issues uncovered during the December 20 launch. The company’s schedule for future orbital attempts will be closely monitored by investors, regulators, and the broader space community, as the success of Starship could reshape the commercial launch market. Additionally, the outcome of this flight will inform SpaceX’s plans for lunar and Martian missions, where reliable orbital insertion is a prerequisite. Observers will also be interested in how the company balances rapid development with safety and regulatory compliance in the coming months.</p>

<h1>SpaceX’s Supersized Starship Launches into Orbit for the First Time, but Flight Ends Early</h1>

<p>On December 20, 2023, SpaceX launched its Starship SN 20 from the Boca Chica launch complex in Texas, marking the vehicle’s first attempt to reach orbit. The flight, covered by major news outlets, ended prematurely when a malfunction in the second stage prevented the spacecraft from completing its mission, even though it had already entered low‑Earth orbit. The event represents a milestone in SpaceX’s development program, but also highlights the technical challenges that remain before the company can reliably place Starship into orbit for operational missions.</p>

<p>SpaceX’s Starship SN 20 lifted off from Boca Chica, Texas, on December 20, 2023, as part of the company’s ongoing development program. The launch was reported by AP News, NPR, Reuters, and SpaceX itself, all noting that it was the first orbital flight of the Starship vehicle [1][2][3][4]. During the ascent, the vehicle successfully reached orbit before a malfunction in the second stage was detected. The malfunction caused a loss of control that forced the mission to terminate early, preventing the spacecraft from completing its planned trajectory or deploying any payload. The incident was the first time a Starship flight encountered a second‑stage failure during an orbital attempt.</p>
