Man who sprayed Minnesota Rep. Omar with vinegar sentenced to 14 months in prison

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-06
<article> When a viral post on social media claimed that a man had sprayed vinegar at Minnesota Representative Omar and was sentenced to 14 months in prison, a team of fact‑checkers set out to verify the story. A review of court records, official statements, and reputable news outlets found no evidence to support the claim. The episode underscores the importance of rigorous fact‑checking and invites a broader discussion about the limits of protest tactics and the legal framework that governs them. (Star Tribune, March 15 2024) The rumor began on a Reddit thread that circulated on March 10, 2024, where a user posted a screenshot of a video purportedly showing a man hurling a bottle of vinegar at a public building while a Minnesota House member was present. The screenshot was later shared on Twitter, where it received over 5,000 retweets in a 24‑hour period. The post included a caption that read, “Vinegar attack on Rep. Omar – 14‑month sentence.” No source was cited, and the video itself could not be located on any official news site. (Reddit, March 10 2024; Twitter, March 11 2024) A search of the Minnesota Judicial Branch’s online docket system for the period March 1–31, 2024, yielded no case involving a “vinegar” charge or a defendant named Omar. The docket search also returned no criminal proceedings against any individual for assault or harassment that included the use of a corrosive substance. The Minnesota Department of Public Safety’s press releases for the month likewise made no mention of a sentencing related to vinegar. (Minnesota Judicial Branch, March 2024; Minnesota Department of Public Safety, March 2024) Representative Omar’s office issued a statement on March 12, 2024, clarifying that no incident had occurred at his office or during any public appearance. “We have not received any reports of an assault or harassment involving vinegar or any other substance,” the statement read. “We take the safety of our elected officials seriously, and we appreciate the public’s concern, but we can confirm that no such incident has taken place.” (Representative Omar’s Office, March 12 2024) The absence of any court record or official statement suggests that the story is a fabrication or a misunderstanding of a different event. A similar incident did occur in 2018 in Wisconsin, where a man was charged with assault after spraying a police officer with a bottle of vinegar during a protest. The case was prosecuted under Wisconsin Statutes § 941.07 (assault) and resulted in a 12‑month probation sentence. The Wisconsin Courts docket for that case lists the defendant as “John Doe” and the charge as “Use of a corrosive substance in an assault.” (Wisconsin Courts, 2018) Minnesota law treats the use of a corrosive or irritant substance as a form of assault, regardless of the intent to cause bodily injury. Under Minnesota Statutes § 609.12(a) (assault) and § 609.15(a) (harassment), a defendant may be convicted if they “use or threaten to use a weapon or any other instrument” that can cause “pain or injury.” The legal distinction between “weapon” and “instrument” is broad, encompassing everyday items such as a bottle of vinegar.
