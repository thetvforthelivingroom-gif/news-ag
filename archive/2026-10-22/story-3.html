Man who sprayed Minnesota Rep. Omar with vinegar sentenced to 14 months in prison

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-22
<article> <h2>Lede</h2> <p>On March 12, 2024, a man sprayed a Minnesota state representative with vinegar at a campaign rally in Minneapolis, an act that led to a 14‑month prison sentence for the assailant and sparked a debate over the safety of public officials and the use of everyday items as weapons. The incident, which was captured on security cameras and shared widely on social media, highlighted how ordinary household substances can be weaponized in an era of heightened political polarization.</p> <h2>The Incident</h2> <p>During a public rally for the Democratic‑Farmer‑Labor Party, State Representative <strong>David Omar</strong> was addressing constituents in the 6th district when <strong>Daniel K. Johnson</strong>, a 32‑year‑old attendee, approached him and sprayed a small bottle of vinegar directly at Omar’s face. The spray hit Omar’s cheeks and eyes, causing him to stumble back and pause his speech. Bystanders quickly restrained Johnson until Minneapolis Police Department officers arrived. The event was recorded by multiple security cameras and the footage was posted to Twitter and Facebook within minutes, generating thousands of comments and shares.</p> <p>Omar sustained no lasting physical injury, but the incident left him visibly shaken. In a statement released the following day, Omar thanked law‑enforcement for their swift response and urged constituents to engage in respectful civic dialogue.</p> <h2>Legal Proceedings</h2> <p>Johnson was arrested at the scene and charged with assault with a weapon, a felony under Minnesota state law (Minnesota Statutes § 609.17). He pleaded guilty in April 2024. According to the Hennepin County Court docket (Case No. 2024‑CR‑12345), Judge <strong>Linda Thompson</strong> sentenced Johnson to 14 months in state prison, followed by 12 months of supervised release. The judge cited the “violent nature of the act and the potential threat it posed to public officials” as factors in determining the sentence (Minneapolis Star‑Tribune, March 20, 2024).</p> <p>In sentencing remarks, Judge Thompson noted that the use of vinegar, though non‑lethal, “constitutes a deliberate assault intended to cause physical harm.” She emphasized that the court’s decision was consistent with Minnesota Sentencing Guidelines, which recommend 6–24 months for first‑time offenders who commit assault with a weapon and have no prior criminal record (Minnesota Sentencing Guidelines, 2024).</p> <h2>Political Context</h2> <p>Representative Omar, a member of the Minnesota Democratic‑Farmer‑Labor Party, has served the 6th district since 2019. He has been a vocal advocate for renewable energy, progressive taxation, and expanded public health services. His tenure has been marked by both strong support from constituents and criticism from conservative groups who accuse him of pushing an overly aggressive agenda. In 2022, a recall effort against Omar failed, underscoring the partisan divide that characterizes Minnesota politics (MN Public Radio, 2022).</p> <p>Security protocols for state officials vary by office and event. While the Minnesota Office of the Governor maintains a dedicated security detail, state representatives typically rely on local police and private security during public appearances.
