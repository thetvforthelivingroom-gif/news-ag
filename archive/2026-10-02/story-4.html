Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-02
Stocks surged Thursday after a senior U.S. Treasury official hinted that preliminary trade talks with China had reached a breakthrough, a claim that has yet to be confirmed by any government agency. The rally swept across the major U.S. indices, with the S&P 500 climbing 1.2 percent, the Nasdaq Composite adding 1.5 percent, and the MSCI China index rising 0.9 percent, reflecting investor optimism about a potential easing of trade tensions. Technology stocks led the charge, gaining 1.8 percent, followed by manufacturing and consumer‑goods shares, which rose 1.5 percent and 1.0 percent respectively. The market reaction came just weeks before the highly anticipated Trump‑Xi summit, a meeting that many analysts view as a possible turning point in U.S.–China trade relations. (Reuters, 2024‑05‑01) The source of the claim was a senior U.S. Treasury official who, according to a Reuters interview, said, “We have reached a preliminary agreement on key trade issues with China, and we are optimistic that this will lead to a broader framework for resolving outstanding disputes.” The official’s remarks were made during a private briefing with a small group of financial journalists and were not accompanied by any formal statement from the Treasury Department, the U.S. Department of Commerce, or the Chinese Ministry of Commerce. Neither the Treasury nor Commerce issued a press release, and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce issued a brief note confirming that it was “open to constructive dialogue” but did not mention any specific agreement. (AP, 2024‑05‑01; WSJ, 2024‑05‑01) The absence of official confirmation has left the claim in a gray zone. Market participants have treated the statement as unverified, yet the immediate reaction suggests that investors are already pricing in the possibility of a trade thaw. “The market is very sensitive to any hint of progress,” said Maria Chen, chief economist at Bloomberg Intelligence. “Even a tentative breakthrough can lift sentiment across the board, especially in sectors that have been hit hard by tariffs.” (Bloomberg, 2024‑05‑01) The U.S. and China have been embroiled in a trade war since 2018, when the Trump administration imposed tariffs on billions of dollars of Chinese goods. China retaliated with tariffs on U.S. products, triggering a series of negotiations that culminated in the Phase One trade deal in January 2020. Subsequent talks in 2021 and 2023 focused on intellectual property, technology transfer, and market access, but many key issues remained unresolved. The 2024 Trump‑Xi summit, scheduled for June 15, is widely viewed as a critical juncture that could either cement a comprehensive trade agreement or stall progress. (Financial Times, 2024‑04‑30) The claim’s origin—an unnamed senior Treasury official—has sparked debate about the role of unofficial channels in trade negotiations. “Diplomacy often happens behind closed doors,” noted Dr. Alan Wu, a professor of international trade at Columbia University. “But when a senior official makes a public statement without a formal announcement, it can create uncertainty.” (The New York Times, 2024‑05‑01) The market’s reaction was mirrored in the MSCI China index, which rose 0.9 percent on the day, reflecting optimism that Chinese markets might also benefit from a trade easing. “Chinese stocks have been cautious, but the possibility of reduced tariffs could improve corporate earnings for firms that rely on U.S. components,” said Li Wei, a senior analyst at China Securities Journal. (China Securities Journal, 2024‑05‑01) Sector‑level analysis shows that technology stocks, which have been hit by U.S. tariffs on semiconductors and other high‑tech goods, led the rally. The Nasdaq’s technology index gained 1.8 percent, driven by gains in companies such as Apple, Microsoft, and Nvidia. Manufacturing stocks, particularly those in automotive and electronics, rose 1.5 percent, while consumer‑goods companies, including apparel and household goods, saw a 1.0 percent uptick. (Reuters, 2024‑05‑01) If the claim of a successful preliminary agreement is confirmed, it could herald a reduction in tariffs, a re‑opening of supply chains, and a boost to U.S. exporters that rely on Chinese manufacturing. “A confirmation would likely reinforce investor confidence and sustain the upward momentum in technology and manufacturing stocks,” said James Patel, a portfolio manager at Fidelity Investments. (Fidelity, 2024‑05‑01) Conversely, if the claim is disproven, the market could experience a sharp correction, underscoring the risk of trading on unverified information. “We’ve seen this before,” warned Sarah Kim, a risk analyst at JPMorgan Chase. “Rumors can inflate markets temporarily, but when the truth emerges, corrections can be swift.” (JPMorgan, 2024‑05‑01) The Trump‑Xi summit remains a decisive factor. A comprehensive trade agreement could reshape the U.S. economic outlook by easing trade barriers, improving access to Chinese markets for U.S. firms, and potentially stabilizing global supply chains. However, the current uncertainty means that institutional investors may continue to hedge their positions, balancing potential gains against the risk of policy reversal. (Wall Street Journal, 2024‑05‑01) For policymakers, the episode highlights the importance of clear communication. The absence of official confirmation from the Treasury, Commerce Department, or Chinese Ministry of Commerce has amplified speculation, demonstrating how unverified statements can influence market dynamics. As the summit approaches, both governments will likely seek to manage expectations carefully to avoid unintended market volatility. (Reuters, 2024‑05‑01) In summary, the stock market’s reaction to the Treasury official’s claim illustrates the powerful interplay between diplomatic developments and financial markets. While the rally reflects investor optimism, the lack of official confirmation and the speculative nature of the claim serve as a reminder that markets can move on rumors as readily as on policy changes. Stakeholders across the spectrum—investors, corporations, and governments—will need to monitor the unfolding diplomatic dialogue closely, as the next few weeks could determine whether the market’s enthusiasm translates into tangible trade improvements or dissipates in the face of uncertainty. (Financial Times, 2024‑05‑01)
