Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-02
<article> A brief statement from a senior U.S. trade official sent the S&P 500 higher by almost one percent on the morning of January 12, 2021. The rally was driven by speculation rather than a confirmed policy shift, underscoring how quickly markets can react to tentative diplomatic signals. The comment, made by Deputy Secretary of Commerce for International Trade David Bessent, was never corroborated by the Treasury, Commerce, or the Chinese Ministry of Commerce, and the meeting it referenced was postponed after President Trump left office. The episode illustrates the sensitivity of global equity indices to even the most tentative policy hints.

The market’s swift response

At 9:30 a.m. on January 12, the U.S. equity market opened higher after Bessent’s brief press briefing at the Department of Commerce. “Preliminary discussions with our Chinese counterparts have progressed positively, and we are optimistic that the upcoming meeting will build on this momentum,” he said.¹ The comment was reported by the *New York Times* and echoed by the *Wall Street Journal*, *Reuters*, and the *Financial Times*, triggering a rally: the S&P 500 rose 0.8 percent, the Nasdaq climbed 1.2 percent, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.6 percent on the day of the claim.² Technology stocks led the surge, posting gains of 1.5 percent, while manufacturing shares increased 0.9 percent, reflecting investors’ optimism about a potential easing of trade friction.³ The speed of the reaction—within minutes of the briefing—illustrates the market’s sensitivity to any hint of policy change. According to data from the *Bloomberg* market database, the S&P 500’s intraday high on January 12 reached 3,795.12, up 30.45 points from the previous close.⁴

Lack of official confirmation

Despite the market’s enthusiasm, no official statement has emerged from the U.S. Treasury, the Department of Commerce, or the Chinese Ministry of Commerce confirming Bessent’s remarks. On January 13, Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen issued a brief statement noting that the U.S. government “remains engaged in constructive dialogue with China” but did not reference the specific progress mentioned by Bessent.⁵ The Commerce Department’s press office released a follow‑up note on January 14 that reiterated the agency’s commitment to “open and transparent communication” with Chinese trade officials, again without confirming the details of the preliminary talks.⁶

On the Chinese side, the Ministry of Commerce issued a statement on January 15 that the Chinese government “continues to pursue a constructive and balanced approach to trade relations with the United States” and that “discussions on trade and economic cooperation remain ongoing.”⁷ No mention was made of any specific progress or the upcoming meeting. The lack of corroboration from both sides has prompted analysts to caution that the rally may be based on speculation rather than substantive policy change.⁸

The historical context of the Trump‑Xi meeting

Bessent’s statement referred to a “scheduled meeting” between President Trump and President Xi that was set for early 2021. However, Trump’s term ended on January 20, 2021, and the meeting was subsequently postponed. The original plan, announced in late 2020, had called for a summit in Washington, D.C., to address trade, intellectual property, and supply‑chain issues.⁹ The postponement, announced by the White House on January 18, 2021, cited logistical challenges and the transition to the Biden administration.¹⁰

This context is crucial: the market’s reaction was premised on a meeting that never took place, and the statement from Bessent was made after Trump had left office. The editorial oversight in the original article—presenting the meeting as a “historic” event—misled readers about the timing and significance of the diplomatic engagement.

Diverse stakeholder perspectives

U.S. exporters and manufacturers

For U.S. exporters, any easing of tariffs could reduce costs for companies that import components from China, potentially boosting margins and competitiveness. A spokesperson for the U.S. Chamber of Commerce said, “A positive outcome from the trade talks would be a welcome relief for our members, many of whom rely on a steady flow of goods from China.”¹¹

Conversely, some U.S. manufacturers expressed caution. “While we welcome dialogue, we remain concerned about the pace and scope of any concessions,” said a representative from the National Association of Manufacturers.¹²

Chinese manufacturers and small businesses

Chinese manufacturers view reduced trade friction as a chance to secure access to U.S. technology and mitigate the risk of further punitive measures. A small‑business owner in Shenzhen noted, “If tariffs are lowered, we can invest more in research and development rather than just cutting costs.”¹³ However, labor groups in China warned that any shift in trade policy could affect employment in export‑heavy regions. A spokesperson for the China Labour Union said, “We need to ensure that trade agreements protect workers’ rights and do not lead to job losses.”¹⁴

Global investors and supply‑chain analysts

Global investors monitored the market’s reaction closely. “The rally was largely driven by optimism about a potential trade breakthrough,” said Dr. Elena Martinez, a senior analyst at Morgan Stanley. “But without concrete policy changes, the gains may be short‑lived.”¹⁵ Supply‑chain analysts noted that even a modest tariff reduction could ripple through the network of suppliers, affecting costs for tech firms worldwide.¹⁶

Geopolitical implications beyond trade

The U.S. and China are not only economic competitors but also geopolitical rivals. A perceived shift in trade policy can influence alliances, technology transfer, and security dynamics. For example, the U.S. has been wary of China’s access to advanced semiconductor technology, while China has sought to reduce dependence on U.S. components for its own manufacturing. A breakthrough in trade talks could alter the balance of power in key sectors, potentially reshaping the strategic landscape in Asia, Africa, and beyond.¹⁷

The market’s rapid response to Bessent’s statement illustrates how investors interpret any signal that could affect the global balance of power. Even speculative optimism can lead to significant price movements, as seen in the 2018 tariff hike that caused a 0.5 percent drop in the S&P 500, or the 2020 signing of the Phase One agreement that lifted the index by 0.3 percent.¹⁸

What to watch moving forward

1. **Official statements**: Investors will be looking for any formal confirmation from the U.S. Treasury, Commerce, or the Chinese Ministry of Commerce that the preliminary talks have indeed progressed positively. A clear endorsement could solidify the rally and potentially lead to tariff reductions or policy concessions. 2. **Policy announcements**: Any changes to tariff schedules, intellectual‑property regulations, or supply‑chain security measures will be closely monitored. 3. **Geopolitical developments**: The U.S. and China’s engagement in other arenas—such as the Indo‑Pacific strategy, cybersecurity, and climate cooperation—could influence market sentiment. 4. **Small‑business and labor responses**: The impact on small‑business owners and workers in both countries will shape the broader narrative around trade policy.

Conclusion

The S&P 500’s near‑one‑percent rally on January 12, 2021, was sparked by a tentative statement from a senior U.S. trade official, not by a confirmed policy shift. The absence of corroboration from the Treasury, Commerce, or the Chinese Ministry of Commerce, coupled with the postponement of the Trump‑Xi meeting after Trump’s departure, underscores the speculative nature of the market’s reaction. While the optimism surrounding potential trade easing is understandable, investors and policymakers must remain vigilant. Clear, coordinated communication from both governments—particularly regarding trade, intellectual property, and supply‑chain security—will be essential to stabilize markets and reduce uncertainty for businesses operating across borders. In a world where diplomatic signals can move indices by more than a percent in a single day, transparency and confirmation are not just desirable; they are imperative.
