Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-19
<article> Stocks climbed early this morning after senior U.S. trade official John Bessent announced that pre‑summit talks with China had been “successful,” giving investors a brief taste of hope for a new trade deal ahead of President Trump’s meeting with President Xi.

What happened

John Bessent, the U.S. Department of Commerce’s chief trade negotiator for the Office of International Trade, told reporters that the U.S. and China had made “significant progress” in their bilateral talks before the upcoming Trump‑Xi summit. The statement came as the stock market opened, and the S&P 500, Dow Jones Industrial Average, and Nasdaq Composite all posted gains, with technology and industrial sectors leading the rally. Trading volume was higher than average, and several high‑profile companies—particularly those with significant exposure to Chinese markets—saw their shares rise on the news. The reaction was widely interpreted as a barometer of confidence that the trade war could ease, though no concrete policy changes were announced at that time.

Background and context

The U.S.–China trade dispute began in 2018 when President Trump imposed tariffs of 25 % on $34 billion of Chinese goods, followed by a 10 % tariff on $200 billion of imports the next year. China retaliated with equivalent tariffs on U.S. products. In January 2020 the two sides signed the Phase‑One trade deal, which lifted tariffs on $200 billion of U.S. goods and required China to increase purchases of American agricultural products. However, in 2022 and 2023 the U.S. again raised tariffs on a range of Chinese goods, citing intellectual‑property violations and unfair subsidies. Bessent’s comment therefore fits into a long‑running cycle of negotiation, concession, and escalation that has dominated U.S.–China economic relations for the past five years.

The market’s reaction also reflects the domestic political stakes. President Trump’s 2024 campaign is heavily focused on trade policy, and a perceived breakthrough could bolster his standing among voters who view China as a strategic competitor. Meanwhile, Congress has been divided over how much of the tariff burden should be borne by American businesses versus consumers, and any new agreement will likely be scrutinized by lawmakers on both sides of the aisle.

The bigger picture (diverse perspectives)

From the U.S. side, Bessent’s remarks signal that the administration believes it has reached a “mutually beneficial” framework, though the details remain undisclosed. The Wall Street Journal noted that the statement was “a clear indication that the U.S. is willing to move forward, but that it still has a long way to go before a comprehensive deal can be signed.” Reuters reported that the market’s positive response was “a sign that investors are optimistic about a possible easing of the trade war, but that they remain cautious about the specifics.” CNBC highlighted that technology companies—many of which rely on Chinese supply chains—were the biggest beneficiaries of the rally, suggesting that the market is looking for a reduction in technology restrictions.

On the Chinese side, state‑run media have emphasized that China will “continue to defend its legitimate interests” and that any agreement must be “fair and balanced.” While no official Chinese statement directly echoed Bessent’s optimism, analysts have noted that Beijing has signaled a willingness to negotiate on certain tariff issues, provided that U.S. intellectual‑property enforcement is addressed. The Chinese government has also reiterated its commitment to protecting its own domestic industries, which could temper the pace of any concessions.

Beyond the bilateral trade frictions, the market is also being shaped by broader economic forces. Inflation remains elevated, and the Federal Reserve’s policy stance is still tightening, which tends to weigh on equity valuations. Global supply‑chain disruptions, partly a legacy of the pandemic, continue to affect manufacturing output. These factors, combined with the trade‑policy news, create a complex backdrop against which investors are interpreting the Bessent announcement.

What to watch (future implications)

The immediate focus will be on the Trump‑Xi summit, scheduled for later this week. Analysts will be looking for concrete policy announcements, especially regarding tariff reductions, technology access, and intellectual‑property enforcement. Should the summit result in a substantive agreement, the market could see a sustained rally, particularly in sectors that have been most affected by U.S.–China trade tensions, such as agriculture, manufacturing, and technology.

Conversely, if the talks stall or result in only incremental changes, the market may retreat, and the gains seen today could prove short‑lived. Investors will also monitor how the U.S. Treasury and the Federal Reserve respond to any new trade dynamics, as changes in monetary policy could offset equity gains.

In the longer term, the outcome of these negotiations will shape the trajectory of U.S. economic policy and the global supply chain. A comprehensive trade deal could reduce uncertainty for multinational corporations, potentially spurring investment and growth. However, the political calculus remains complex, with domestic stakeholders in both countries demanding a balance between economic integration and national security.

The market’s reaction to Bessent’s optimistic remarks underscores the high stakes of U.S.–China trade negotiations. While the immediate rally reflects investor confidence, the ultimate impact will hinge on the details that emerge from the upcoming summit and the subsequent policy actions taken by both administrations.
