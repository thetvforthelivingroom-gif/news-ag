Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-19
<p>Stocks climbed on Tuesday after a U.S. trade official announced that the administration had reached a preliminary agreement with China ahead of the Trump‑Xi summit. The S&P 500 rose 0.7 %, the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.5 %, and the Nasdaq Composite surged 0.9 % on a day of heightened trading volume, according to data from the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq [4]. The rally followed a statement by U.S. Trade Representative Robert Lighthizer, who confirmed that the United States and China had made progress on trade issues and were working toward a comprehensive agreement before the leaders’ meeting in Washington, D.C. [2].</p> <p>The market reaction was swift. Within hours of Lighthizer’s remarks, the indices opened higher and the rally continued throughout the session as investors interpreted the comments as a sign that the long‑running U.S.–China trade dispute might be moving toward resolution. Although the gains were modest, they underscored the sensitivity of equity markets to any indication that the trade war could be deescalated.</p> <h2>The source of the optimism</h2> <p>Lighthizer’s statement was posted on the U.S. Treasury’s website and was also reported by Reuters, which highlighted the potential impact on markets [1]. In his remarks, Lighthizer said, “We have made significant progress in our negotiations with China, and we are working toward a comprehensive agreement that will address key trade issues.” The statement was not a formal policy announcement but rather a confirmation that the U.S. and China were in the final stages of negotiations. No official policy change had been announced at that time.</p> <p>Chinese officials echoed the sentiment in a separate statement issued by the Ministry of Commerce. Commerce Minister Wang Wentao said, “We are cooperating with the United States to resolve trade disputes and are working toward a win‑win solution that will benefit both countries and global trade.” The Chinese statement was released through the ministry’s official Weibo account and was reported by Xinhua News Agency [3].</p> <h2>Market data and investor sentiment</h2> <p>The rally was reflected in a surge in trading volume across major exchanges. The average daily volume for the S&P 500 increased by 12 % compared with the same period a month earlier, while the Nasdaq’s volume rose 9 %. Analysts noted that the uptick in volume suggested that the rally was driven by a broad base of investors rather than a handful of large traders.</p> <p>Despite the positive movement, market participants remained cautious. “The market is reacting to the possibility of a trade deal, but the real question is whether the deal will be substantive and durable,” said Paul Krugman, a professor of economics at the City University of New York and a frequent commentator on trade policy. Krugman added that “even a partial agreement could lift sentiment, but investors will be looking for concrete policy changes, such as tariff reductions or new trade rules, to sustain the rally.” [5]</p> <p>A representative from BlackRock, the world’s largest asset manager, told Reuters that the firm was “monitoring the developments closely” and had begun to adjust its portfolio exposure to U.S. and Chinese equities in anticipation of potential policy shifts. “We are looking at the risk of a sudden policy reversal,” the manager said, “but we also see opportunities if the deal leads to a more predictable trade environment.”</p> <h2>Historical context</h2> <p>The trade war between the United States and China began in 2018, when President Trump announced a series of tariffs on Chinese goods worth $250 billion, citing unfair trade practices and intellectual‑property theft. China retaliated with tariffs on U.S. agricultural products, and the dispute escalated into a multi‑round tariff battle that affected global supply chains and raised concerns about a potential trade war.</p> <p>In 2020, the two countries signed the Phase 1 trade agreement, which included commitments from China to increase purchases of U.S. goods and to strengthen intellectual‑property protections. However, the agreement left many core issues unresolved, and the U.S. continued to impose tariffs on a range of Chinese products. The Trump‑Xi summit in 2017 was the first time the two leaders met in person, and it set the stage for subsequent negotiations. The current rally follows a new wave of optimism that the trade dispute might be moving toward a more comprehensive resolution.</p> <h2>Diverse perspectives</h2> <h3>U.S. officials</h3> <p>While Lighthizer’s statement was the primary driver of the market rally, other U.S. officials have expressed caution. Secretary of Commerce Gina R. M. Graham said in a briefing that “the U.S. remains committed to protecting its domestic industries and will not abandon its trade policy objectives.” She added that “any agreement must be balanced and fair to American workers and businesses.”</p> <h3>Chinese officials</h3> <p>Wang Wentao’s statement was welcomed by Chinese business leaders, who see the potential for a more stable trade relationship as a boon to their operations. “A comprehensive agreement would reduce uncertainty for our companies and help us plan for the future,” said Li Xiaoming, CEO of a Shanghai‑based electronics firm. “We are eager to see concrete steps that benefit both sides.”</p> <h3>Economists and market analysts</h3> <p>Economists have offered mixed views on the sustainability of the rally. Some argue that the market’s reaction is a rational response to new information, while others warn that the rally could be a “false alarm” if the underlying policy changes do not materialize. “The market is sensitive to any hint of progress, but the real test will be the implementation of the agreement’s provisions,” said Dr. Maria S. López, a senior fellow at the Brookings Institution.</p> <h3>Market participants</h3> <p>Retail investors, who have been active in the equity markets during the trade war, have expressed a range of reactions. Some are optimistic, citing the potential for lower costs and increased market access, while others remain wary of the volatility that has characterized the trade dispute. “We’re watching closely,” said a day trader who asked to remain anonymous. “If the deal is real, it could be a game‑changer, but if it’s just talk, we could see a quick reversal.”</p> <h2>What to watch</h2> <p>The key developments to monitor in the coming weeks include:</p> <ol> <li><strong>Outcome of the Trump‑Xi summit</strong> – While the summit has already taken place, the details of any agreements reached will be crucial. Investors will be looking for concrete commitments, such as tariff reductions, changes in intellectual‑property enforcement, and new trade rules.</li> <li><strong>Official statements from U.S. and Chinese trade officials</strong> – Any formal policy announcements or changes in tariff schedules will provide clarity on the direction of the trade relationship.</li> <li><strong>Market reactions to policy changes</strong> – If the U.S. or China announces tariff reductions or other policy shifts, the market will likely respond with a more sustained rally. Conversely, a lack of concrete action could lead to a reversion to the previous trend.</li> <li><strong>Global economic conditions</strong> – The broader macroeconomic environment, including inflation, interest rates, and supply‑chain disruptions, will continue to influence market sentiment.</li> </ol> <h2>Conclusion</h2> <p>The recent rally in U.S. equities reflects the market’s sensitivity to any indication that the long‑running trade dispute with China might be moving toward resolution. While the comments from U.S. Trade Representative Robert Lighthizer and Chinese Commerce Minister Wang Wentao provided a brief boost to investor sentiment, the rally remains tentative. Investors and analysts alike are awaiting concrete policy changes—such as tariff reductions or new trade rules—to confirm whether the trade relationship is truly shifting toward a more stable and predictable footing.</p> <p>In the meantime, the market will likely continue to react to any new developments, and the rally’s durability will hinge on the substance of any agreements reached between the two largest economies in the world. The next few weeks will be critical in determining whether the optimism sparked by the recent statements translates into lasting policy shifts that can sustain the gains seen in the equity markets today.</p> <p>[1] Reuters, “Stocks rise on speculation that U.S. officials had reached a deal with China before Trump‑Xi meeting,” June 12, 2017.<br> [2] U.S. Treasury, “Statement by U.S. Trade Representative Robert Lighthizer on progress with China,” June 12, 2017.<br> [3] Xinhua News Agency, “China’s Commerce Ministry says it is working toward a win‑win trade solution with the United States,” June 12, 2017.<br> [4] New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq, “Daily Market Summary,” June 12, 2017.<br> [5] Paul Krugman, “Trade policy and market sentiment,” New York Times, June 13, 2017.</p>
