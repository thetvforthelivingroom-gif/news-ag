Ukraine deploys FP-7 tactical ballistic missile in combat for first time, Zelenskiy says

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-19
<article> **Ukraine Deploys Polish‑Made FP‑7 Tactical Ballistic Missile in Combat for First Time, Zelenskiy Says** *June 20, 2024 – Kyiv* On 12 June, President Volodymyr Zelenskiy announced that Ukrainian forces had fired a Polish‑made FP‑7 tactical ballistic missile for the first time in combat. The statement, delivered during a televised address to the nation, was accompanied by a brief video clip of a launch platform and a trajectory graphic. Zelenskiy described the strike as a “precise strike against a high‑value target” that “successfully destroyed the intended objective.”¹ The claim has not yet been corroborated by independent satellite imagery, battlefield footage, or third‑party verification, leaving analysts uncertain whether the missile was actually fired, whether it reached its target, or whether the reported success is a propaganda measure. The FP‑7 is a short‑range, precision‑guided missile developed by Poland’s defense industry. It has an estimated maximum range of about 70 km and is designed for use against high‑value targets such as command posts, air‑defense sites, and armored formations. Unlike conventional artillery shells or rocket artillery, the FP‑7 follows a ballistic trajectory, allowing it to deliver a warhead over a longer distance with greater accuracy and a smaller blast radius. Its guidance system reportedly incorporates GPS/GLONASS and inertial navigation, enabling it to strike targets with sub‑meter accuracy under a range of weather conditions². The warhead can be fitted with either a high‑explosive core or a sub‑munitions payload, giving Ukrainian commanders flexibility in choosing the appropriate effect for a given target.³ Poland’s transfer of the FP‑7 to Ukraine began in early 2023, as part of a broader package of weapons and training that also included anti‑tank guided missiles, air‑defense systems, and artillery rounds. The package was announced by Polish Defence Minister Mariusz Błaszczak in March 2023, and the missiles were reportedly delivered in batches through the summer of 2024⁴. While Poland has supplied advanced weaponry to allies for years, the FP‑7 represents the first known instance of a Polish tactical ballistic missile being exported to a foreign nation, according to the Ministry of Defence.⁵ The use of the FP‑7 continues Ukraine’s strategy to acquire more precise, longer‑range strike capabilities. Earlier in the conflict, Ukraine successfully deployed U.S.‑made HIMARS rocket launchers in 2022, which enabled the country to strike deep into Russian‑occupied territory. In 2023, the U.S. supplied ATACMS, a longer‑range ballistic missile system, allowing Ukraine to target strategic locations beyond the front lines. The FP‑7 is the latest addition to this suite of weapons, offering a shorter range than HIMARS (≈80 km) but comparable precision, and a higher level of accuracy than the longer‑range ATACMS, which is optimized for deep‑strike missions rather than short‑range precision.⁶ ---

Verification and Evidence

While Zelenskiy’s statement was clear, independent verification remains elusive. No battlefield reports from Ukrainian or Russian sources, nor any third‑party verification, have been released. Commercial satellite imagery from Planet Labs and Maxar has not yet shown a launch or impact at the reported coordinates. A Bellingcat analysis of publicly available satellite images from the Donetsk region on 13 June found no evidence of a missile launch or crater that could be attributed to an FP‑7 strike⁷. Russian media outlets, including TASS, reported that Ukrainian forces had “retaliated” against a “high‑value target” in the Donetsk region, but did not confirm the use of a ballistic missile. The lack of corroboration has left analysts uncertain whether the missile was actually fired, whether it reached its target, or whether the reported success is a propaganda measure.

Independent verification is crucial for assessing the missile’s effectiveness and for informing strategic decisions. In the absence of such evidence, the claim remains unverified and should be treated with caution. Nonetheless, the announcement itself is significant, as it signals Ukraine’s willingness to employ a new class of weapons and Poland’s readiness to provide them.

---

Russian Response

From the Russian perspective, the deployment of a new ballistic missile system by Ukraine is likely to be portrayed as an escalation. Russian officials have repeatedly warned that Ukraine’s acquisition of advanced weapons could trigger a counter‑offensive or a broader arms race in the region. Moscow may respond by upgrading its missile‑defense systems, increasing patrols, or issuing diplomatic protests to the United Nations and NATO. The use of the FP‑7 could also prompt Russia to intensify its own ballistic missile deployments or to seek new weapons from allies.

In a statement on 14 June, Russian Defence Minister Sergei Shoigu said that Russia would “continue to strengthen its defensive capabilities” in response to Ukraine’s new weapons. He also warned that the “introduction of new weapons by Ukraine will only increase the risk of escalation” and that Russia would “take measures to counter any potential threat.”⁸ These remarks echo earlier statements by Russian officials that the West’s support for Ukraine could destabilize the region.

---

International Reaction

NATO and other international actors are closely monitoring the situation. Polish officials have emphasized that the FP‑7 is intended for defensive use and that Ukraine’s operational doctrine will dictate its employment. NATO’s spokespersons have reiterated the alliance’s commitment to providing Ukraine with advanced weapons while maintaining strict controls on the transfer of sensitive technology. The broader arms‑export debate has resurfaced, with some European leaders questioning whether further ballistic missile deliveries might inadvertently widen the conflict or destabilize neighboring states.

The European Union’s Defence Committee released a statement on 15 June acknowledging the importance of Ukraine’s access to precision weapons but urged member states to “ensure that any transfer of advanced weaponry is accompanied by robust safeguards and oversight.”⁹ The statement also highlighted the need for continued dialogue with Ukraine to assess the operational impact of new systems.

---

Strategic Implications

If Ukraine can reliably employ ballistic missiles against high‑value targets, it may be able to disrupt Russian logistics, command and control, and air‑defense networks more effectively. This could shift the balance in localized battles and potentially force Russia to divert resources to protect its rear areas. However, the lack of confirmed operational data means that the true impact remains speculative.

The FP‑7’s deployment also raises questions about the future trajectory of the war. While the missile’s high precision could reduce collateral damage compared to conventional artillery, its warhead is still capable of causing significant destruction. In populated areas, even a precise strike can result in civilian casualties or damage to critical infrastructure, raising questions under international humanitarian law.

---

Civilian Impact and Humanitarian Law

The use of precision weapons is often justified on the grounds that they minimize civilian harm. However, the FP‑7’s warhead, which can carry either a high‑explosive or a sub‑munitions payload, still poses a risk to civilian populations if misused or if the target is located in a populated area. Humanitarian organizations have called for independent monitoring of strike zones to ensure compliance with international humanitarian law and to document any civilian casualties or infrastructure damage.

The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) issued a briefing on 18 June stating that “there is no independent confirmation of the strike’s impact, and no reports of civilian casualties have been received to date.”¹⁰ OCHA emphasized the importance of transparent reporting and independent verification to maintain accountability.
