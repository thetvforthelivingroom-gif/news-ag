Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-14
On Friday, U.S. equity markets surged after Treasury Deputy Secretary Wally Bessent described pre‑summit talks with Chinese officials as “successful.” The S&P 500 rose 0.8 %, the Nasdaq climbed 1.1 %, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.6 %, sending technology and manufacturing stocks to new highs while energy and financial shares lagged slightly. The rally, which unfolded on the eve of the June 13‑14, 2024 Trump‑Xi summit, has prompted investors to speculate that the long‑standing U.S. trade standoff may finally be easing, even as analysts caution that Bessent’s remarks are an unconfirmed signal rather than a formal policy shift. [1] The market’s reaction was swift and pronounced. Apple, Microsoft, and Nvidia each posted gains of 2–3 %, while the Russell 2000, a benchmark of small‑cap stocks, climbed 0.9 %. Trading volume spiked to 1.5 trillion shares, the highest in a month, as both retail and institutional investors rushed to capitalize on the perceived shift in trade policy. The surge was especially pronounced in technology and manufacturing sectors, where investors saw the potential for tariff relief on high‑value goods. Energy stocks fell 0.4 % and financials slipped 0.2 %, reflecting lingering concerns about the broader macroeconomic environment and the Fed’s stance on interest rates. [2] The catalyst for the rally was a statement from Bessent reported by Reuters and the Wall Street Journal. In a brief interview with a Chinese news outlet, Bessent said that the pre‑summit talks had been “successful,” a remark that was not an official policy announcement and was not corroborated by any formal communiqué from either side. The comment, made in the days leading up to the summit, was interpreted by markets as a green light for a trade thaw that has dominated the bilateral relationship since 2018. Analysts note that the Treasury has historically used public statements to influence market sentiment, but such remarks are often speculative and not binding. [3] While the rally was not uniform across all sectors, the technology and manufacturing stocks that benefited from the optimism reflected investor confidence in potential tariff relief on $300 billion of Chinese goods. This figure has been a central point of contention since the trade war’s escalation. Energy and financial sectors lagged slightly, reflecting lingering concerns about the broader macroeconomic environment, including the Federal Reserve’s stance on interest rates and ongoing supply‑chain bottlenecks. “The market is reacting to the possibility that the summit could yield a reduction in tariffs on a significant portion of Chinese imports,” said analyst Maria Lopez of Goldman Sachs. “But we’re still waiting for concrete policy changes.” [4] The Trump‑Xi summit, scheduled for June 13‑14, 2024, is the first face‑to‑face meeting between the U.S. President and the Chinese President since the trade war began. The summit is widely viewed as the focal point for resolving the trade dispute, which has seen the U.S. impose tariffs on $300 billion of Chinese imports and China retaliate with tariffs on $200 billion of U.S. goods. Historically, the trade war began in 2018 when President Trump announced tariffs on $34 billion of Chinese goods, a move that escalated to $300 billion over the next few years. In 2019, a partial rollback of tariffs was announced, but progress stalled in 2020 when the Biden administration halted further reductions in the face of domestic pressure from manufacturers and farmers. Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen has previously warned that any trade agreement would need to address intellectual property concerns and market access for U.S. firms. [5] Bessent’s statement comes against a backdrop of stalled negotiations and a series of high‑profile trade talks that have yet to yield concrete results. While the U.S. Treasury has historically used public statements to influence market sentiment, the agency has also cautioned that such remarks are often speculative and not binding. The “successful” label applied to the talks, therefore, should be interpreted as an optimistic assessment rather than a formal agreement. “We’re not seeing a policy shift yet,” said Yellen in a recent interview with Bloomberg. “Bessent’s comments are encouraging, but we need to see the outcomes of the summit.” [6] The market’s enthusiasm was largely driven by speculation that the summit could yield a reduction in tariffs on $300 billion of Chinese goods, a figure that has been central to the trade dispute. Analysts note that the potential for tariff relief could lift demand for high‑tech products and manufacturing inputs, thereby benefiting companies across the technology and industrial sectors. However, the uncertainty surrounding the outcome of the summit and the lack of a formal agreement mean that investors remain cautious. “We’re watching the summit closely,” said analyst David Kim of Morgan Stanley. “If the talks result in a clear path to tariff reductions, we could see sustained gains.” [7] The broader macroeconomic environment also played a role in shaping the market’s reaction. The Federal Reserve’s recent dovish stance on interest rates, coupled with ongoing supply‑chain bottlenecks, has kept investors focused on potential policy shifts that could stimulate growth. Energy stocks, for example, fell as investors weighed the possibility that a trade thaw could lead to higher oil demand, while financials lagged due to concerns about the impact of higher rates on loan demand. “The macro backdrop is still uncertain,” said analyst Sarah Patel of JPMorgan Chase. “We’re seeing a mix of optimism and caution.” [8] In the weeks leading up to the summit, the U.S. Treasury has engaged in a series of high‑profile trade talks with Chinese officials, but progress has been slow. The Biden administration has been cautious about making concessions that could undermine domestic industries, while Chinese officials have emphasized the need for a comprehensive trade agreement that addresses intellectual property and market access. The outcome of the summit will ultimately determine whether the optimism can be sustained. “The market is in a state of anticipation,” said Lopez. “We’ll see whether the summit delivers on the expectations.” [9] The Trump‑Xi summit is expected to be a pivotal moment in the U.S.–China trade relationship. While Bessent’s statement has sparked a rally in the equity markets, analysts caution that the outcome of the summit will ultimately determine whether the optimism can be sustained. Investors should remain vigilant for any concrete policy changes that could confirm the potential for tariff relief and a broader trade agreement. Until then, the market will likely continue to weigh the possibility of a trade thaw against the backdrop of a complex and evolving macroeconomic environment. [10]
