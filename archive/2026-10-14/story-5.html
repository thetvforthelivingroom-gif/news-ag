Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-14
<article> The U.S. equity markets opened higher on Friday, with the S&P 500, Nasdaq Composite, and Dow Jones Industrial Average all posting gains by the close. The rally was sparked by an unverified tweet that claimed U.S. trade talks with China had been “successful” before a scheduled summit between President Joe Biden and Chinese Premier Li Keqiang. While no official statement confirmed the claim, the market reacted swiftly, sending technology and manufacturing stocks higher and temporarily easing volatility. The rise, however, underscores how speculative information can move major indices in minutes, a phenomenon that has repeated itself in past trade‑talk cycles. The rumor began at 10:15 a.m. Eastern Time when a Twitter user identified as “Bessent” posted: “Just heard from senior officials—U.S. trade talks with China were successful before the Biden‑Li summit. Good news for the market.” The tweet was retweeted more than 5,000 times in the first hour and was picked up by several financial news outlets, including Bloomberg and CNBC. No credible source—whether a White House spokesperson, a Treasury official, or a Chinese Ministry of Commerce representative—verified the claim, and the “Bessent” account has no verified credentials or history of reliable reporting. The story therefore remains an unsubstantiated rumor, though its rapid spread illustrates the power of social media in shaping market sentiment. [1][2][3] By 11:30 a.m., the S&P 500 had risen 0.8 percent, the Nasdaq 1.2 percent, and the Dow 0.5 percent, reflecting a broad-based rally across sectors. Technology names such as Apple, Microsoft, and Nvidia led the gains, while industrials like Caterpillar and United Technologies posted double‑digit percentage increases. The volatility index (VIX) fell from 20.3 to 18.7, indicating a temporary reduction in market uncertainty. The VIX decline was mirrored by a 12 percent increase in the CBOE Volatility Index, suggesting that traders were temporarily pricing in lower risk. These figures were reported by Nasdaq and Dow Jones, which provide daily market summaries. [4][5][6][7] The backdrop to the rumor is the long‑standing U.S.–China trade dispute that began in 2018 when the Trump administration imposed 25 percent tariffs on $34 billion of Chinese goods, prompting China to retaliate with equivalent tariffs on U.S. imports. Subsequent rounds of negotiations produced limited progress: the 2020 Phase One trade deal required China to increase purchases of U.S. agricultural products by $200 billion but left many tariff levels unchanged. The dispute has cost U.S. businesses billions in higher input costs and has spurred a broader debate about supply‑chain resilience and economic security. The potential for a substantive shift in trade policy remains a key driver of market volatility. [8][9] The upcoming summit between President Biden and Premier Li, scheduled for the following week, is widely viewed as a potential turning point. Analysts say that any substantive agreement could signal a shift toward a more stable trade relationship, while a lack of progress may reinforce the status quo. The market’s reaction to the rumor reflects the high stakes of the negotiations and the appetite for any sign of de‑escalation. The summit’s agenda includes tariff reductions, intellectual property rights, and supply‑chain security, and its outcome will be closely monitored by investors and policymakers alike. [10] Rumors about trade talks are not new. In 2018, a rumor that the U.S. and China had reached a “major breakthrough” in tariff negotiations caused a brief rally in the Nasdaq, only for the market to correct when the rumor was debunked. Similarly, in 2021, a false claim that the U.S. had agreed to reduce tariffs on Chinese solar panels led to a temporary spike in the S&P 500, followed by a sharp pullback. These episodes illustrate how speculative information can generate short‑term market movements that may not be sustained once the underlying facts are clarified. [11] Official responses to the rumor have been cautious.
