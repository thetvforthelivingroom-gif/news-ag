Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-21
<strong>Stocks Rise as Trade Representative Katherine Tai Signals Successful Talks with China Ahead of Trump‑Xi Summit</strong> Stocks rallied on Friday after U.S. Trade Representative Katherine Tai announced that the administration had reached a “successful” stage in trade negotiations with China, a development that comes just days before the Trump‑Xi summit scheduled for June 12, 2024. The S&P 500 closed 1.2 % higher at 4,260.5, the Nasdaq gained 1.8 % to 13,210.3, and the Russell 2000 advanced 1.5 %—all gains accompanied by a 30 % jump in average daily trading volume, the largest in more than a month. Technology shares lifted 2.7 %, while energy stocks slipped 1.2 % as oil prices fell on expectations that China’s demand for crude could soften. ---

Immediate Market Reaction

During a press briefing on Friday, Tai said the U.S. and China had made “significant progress” in talks that focused on tariff reductions, technology transfer, and intellectual‑property protections. “We are pleased with the progress made,” she added, noting that the discussions had moved beyond the limited scope of the 2020 Phase One agreement. The statement was issued without a formal agreement or detailed list of concessions, and the Treasury Department has yet to release an official statement confirming the specifics of any deal.

Within minutes of the announcement, the S&P 500 surged 51.8 points, the Nasdaq climbed 237.6 points, and the Russell 2000 rose 1.5 %. NYSE and Nasdaq trading‑volume data showed a 30 % increase in daily volume, indicating that both institutional and retail investors were eager to capitalize on the optimism surrounding the talks. The Technology Select Sector SPDR Fund (XLK) rose 2.7 %, while the Consumer Discretionary Index (XLY) gained 1.9 %. Energy stocks fell 1.2 % as Brent crude slipped 1.5 % to $84.30 a barrel, reflecting fears that a potential easing of Chinese tariffs could dampen demand for crude and refined products.

---

The U.S.–China Trade War in Retrospect

The trade war began in 2018 when the Trump administration imposed a 25 % tariff on $34 billion of Chinese goods, followed by a 37 % tariff on an additional $200 billion in 2019. China retaliated with a 25 % tariff on $34 billion of U.S. goods and a 15 % tariff on $200 billion of American products. The conflict disrupted global supply chains, particularly in technology and manufacturing, and prompted a wave of tariffs that reached 200 billion dollars in U.S. imports by 2020.

In 2019, the two sides signed a Phase One trade agreement that required China to increase purchases of U.S. agricultural products and strengthen intellectual‑property protections. However, the deal stalled in 2020 when the Trump administration withheld the necessary tariff reductions, citing concerns over China’s technology‑transfer practices and national security. The trade friction has since become a central theme in U.S. foreign policy, influencing commodity prices, corporate earnings forecasts, and the broader geopolitical landscape.

---

Chinese Perspective and Official Response

China’s Ministry of Commerce issued no comment on the U.S. statement, leaving Beijing’s position on tariff concessions and technology restrictions unclear. A spokesperson for the ministry said, “We are monitoring the U.S. side’s statements and will respond in due course.” Analysts note that Chinese officials have historically been cautious in publicizing progress in trade talks, preferring to keep negotiations confidential until a formal agreement is reached.

Tai’s remarks come at a time when Chinese officials are reportedly preparing for the upcoming summit, which will cover trade, technology, and national‑security issues. The Chinese side has been working to balance its domestic industrial policy with the need to maintain a stable trade relationship with the United States, especially as China seeks to reduce its dependence on U.S. technology.

---

Analyst Views: Optimism Meets Caution

“Tai’s language is intentionally vague,” said Dr. Elena Martinez, a trade‑policy analyst at the Brookings Institution. “There is no confirmation that tariffs will be lowered or that technology restrictions will be eased.” She added that the market’s optimism is premature until concrete tariff reductions are announced.

Conversely, portfolio manager Maria Lopez of Global Capital Partners noted that even a modest tariff reduction could boost demand for Chinese imports and lift earnings for U.S. companies that rely on Chinese supply chains. “The ripple effect could be significant,” Lopez said.

Policy analysts from the Council on Foreign Relations and the Center for Strategic and International Studies highlighted the strategic dimensions of the talks. “Beyond the economic implications, these negotiations touch on broader issues of technology sovereignty and geopolitical influence,” noted Dr. Wei Liu, a senior fellow at the CFR. “The outcome could signal a shift in the U.S. approach to technology transfer and intellectual‑property rights.”

---

Potential Policy Implications

If the talks culminate in tariff reductions, U.S. manufacturers could see lower input costs and improved competitiveness in global markets. A relaxation of technology‑transfer restrictions could also ease the flow of high‑tech components into the U.S. supply chain, potentially benefiting firms in the semiconductor and aerospace sectors.

On the other hand, if China maintains its current stance on technology restrictions, U.S. companies may continue to face barriers to accessing critical components and markets in China. The trade war’s legacy could persist, keeping volatility in global commodity markets and investment flows.

The Treasury Department’s forthcoming release on June 10, which will detail any tariff changes, will be closely watched. A formal announcement could further boost equity markets, while a failure to confirm the talks could trigger a reversal.

---

The Trump‑Xi Summit: A Litmus Test

The summit, set for June 12, 2024, will be the ultimate test of the “successful talks” claim. The agenda is expected to cover trade, technology, and national‑security issues. “The summit could either confirm the market’s optimism or expose the fragility of the current gains,” said Dr. Martinez. “We will see whether the U.S. can secure meaningful tariff reductions or whether China will maintain its current policy stance.”

In the short term, traders will be watching for any official release from the Treasury or the USTR that clarifies the scope of the agreement. A formal announcement could further boost equity markets, while a failure to confirm the talks could trigger a reversal.

Beyond the immediate market impact, the outcome of the talks could set a precedent for future trade negotiations. If the U.S. secures tariff reductions and better technology‑transfer terms, it could ease pressure on American manufacturers and open new avenues for U.S. firms in China’s market. Conversely, if the talks stall or result in only modest concessions, the trade war’s legacy could persist, keeping volatility in global supply chains and commodity prices.

---

Looking Ahead

As the Trump‑Xi summit approaches, the market remains on edge, balancing the promise of a potential easing of trade tensions against the reality that no formal agreement has yet been announced. The next few days will reveal whether the optimism sparked by the USTR’s brief statement translates into concrete policy changes or remains a fleeting market rally.
