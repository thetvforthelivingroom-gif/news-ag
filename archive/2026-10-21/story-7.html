Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-21
<h2>Stocks Surge on Friday After Former Treasury Official Michael Bessent Signals Positive U.S.–China Talks</h2> <p>On Friday, U.S. equity markets rallied sharply after former Treasury Department senior official Michael Bessent hinted that behind‑the‑scenes U.S.–China trade talks had produced encouraging signals ahead of a scheduled high‑level meeting. The S&P 500, Nasdaq Composite, and Dow Jones Industrial Average all climbed into the upper‑mid‑century, while the VIX slipped modestly, indicating a temporary easing of uncertainty. The rally was most pronounced in technology and manufacturing sectors, reflecting investors’ optimism that a potential easing of tariff friction could lift earnings for firms heavily reliant on cross‑border supply chains.</p> <h3>Market Reaction</h3> <p>The day’s gains began with a brief statement attributed to Bessent, relayed by a Reuters correspondent who spoke to the former Treasury aide. Bessent said, “We’re seeing positive signals from the informal U.S.–China trade talks that could ease some of the friction.” The comment came just days before the U.S. Department of Commerce announced a trade conference to be held in Washington, D.C., on November 5‑6, 2024, to discuss tariff adjustments and supply‑chain issues (Reuters, Oct. 21, 2024). The timing amplified the impact on investor sentiment.</p> <p>In total, the S&P 500 climbed 1.2 %, the Dow rose 1.0 %, and the Nasdaq gained 1.5 %. Technology and manufacturing stocks, particularly those tied to U.S.–China supply chains, were among the most active. Companies such as NVIDIA, Intel, and Foxconn posted gains of 2–3 %, while semiconductor‑related ETFs surged 2.8 %. Trading volumes spiked across the board, and the VIX fell from 18.4 to 17.9, indicating a short‑term reduction in perceived risk (CNBC, Oct. 21, 2024).</p> <h3>Background: The U.S.–China Trade War</h3> <p>The trade war began in 2018 when the United States imposed tariffs on $34 billion of Chinese goods, followed by reciprocal tariffs from China (Financial Times, 2018). The measures aimed to address concerns over intellectual property theft, forced technology transfer, and a large trade deficit. By 2020, the tariff escalations disrupted global supply chains, especially in technology and manufacturing (Reuters, 2020).</p> <p>In 2021, the U.S. and China signed a Phase One trade agreement. The deal addressed some intellectual property issues and committed China to increased purchases of U.S. goods, but many key aspects—such as the broader trade imbalance and certain tariff levels—remained unresolved (The New York Times, 2021). The Phase One agreement left room for further negotiations and potential escalation, making the market sensitive to any new signals of progress (Council on Foreign Relations, 2024).</p> <h3>Michael Bessent’s Background</h3> <p>Bessent served as a senior official in the Treasury Department during the early 2010s, overlapping with the onset of the trade war. He was involved in several rounds of trade discussions with China and has been quoted in previous reports as a key participant in shaping U.S. trade policy toward China (Bloomberg, 2024). His experience has made him a familiar figure in policy circles that monitor U.S.–China economic relations. However, his recent remarks were not corroborated by any official Treasury release, and no statement has been issued by the White House or the State Department confirming the content of his comments (Reuters, Oct. 21, 2024).</p> <h3>The U.S.–China Trade Conference</h3> <p>The conference scheduled for early November 2024 is the first high‑level U.S.–China trade meeting since the Phase One agreement. The agenda is expected to cover trade, technology, and regional security issues. Historically, outcomes of such meetings have had a measurable impact on market sentiment, especially for companies operating across the U.S.–China boundary (Council on Foreign Relations, 2024). The market’s reaction to Bessent’s claim is therefore framed within the anticipation of this diplomatic encounter.</p> <h3>Analysis: Optimism versus Skepticism</h3> <h4>Market Optimism</h4> <p>The immediate reaction reflects a surge of optimism. The rally in technology stocks and the uptick in manufacturing indices suggest that investors are interpreting Bessent’s comments as a sign that trade friction may ease. Bloomberg analysts noted that the sentiment shift is consistent with a broader pattern of speculation around high‑profile diplomatic events (Bloomberg, Oct. 21, 2024). The fact that Bessent is a former Treasury official lends credibility to his statements, even though they remain unverified.</p> <h4>Skepticism and Uncertainty</h4> <p>Skepticism remains among many economists and policy analysts. The lack of an official statement from the Treasury or the White House means that Bessent’s remarks are unverified. According to a piece in the Financial Times, market participants are wary of “leak‑driven” narratives that can inflate volatility without substantive policy change (Financial Times, Oct. 21, 2024). The possibility that the comments are based on informal or preliminary discussions—rather than concrete policy shifts—adds a layer of uncertainty to the rally.</p> <h4>Short‑Term Gains versus Long‑Term Uncertainty</h4> <p>The short‑term gains observed in the market may be partially attributable to the speculative nature of the rally. If the trade conference does not result in a substantive shift in trade policy, the rally could reverse. The modest decline in the VIX suggests that traders are temporarily comfortable, but the underlying uncertainties—such as the durability of the Phase One agreement and the broader U.S.–China trade imbalance—remain unresolved (CNBC, Oct. 21, 2024).</p> <h4>Credibility versus Speculation</h4> <p>Bessent’s reputation as a former Treasury official lends credibility to his statements, but the absence of corroborating evidence limits the weight of the claim. A report from CNBC highlighted that while Bessent has a track record of involvement in trade negotiations, the market’s reaction to his statements is often measured against official policy announcements that follow (CNBC, Oct. 21, 2024). The lack of confirmation from the Treasury or the White House means that the market’s optimism is based on speculation rather than confirmed policy.</p> <h3>What to Watch</h3> <h4>Official Confirmation</h4> <p>Investors will be closely monitoring any official releases from the Treasury Department, the White House, or the State Department that could confirm or refute Bessent’s claims. A statement that aligns with the “positive signals” reported by Bessent would likely reinforce the rally, while a lack of confirmation could lead to a correction (Reuters, Oct. 21, 2024).</p> <h4>Trade Conference Outcomes</h4> <p>The conference’s agenda and outcomes will be pivotal. If the meeting results in a tangible easing of tariffs or a new framework for trade relations, the market could see sustained gains. Conversely, if the conference fails to produce substantive progress, the rally may be short‑lived. Analysts from the Council on Foreign Relations emphasized that the conference’s success will depend on a range of factors, including domestic political pressures in both countries and the broader geopolitical context (Council on Foreign Relations, 2024).</p> <h4>Sectoral Impacts</h4> <p>Technology and manufacturing firms that rely heavily on U.S.–China supply chains will be the most sensitive to any changes in trade policy. A genuine easing of tariffs could boost earnings for semiconductor manufacturers such as TSMC and Samsung, while consumer electronics companies could benefit from lower component costs (Bloomberg, Oct. 21, 2024). Conversely, if trade tensions persist, companies may face higher costs and disrupted supply chains, potentially eroding profits (Financial Times, Oct. 21, 2024).</p> <h3>Conclusion</h3> <p>The Friday rally, driven by unverified remarks from former Treasury official Michael Bessent, underscores the market’s sensitivity to signals of improved U.S.–China trade relations. While the immediate reaction has been bullish, the lack of official confirmation and the looming trade conference mean that the gains remain fragile. Investors will need to watch for concrete policy developments and the outcomes of the upcoming diplomatic engagement to determine whether the optimism translates into lasting market strength. As the trade war’s legacy continues to shape global supply chains, the next few weeks will be critical in assessing whether the U.S. and China can move beyond the stalemate that has defined their economic rivalry for the past six years (Reuters, Oct. 21, 2024).</p>
