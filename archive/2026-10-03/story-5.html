Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-03
<article> <h1>Stocks Rally on U.S. Trade Representative’s Positive China Talks Ahead of Biden‑Xi Summit</h1> <p>On Tuesday, U.S. equities surged after the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) announced that preliminary trade talks with China had been “constructive and productive.” The statement, released at 8:30 a.m. EDT, was followed by gains of 0.9 % in the Dow Jones Industrial Average, 1.2 % in the S&P 500, and 1.5 % in the Nasdaq Composite, with technology, consumer staples, and industrials leading the rally. Investors interpreted the comment as a sign that the Biden administration was making headway on the long‑standing trade and technology impasse that has dominated U.S.–China relations for the past decade.</p> <h2>What Happened</h2> <p>In a brief statement released by the USTR’s office, Katherine Tai said, “We are pleased to report that the preliminary round of discussions with the People's Republic of China was constructive and productive.” The statement was accompanied by a press release that noted progress on key issues, including intellectual property protection, market access, and the removal of non‑tariff barriers. It also referenced a joint statement on the U.S. tariff on Chinese solar panels, which the USTR said could be reduced from the current 25 % to 15 % if China meets certain market‑access commitments. The release did not mention forced technology transfer, a point that has been a sticking point in previous negotiations.</p> <p>“We are optimistic that the momentum from these early talks will carry forward into the upcoming summit between President Biden and President Xi on April 20,” Tai added. “A comprehensive agreement would not only help close the U.S. trade deficit but also provide clarity for businesses on both sides of the Pacific.”<sup>[1]</sup></p> <p>Market participants reacted swiftly. The Dow rose 0.9 % to 33,210 points, the S&P 500 climbed 1.2 % to 4,280 points, and the Nasdaq gained 1.5 % to 13,750 points. The technology sector, in particular, saw a 2.1 % uptick, driven by gains in semiconductor and cloud‑computing stocks. Consumer staples also benefited, with a 1.4 % rise in the Dow Jones U.S. Select Sector Index for consumer staples, reflecting optimism about lower tariffs on agricultural imports.</p> <h2>Historical Context</h2> <p>The U.S.–China trade relationship has been fraught with conflict since the 2018 tariff war, when both sides imposed duties on hundreds of billions of dollars of goods. The Trump administration’s “phase one” trade deal, signed in January 2020, aimed to curb intellectual property theft and secure better market access for U.S. firms. However, the deal left many issues unresolved, and the Biden administration has continued to pursue a hard‑line stance on technology restrictions while seeking to reduce the trade deficit.</p> <p>Since taking office, President Biden has emphasized the need for a “fair and reciprocal” trade relationship. The USTR’s office has been negotiating with China on a range of issues, from tariffs on solar panels and electric vehicles to the enforcement of U.S. intellectual property laws. Tai’s announcement comes after a series of back‑to‑back meetings in Washington and Beijing that have focused on narrowing the remaining gaps in the trade framework.</p> <p>Historically, U.S. trade negotiations with China have involved multiple rounds of talks, often conducted in secret to avoid public backlash. The recent talks were notable for their transparency; Tai’s statement was released to the public and accompanied by a detailed press release, a move intended to reassure domestic stakeholders—including manufacturers, exporters, and consumers—about the direction of U.S. trade policy.</p> <h2>Chinese Perspective</h2> <p>China’s Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) issued a statement on the same day, saying, “We welcome the U.S. Trade Representative’s comments and look forward to continuing constructive dialogue to promote the healthy development of bilateral trade.” The MOFCOM added that China would “continue to adhere to the principles of fairness, reciprocity, and mutual benefit” and that it “will take concrete measures to improve the business environment for foreign enterprises.”<sup>[2]</sup></p> <p>Chinese Ambassador to the United States, Liu Xiaoming, told reporters that Beijing was “open to exploring ways to reduce trade friction” but cautioned that “any agreement must be based on a comprehensive understanding of each other’s interests and concerns.” He added that China would “maintain its commitment to the existing tariff schedule until the completion of the trade negotiations.”<sup>[3]</sup></p> <h2>Media Coverage and Attribution</h2> <p>Major news outlets covered the announcement with a mix of optimism and caution. The <em>New York Times</em> reported that the statement “could signal a shift toward a more collaborative U.S.–China relationship, potentially easing long‑standing trade frictions.”<sup>[4]</sup> <em>AP News</em> focused on the investor perspective, noting that the stock market’s positive response reflected growing confidence in the U.S. economy’s resilience amid geopolitical uncertainty.<sup>[5]</sup> The <em>Wall Street Journal</em> emphasized policy ramifications, arguing that the preliminary success could force the Biden administration to reconsider its hard‑line stance on technology restrictions and intellectual property, thereby affecting future regulatory frameworks.<sup>[6]</sup> Reuters provided a balanced view, reporting the market gains while noting that the final outcome of the Biden‑Xi summit would still be uncertain and that the preliminary talks were only one step in a complex negotiation process.<sup>[7]</sup></p> <h2>Industry Perspectives</h2> <p>Industry groups have weighed in on the potential impact of a new trade agreement. The American Chamber of Commerce in China (AmCham China) released a statement saying, “A comprehensive trade deal would provide much‑needed clarity for U.S. exporters and help reduce the uncertainty that has plagued businesses operating in China.”<sup>[8]</sup> The National Association of Manufacturers (NAM) echoed this sentiment, noting that a reduction in tariffs on industrial machinery could lower production costs for U.S. manufacturers.</p> <p>On the agricultural side, the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) highlighted that a potential agreement could open new markets for U.S. corn and soybeans. “We are optimistic that the U.S.–China trade talks will lead to increased demand for our agricultural products,” said USDA spokesperson Thomas Miller. “This would benefit farmers across the country.”<sup>[9]</sup></p> <p>Technology companies, however, remain cautious. A spokesperson for the Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA) said, “While progress on intellectual property protection is encouraging, we need concrete commitments on the enforcement of U.S. IP laws and the removal of forced technology transfer requirements.”<sup>[10]</sup></p> <h2>Economic Analysis</h2> <p>Economists point to the broader implications of a potential trade deal. Dr. Emily Chen, a professor of international economics at Stanford University, noted that “a comprehensive agreement could reduce the U.S. trade deficit by an estimated $30 billion annually, assuming a 10 % reduction in tariff rates on Chinese imports.”<sup>[11]</sup> She added that “the benefits would be uneven, with sectors that rely heavily on Chinese inputs—such as electronics and automotive—standing to gain the most.”</p> <p>Financial analysts also highlighted the potential impact on supply chains. “If China agrees to lower tariffs on critical components, it could spur a shift in global supply chains toward more integrated U.S.–China production networks,” said analyst Mark Rogers of Goldman Sachs. “This could reduce costs for U.S. firms and improve competitiveness.”<sup>[12]</sup></p> <h2>Political Context and the Biden‑Xi Summit</h2> <p>The announcement comes ahead of President Biden’s scheduled summit with President Xi on April 20, a meeting that has been described as “the most consequential U.S.–China meeting since the 1979 normalization of relations.” The summit is expected to address a range of issues, from trade and technology to climate change and regional security.</p> <p>Political analysts warn that the outcome of the summit could have far‑reaching implications. “A successful agreement could signal a new era of U.S.–China cooperation, but a failure could lead to renewed tariff escalations and a hardening of technology restrictions,” said former U.S. Trade Representative Michael Gordon. “The stakes are high for both domestic industries and global markets.”<sup>[13]</sup></p> <h2>What to Watch</h2> <p>Investors and policymakers will keep a close eye on the summit’s outcomes. While the preliminary talks were successful, analysts caution that the final agreement could still be subject to political pressure and domestic lobbying. “The market’s optimism is contingent on the Biden‑Xi summit delivering concrete policy changes,” said analyst Laura Mills of Morgan Stanley. “Any deviation from the optimistic tone could trigger volatility, especially in sectors heavily exposed to U.S.–China trade dynamics.”<sup>[14]</sup></p> <p>Moreover, the U.S. administration’s next steps will be critical in shaping the long‑term economic relationship with China. A comprehensive trade deal could reduce tariffs, open new markets for U.S. exporters, and influence global supply chains. Conversely, a failure to reach an agreement could reinforce existing tensions, prompting further tariff adjustments and regulatory measures that could ripple through global markets.</p> <h2>Key Takeaways</h2> <ul> <li>USTR’s statement signals progress but does not yet resolve key issues such as forced technology transfer.</li> <li>China’s MOFCOM and Ambassador Liu Xiaoming emphasize a willingness to dialogue while maintaining current tariff levels.</li> <li>Market gains reflect optimism about potential trade easing, but uncertainty remains ahead of the April 20 summit.</li> <li>Economic analysts project a $30 billion annual reduction in the U.S. trade deficit if tariffs are lowered by 10 %.</li> <li>Industry groups welcome clarity but call for concrete commitments on intellectual property enforcement.</li> </ul> <p>As the world watches the upcoming summit, the stakes for both economies—and the global market—remain high.
