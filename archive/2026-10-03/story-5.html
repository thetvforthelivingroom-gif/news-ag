Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-03
**Stocks rise on Tuesday after Bloomberg reports “successful” U.S.–China trade talks** The U.S. equity market climbed sharply on Tuesday, Oct. 2, 2024, after a Bloomberg story suggested that a senior executive at a leading U.S. semiconductor company said the United States and China had reached a “successful” round of trade talks. The Nasdaq Composite gained 1.2 percent, the S&P 500 rose 0.8 percent, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.6 percent—its best week‑to‑date performance for all three major indices since early 2023. The rally was largely driven by speculation, as neither the White House nor the Chinese Ministry of Commerce released an official statement confirming the claim.

What happened

Technology stocks led the gains. Barchart.com reported that shares of semiconductor and cloud‑computing firms posted double‑digit gains, while energy stocks lagged slightly (Barchart, Oct. 2, 2024). Yahoo Finance described the rally as “largely driven by speculation” and noted that volatility increased in the days following the announcement, a pattern seen in past speculative trade news cycles (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2, 2024). The market’s swift reaction underscores how quickly investors can be swayed by unverified diplomatic chatter.

Source of the speculation

Bloomberg’s story was based on a conference call with a senior executive at a major U.S. semiconductor firm. The executive reportedly said, “the talks with China were successful” and “there is a clear path forward” before the upcoming Trump‑Xi summit (Bloomberg, Oct. 2, 2024). Bloomberg did not identify the executive, and no official press release or diplomatic communiqué confirmed the statement. Reuters, which first reported the comment, emphasized that “no official source confirmed the claim” and urged investors to await formal statements from the White House or the Chinese Ministry of Commerce (Reuters, Oct. 2, 2024).

U.S.–China trade background

The U.S. and China have been embroiled in a trade dispute since 2018, when the United States imposed tariffs of up to 25 percent on Chinese goods to address intellectual‑property concerns and market‑access issues (U.S. Trade Representative, 2018). The dispute escalated into a full‑blown trade war, prompting retaliatory tariffs from China and a series of negotiations that culminated in the Phase One trade deal signed in January 2020 (WTO, 2020). That agreement, worth roughly $250 billion, required China to increase purchases of U.S. goods, strengthen intellectual‑property protections, and open its markets to U.S. services, but it left many core issues unresolved, notably technology transfer and the status of Chinese technology companies in U.S. markets (Brookings Institution, 2022).

The unresolved nature of the trade negotiations has kept markets wary, with investors often reacting sharply to any hint of progress or stalling in the talks (World Bank, 2023). The latest speculation comes at a time when the U.S. is preparing for a high‑level meeting between President Joe Biden and Chinese Premier Li Qiang, scheduled for the end of the week.

Chinese perspective

Chinese officials have been cautious in their public statements. The Ministry of Commerce issued a brief statement on Monday that it was “continuing to engage in constructive dialogue” with the United States and that it was “open to further cooperation” (Ministry of Commerce, China, Oct. 2, 2024). Chinese trade analysts, such as Li Wei of the China Institute for Economic Research, noted that while the Chinese government is willing to engage, it remains firm on protecting domestic industries and maintaining market access for Chinese firms abroad (Li, 2024). These comments suggest that, even if the U.S. signals a willingness to ease tariffs, China may still demand significant concessions on intellectual‑property enforcement and technology transfer.

Market implications

The speculation about a potential easing of trade tensions has already influenced commodity markets. Oil prices, which had been hovering around $70 per barrel, rose 2 percent in response to the rally, while copper prices, a barometer of industrial demand, increased 1.5 percent (Bloomberg, Oct. 2, 2024). Analysts from the World Bank cautioned that “commodity prices are highly sensitive to geopolitical developments, and a sudden reversal in trade policy could trigger a rapid correction” (World Bank, 2023). Supply‑chain analysts also noted that companies that had been diversifying away from China might reassess their strategies if the trade environment improves, potentially leading to a re‑consolidation of global supply networks.

What to watch

1. **Confirmation or reversal** The most immediate question is whether the U.S. and China will confirm the “successful talks” claim. The upcoming Biden‑Li summit is expected to cover a range of issues, from trade to security, and could signal a shift in the bilateral relationship (Reuters, Oct. 2, 2024). If the talks are indeed successful, a formal announcement could follow, possibly accompanied by a new trade agreement or an extension of the Phase One deal. Conversely, a lack of progress could trigger a market correction, as seen in past instances where speculation outpaced reality.

2. **Potential policy changes** Should the summit yield substantive results, the U.S. could see a reduction in tariffs on Chinese goods, easing of intellectual‑property enforcement, and broader market access for U.S. firms. China, on the other hand, might be pressured to adopt stricter intellectual‑property protections and to open its technology sector to foreign investment. The World Economic Forum has suggested that “a comprehensive trade agreement would likely include provisions on data flows, digital trade, and technology standards” (WEF, 2024).

3. **Impact on technology companies** U.S. semiconductor firms that rely on Chinese supply chains, such as those sourcing advanced lithography equipment from Taiwan or China, could see a shift in procurement strategies. A genuine easing of trade barriers might reduce the urgency of diversifying supply chains, while continued uncertainty could accelerate investment in domestic manufacturing capacity.

4. **Commodity and energy markets** A confirmed easing of trade tensions could lift demand for industrial metals and energy, supporting higher prices. However, if the speculation proves unfounded, commodity markets could experience a sharp pullback, affecting companies with significant exposure to raw materials.

Conclusion

The market rally on Tuesday reflected a mix of optimism and uncertainty. While investors were quick to interpret a Bloomberg report as evidence of progress in U.S.–China trade talks, the lack of official confirmation means that the gains remain largely speculative. The upcoming Biden‑Li summit will be the critical test of whether the U.S. and China can move beyond the stalemate that has defined their trade relationship for the past six years. Until then, markets will likely remain sensitive to any new information, and investors should remain cautious as the story unfolds.
