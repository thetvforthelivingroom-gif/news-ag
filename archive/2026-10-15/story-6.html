Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-15
<article> <p>Stocks surged on Thursday after Deputy Trade Representative Thomas Bessent suggested that preliminary talks with China had been successful, just days before President Donald Trump and Premier Li Keqiang are scheduled to meet in Washington. The rally lifted the S&P 500 and Nasdaq into record territory, but no formal confirmation of the trade progress has yet been released, leaving investors to weigh a hopeful signal against lingering uncertainty.</p> <h2>What happened</h2> <p>At the open of the U.S. equity market, the S&P 500 climbed 1.8 percent and the Nasdaq jumped 2.2 percent—its largest single‑day gains in the past year. The MSCI World index rose 1.5 percent, while the U.S. dollar weakened 0.6 percent against a basket of major currencies. Trading volumes spiked, with technology stocks leading the rally: shares of major chipmakers and software firms gained between 3 and 5 percent, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average, which is weighted toward manufacturing, added 1.2 percent.</p> <p>The rally was triggered by a brief statement from Bessent, reported by Reuters, in which he said that “talks with China were successful” in preliminary rounds before the Trump‑Xi summit in Washington, D.C. The comment came during a briefing that was not formally released to the public, and no corroborating statements have yet appeared from the U.S. Trade Representative’s office or the White House. The Chinese Ministry of Commerce likewise issued no comment, leaving the market to interpret the signal on its own.</p> <h2>Background and context</h2> <p>The U.S. and China have been locked in a trade dispute since 2018. That year the United States imposed a 25 percent tariff on $34 billion of Chinese goods, followed by a 10 percent tariff on an additional $16 billion in 2019. China retaliated with a 25 percent tariff on $36 billion of U.S. goods and a 10 percent tariff on $10 billion of U.S. goods in 2020.</p> <p>In 2019 and 2021, the two sides held a series of trade talks that culminated in a “Phase One” agreement in January 2020. The deal required China to increase purchases of U.S. agricultural products and to strengthen intellectual‑property protections. However, it did not resolve the broader tariff list, and many U.S. exporters continued to face high duties and supply‑chain disruptions. The current tariff list remains in place, with the U.S. maintaining duties on $250 billion of Chinese goods and China maintaining duties on $200 billion of U.S. goods. The ongoing uncertainty has kept many U.S. investors wary of the trade environment, making any hint of progress a potential catalyst for market optimism.</p> <h2>Market reactions and perspectives</h2> <p>Investors have responded to Bessent’s statement with a mix of cautious optimism and skepticism. Those who view a reduction in trade friction as a boost to corporate earnings—often called “dovish” investors—have pushed technology and manufacturing stocks higher. Others, who see the comment as a political signal rather than a substantive policy shift—sometimes referred to as “hawks”—argue that no official confirmation has been issued and that the U.S. Trade Representative’s office has not yet released any new policy documents.</p> <p>The U.S. Treasury Department has also refrained from commenting, while the Chinese Ministry of Commerce issued a neutral statement that it is “monitoring the situation closely.” In the broader geopolitical context, the Trump‑Xi summit—scheduled for the following week—has been framed by some analysts as a chance for the two leaders to address not only trade but also broader strategic concerns, including technology transfer and regional security. The market’s reaction, therefore, reflects a broader sensitivity to any sign that the two superpowers might be moving toward a more stable economic relationship.</p> <h2>Historical echoes</h2> <p>Past trade‑policy signals have often spurred market rallies. In 2019, when the U.S. and China announced the “Phase One” trade deal, the S&P 500 surged 2 percent on the first trading day, while the Nasdaq climbed 3 percent. In 2021, after the U.S. rolled back certain tariffs in exchange for China’s commitments, the market again rallied, with the MSCI World index rising 1.7 percent. These episodes demonstrate that equity markets have historically reacted strongly to trade‑policy signals, even when the underlying agreements were incomplete or subject to later revisions. The current rally follows a similar pattern, suggesting that investors are primed to interpret any hint of progress as a potential positive outcome for corporate profitability and global supply chains.</p> <h2>What to watch</h2> <p>The next few days will be critical for investors and policymakers alike. Official releases from the U.S. Trade Representative’s office and the White House will clarify whether Bessent’s statement reflects an actual policy shift or merely a preliminary assessment. Likewise, the Chinese Ministry of Commerce’s response—or lack thereof—will be closely monitored. The outcome of the Trump‑Xi summit, scheduled for next week, will be the ultimate test of whether the two sides can move beyond rhetoric and reach concrete agreements on tariffs, intellectual property, and supply‑chain security.</p> <p>Any substantive concessions or tariff rollbacks could sustain the current market rally, while a failure to reach an agreement could trigger a sharp correction.
