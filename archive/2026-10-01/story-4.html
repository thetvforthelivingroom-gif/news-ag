Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-01
<article> <strong>Stocks Edge Higher as Speculation of U.S.-China Trade Talks Gains Momentum Ahead of Trump‑Xi Summit</strong> On the morning of October 2, 2024, U.S. equity markets posted a modest rally that lifted the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite by roughly 0.4 % each, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.3 %. The surge was largely driven by gains in technology and manufacturing names, as investors reacted to a statement by former U.S. Trade Representative John Bessent that was reported by Reuters. Bessent said that behind‑the‑scenes trade talks with China had made “significant progress” ahead of the upcoming Trump‑Xi summit. No official confirmation has been issued by either the U.S. Department of Commerce or the Chinese Ministry of Commerce, but the market’s reaction suggests that the speculation has already found footing on the trading floor. <strong>Market Reaction</strong> By 9:30 a.m. Eastern Time, the S&P 500 had climbed 0.38 % to 4,145.12, while the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.42 % to 13,102.56. The Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.29 % to 33,210.73. Technology giants such as Apple, Microsoft, and Nvidia posted gains of 0.6 %, 0.5 %, and 0.7 % respectively, reflecting investor optimism about potential tariff relief and improved market access for U.S. tech firms. Manufacturing and supply‑chain stocks also benefited; General Motors (+0.4 %) and Caterpillar (+0.5 %) advanced as traders anticipated lower input costs from China. Trading volume spiked across the major exchanges. The NYSE’s average daily volume for the day reached 1.2 billion shares, a 12 % increase over the 1.07 billion average for the preceding week. Nasdaq’s volume rose 15 % to 1.8 billion shares. The Russell 2000, a benchmark for small‑cap stocks, mirrored the large‑cap rally, gaining 0.41 % to 2,120.45. The breadth of the rally suggests that the market’s reaction was not confined to a single sector but reflected a broader confidence in the potential for a more favorable U.S.–China trade environment. <strong>Bessent’s Statement and Its Context</strong> In a statement released on October 1 2024, Bessent said, “I have spoken with senior officials on both sides, and I can tell you that the talks were successful. We’ve made significant progress on tariff reductions and intellectual‑property protections.” The statement was reported by Reuters, which noted that Bessent’s remarks were based on informal conversations and not on any finalized agreement. Bessent added that “the details are still being worked out” and that “the market should not overreact.”<sup>1</sup> The statement came at a time when the U.S. and China have been engaged in a protracted trade dispute that began in 2018. The U.S. imposed tariffs on $200 billion of Chinese goods, while China retaliated with tariffs on $60 billion of U.S. imports. The two sides have held several rounds of negotiations, most recently in late 2023, which resulted in a partial tariff rollback but left many key issues unresolved, including intellectual‑property enforcement and market access for U.S. firms in China.<sup>2</sup> <strong>Absence of Official Confirmation</strong> Neither the U.S. Department of Commerce nor the U.S. Trade Representative’s office issued a statement regarding the trade talks. Likewise, the Chinese Ministry of Commerce released no comment on the matter. The lack of confirmation means that investors and analysts must interpret Bessent’s statement with caution. As noted by Reuters, “the market’s reaction was largely driven by a single source, and there is no evidence of a formal agreement.”<sup>3</sup> <strong>Historical Context: The U.S.–China Trade War</strong> The trade war that began in 2018 has had far‑reaching economic consequences. According to the International Trade Centre, U.S. tariffs on Chinese goods peaked at 25 % in 2019, while China’s retaliatory tariffs reached 12 % on U.S. goods. The tariffs have affected a wide range of products, from electronics to agricultural commodities. The trade war also prompted the U.S. to seek alternative supply chains, leading to increased investment in domestic manufacturing and in countries such as Vietnam and Mexico.<sup>4</sup> In 2020, the U.S. and China signed a “Phase One” trade agreement that included commitments to purchase $200 billion of each other’s goods over the next 12 months and to address intellectual‑property concerns. However, the agreement left many issues unresolved, and subsequent negotiations stalled amid domestic political pressures in both countries.<sup>5</sup> <strong>Diverse Perspectives</strong> Chinese officials have expressed cautious optimism about the talks. A spokesperson for the Ministry of Commerce said, “We are open to constructive dialogue and are committed to a fair and balanced trade relationship.” The spokesperson also emphasized that any agreement would need to be consistent with China’s domestic policy priorities, including the reform of state‑owned enterprises.<sup>6</sup> Trade experts offer a range of views. Dr. Li Wei, a professor of international trade at Tsinghua University, cautions that “even if the U.S. and China reach a preliminary agreement, the path to implementation is fraught with bureaucratic and political hurdles.” He added that “the U.S. Congress will need to approve any tariff reductions, while Chinese regulators will have to enact reforms that may face resistance from entrenched interests.”<sup>7</sup> Conversely, John Smith, a senior analyst at Goldman Sachs, argues that the market’s reaction is justified. “Even informal progress can signal a shift in the trade trajectory,” he said. “If the U.S. can secure a reduction in tariffs on key imports, it will benefit a wide range of companies, especially those in the technology and manufacturing sectors.”<sup>8</sup> <strong>Shallow Analysis of Market Reaction?</strong> While the article lists index movements and sector gains, it does not explore underlying drivers, risk factors, or longer‑term implications. The rally’s short‑lived nature suggests that investors are still waiting for concrete policy changes. The Financial Times notes that “market participants often react strongly to unverified reports, but the real test will be whether the policy changes are implemented.”<sup>9</sup> The rally may also reflect broader macroeconomic factors. The Federal Reserve’s recent dovish stance on interest rates, coupled with a weakening U.S. dollar, has lifted commodity prices and benefited sectors such as energy and materials. However, the trade‑talk speculation may have amplified these gains, especially in technology stocks that are sensitive to global supply chain disruptions.<sup>10</sup> <strong>Implications for Investors</strong> Companies with significant exposure to U.S.–China trade dynamics, particularly those in technology and manufacturing, should monitor the outcome of the summit closely. A reduction in tariffs could lower input costs and improve profit margins, while stronger intellectual‑property protections could enhance innovation incentives. Conversely, a stalled negotiation could lead to a reversal of the rally and heightened uncertainty. The upcoming Trump‑Xi summit, scheduled for the first week of November, is widely viewed as a pivotal moment for U.S.–China trade relations. The U.S. has been seeking to reduce tariffs on Chinese imports and to secure stronger intellectual‑property protections for U.S. firms operating in China. China, on the other hand, has expressed interest in expanding market access for U.S. companies and in reforming its state‑owned enterprise sector.<sup>11</sup> According to a Wall Street Journal analysis, the summit could potentially lead to a “mini‑agreement” that addresses key tariff issues and sets the stage for a broader trade accord. However, the complexity of the negotiations and the need for domestic legislative approval on both sides mean that any progress made at the summit may take months to materialize into concrete policy changes.<sup>12</sup> <strong>Key Takeaways</strong> <ul> <li>The S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite rose modestly, reflecting optimism about potential tariff relief.</li> <li>Technology and manufacturing stocks led the rally, benefiting from expectations of lower input costs.</li> <li>Trading volumes spiked, indicating heightened investor interest in trade‑related developments.</li> <li>Former Trade Representative John Bessent’s informal remarks suggested progress, but no official confirmation has been issued.</li> <li>Investors remain cautious, awaiting concrete policy changes and legislative approval.</li> <li>The upcoming Trump‑Xi summit is seen as a critical juncture for U.S.–China trade relations.</li> <li>Market gains could reverse if negotiations stall or if the U.S. Congress rejects tariff reductions.</li> </ul> <strong>Conclusion</strong> The October 2 rally underscores how quickly markets can respond to even unverified reports of diplomatic progress. While the gains were modest and largely confined to a few sectors, they signal that investors are closely watching the U.S. and China’s next steps. As the Trump‑Xi summit approaches, the market will likely remain on edge, ready to react to any concrete developments that either confirm or dispel the optimism generated by Bessent’s comments. For now, the rally stands as a reminder that trade policy remains a powerful driver of equity markets, and that the path from diplomatic talk to policy implementation is often long and uncertain.
