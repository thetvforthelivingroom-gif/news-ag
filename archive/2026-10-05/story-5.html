Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-05
<article> <strong>Stocks Rally on Tom Bessent’s Optimistic China Talks, but Are Markets Reading Too Much into a Former Trade Negotiator’s Claim?</strong> On Thursday, April 24, the U.S. equity market surged after former senior trade negotiator Tom Bessent told a CNBC panel that “we reached a breakthrough” in talks with China. The S&P 500 jumped 1.2 percent, the Nasdaq climbed 1.8 percent, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 1.0 percent, sending technology, industrial, and agricultural stocks higher. While the rally was swift and broad, investors and analysts are questioning whether Bessent’s statement reflects a genuine policy shift or merely a political signal ahead of President Trump’s highly publicized summit with President Xi on May 15 [1]. Bessent, who helped draft the Phase 1 trade deal signed in January 2020, appeared on CNBC’s “Squawk Box” at 9:30 a.m. ET and said, “We reached a breakthrough in our latest round of negotiations with China.” He added that the talks had “cleared a major hurdle” and that the United States was “in a position to roll back tariffs if the summit goes well.” The comments were made just two weeks before the Trump‑Xi meeting, scheduled for Washington, D.C. The market reacted immediately: the Nasdaq’s average daily turnover rose to a multi‑month high, and the S&P 500’s technology sector gained 2.3 percent, the largest one‑day gain in the sector since March 2022. Meanwhile, the U.S. soybean futures market, which had been under pressure from Chinese tariffs, saw a 3.5 percent uptick in trading volume, reflecting optimism among agricultural exporters [2]. Bessent’s remarks carry weight because he was a key architect of the Phase 1 trade deal, which temporarily eased tensions by requiring China to purchase $200 billion of U.S. goods over two years and by reducing some tariffs. Since then, the U.S. and China have been negotiating a Phase 2 agreement, which would address remaining issues such as intellectual property, technology transfer, and market access. The negotiations have stalled over disagreements about the scope of tariff reductions and the enforcement of intellectual property rights. Former U.S. Trade Representative Robert Lighthizer, who succeeded Bessent in the Trump administration, said in a Senate hearing that “the U.S. remains committed to a comprehensive trade agreement that protects American intellectual property and ensures market access for U.S. firms” [3]. Meanwhile, Chinese Commerce Minister Wang Wentao has repeatedly emphasized that China seeks a “fair, reciprocal, and mutually beneficial” trade relationship, but he has also warned that the U.S. must “respect China’s sovereignty and non‑interference in internal affairs” [4]. The juxtaposition of Bessent’s optimistic tone with the cautious stances of both sides raises questions about whether the U.S. is truly ready to roll back tariffs or whether the statement is a strategic move to influence market sentiment ahead of the summit. The market’s enthusiasm may be premature, especially given that the U.S. dollar’s strength could offset any gains from tariff reductions. The dollar was up 0.4 percent against the yuan on the day of Bessent’s comments, dampening the potential impact on corporate earnings [5]. Historical Context: From Trade War to Trade Talks The U.S.–China trade war began in 2018 when President Trump imposed tariffs on $250 billion of Chinese goods, citing unfair trade practices. The tariffs triggered retaliatory measures from China and led to a series of trade disputes that lasted until the Phase 1 deal in 2020. Although the deal temporarily eased tensions, many provisions remained unresolved, and the U.S. continued to impose tariffs on $50 billion of Chinese goods in 2021 and 2022. Since the Phase 1 agreement, the U.S. has pursued a “phase‑by‑phase” approach to trade normalization, with the Phase 2 talks focusing on deeper issues such as technology, data security, and market access. The talks have been stalled by disagreements over the scope of tariff reductions and the enforcement of intellectual property rights. The U.S. has also expressed concern that China’s “Made in China 2025” plan could undermine U.S. technology firms, while China has argued that U.S. tariffs harm its domestic industries and consumers [6]. The upcoming Trump‑Xi summit is seen as a potential turning point. If the leaders can agree on a comprehensive trade framework, it could signal a new era of cooperation and reduce uncertainty for investors. However, the mere announcement of a summit does not guarantee substantive progress. Analysts caution that the U.S. and China have historically used high‑profile meetings to signal intent while leaving substantive negotiations to later stages. Diverse Perspectives on the Market Reaction <strong>Industry Analysts</strong> Tech analyst Sarah Kim of Bloomberg News cautions that “the market’s enthusiasm may be premature.” Kim notes that the technology sector’s gains are largely driven by speculative bets on tariff reductions rather than concrete policy changes. She highlights that companies such as Intel and NVIDIA rely on complex supply chains that span multiple countries, and any tariff rollback would need to be accompanied by clear regulatory guidance to be effective [7]. <strong>Economists</strong> Professor David Liu of the University of Chicago argues that the rally reflects “a classic example of market overreaction.” Liu points out that the U.S. economy has already been adjusting to higher tariffs through supply‑chain diversification, and that a modest tariff reduction would likely have a limited impact on overall trade balances. He also notes that the U.S. dollar’s strength could offset any gains from reduced tariffs, dampening the effect on corporate earnings [8]. <strong>Chinese Officials</strong> In a statement released by the Ministry of Commerce, Wang Wentao said, “We welcome the U.S. to engage in constructive dialogue, but we remain firm that any agreement must respect China’s sovereignty and non‑interference in internal affairs.” The statement underscored China’s willingness to negotiate but also its reluctance to concede to U.S. demands that could be perceived as political pressure [9]. <strong>Corporate Executives</strong> Apple CEO Tim Cook, speaking at a shareholder meeting, emphasized the importance of “stable trade relations” for the company’s global supply chain. Cook noted that Apple had already diversified its suppliers to mitigate tariff risks, but that “a clear, predictable trade environment would still benefit our long‑term planning” [10]. Counterarguments and Alternative Interpretations 1. <strong>Political Timing</strong> Some market participants view Bessent’s comments as a political maneuver to boost his personal profile ahead of the Trump‑Xi summit. The timing—just weeks before the meeting—raises questions about whether the statement was intended to influence market sentiment rather than reflect actual progress. 2. <strong>Lack of Official Confirmation</strong> No official statement has been issued by the U.S. Trade Representative’s office or China’s Ministry of Commerce confirming the “breakthrough.” The absence of corroborating evidence fuels skepticism that the market’s reaction is based on speculation rather than fact. 3. <strong>Potential for Stalled Negotiations</strong> Even if the summit leads to a breakthrough, the implementation of tariff reductions could be delayed by domestic political pressures in both countries. Congressional opposition in the U.S. and political factions in China could stall any agreement, rendering the market’s optimism premature. 4. <strong>Impact on Other Sectors</strong> While technology and agriculture stocks benefited, other sectors such as energy and manufacturing have not seen similar gains. This uneven response suggests that the market’s reaction may be driven by specific expectations rather than a broad‑based confidence in trade normalization. Market Implications: What Investors Should Watch <strong>Tariff Adjustments</strong> Investors should monitor any announcements of tariff reductions, particularly for high‑value goods such as semiconductors and agricultural products. Even a modest rollback could boost earnings for companies that rely on Chinese markets. <strong>Supply‑Chain Reconfiguration</strong> Companies that have diversified their supply chains away from China may reassess their strategies if the market signals a genuine easing of trade barriers. The cost of re‑establishing supply chains could offset potential gains from tariff reductions. <strong>Currency Movements</strong> The U.S. dollar’s strength could diminish the positive impact of tariff cuts on U.S. exporters. A stronger dollar raises the cost of U.S. goods abroad, potentially offsetting the benefit of lower tariffs. Investors should watch for shifts in the dollar‑yuan exchange rate, as it directly affects trade‑related earnings. <strong>Political Developments</strong> Domestic politics in both the United States and China will shape the pace and scope of any trade agreement. Congressional hearings, regulatory approvals, and party politics in China can all delay or derail progress. Market participants should stay alert to legislative developments that could influence the implementation of any tariff changes. <strong>Sector‑Specific Outlook</strong> While technology and agriculture have already benefited from the rally, other sectors—particularly energy, industrial manufacturing, and consumer staples—may experience slower gains. Investors should evaluate how tariff changes will affect each sector’s cost structure and pricing power. Conclusion Tom Bessent’s announcement of a “breakthrough” in U.S.–China trade talks sparked a swift market rally, but the underlying reality remains uncertain. The optimism reflected in the stock indices may be driven more by speculation than by concrete policy shifts. Key questions persist: Will the Trump‑Xi summit deliver a comprehensive trade framework? Will tariff reductions materialize, and if so, how quickly will they be implemented? How will currency movements and domestic politics influence the final outcome? For investors, the lesson is clear: remain cautious and focus on tangible indicators—official statements, tariff schedules, and regulatory approvals—rather than on headlines alone. While the market’s enthusiasm is understandable, a measured approach that weighs both the potential benefits and the risks will serve those navigating the evolving U.S.–China trade landscape best.
