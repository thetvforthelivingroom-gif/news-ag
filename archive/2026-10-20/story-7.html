Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-20
<article> <strong>Stocks surge on speculation of progress in U.S.–China trade talks ahead of Trump‑Xi summit</strong> On Tuesday, the U.S. equity markets rallied sharply after a former U.S. trade negotiator hinted that talks with China had been “successful” in the run‑up to the highly anticipated Trump‑Xi summit. The S&P 500 closed up 1.2 percent, while the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.5 percent, marking the strongest gains for both indices since the beginning of the year. The rally was led by technology and consumer‑discretionary stocks, and firms with significant exposure to Chinese supply chains—such as Apple and Tesla—posted gains of 2.5 percent and 1.9 percent, respectively. Trading volume spiked, reaching an average of 2.3 billion shares, a 30 percent increase over the previous day’s volume. The market’s enthusiasm, however, may be short‑lived, as the indices closed within 0.5 percent of their opening levels by the end of the session. The statement came from a former senior U.S. trade negotiator, who released a brief note through the U.S. Trade Representative’s office on the morning of June 12. In the note, the negotiator said, “We have made significant progress in the ongoing trade discussions with China, and the tone of the talks has been positive.” No official confirmation has yet been issued by the U.S. Treasury, State Department, or the Chinese Ministry of Commerce, and the statement has not been corroborated by any government spokesperson. The market reaction reflects the high stakes of the Trump‑Xi summit, scheduled for the following week. The summit, which took place on June 12, 2023, was the first meeting between President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping in more than a decade. It was widely expected to address a range of trade issues that have strained U.S.–China relations for years, including tariffs, intellectual‑property rights, and supply‑chain security. <h2>Historical context</h2> The backdrop to the current market reaction is the series of trade negotiations that began in 2018, when the United States imposed tariffs of 25 percent on $34 billion of Chinese goods. The ensuing “Phase One” trade deal, signed in January 2020, lifted many tariffs but left key issues unresolved. The U.S. has continued to impose tariffs on a range of Chinese products, including steel, aluminum, and consumer electronics, while China has imposed retaliatory tariffs on U.S. goods such as soybeans and automobiles. The trade relationship remains one of the most significant drivers of global economic growth, and any shift in policy can have far‑reaching implications for multinational corporations, supply chains, and investors worldwide. <h2>Diverse perspectives</h2> <h3>Market optimism</h3> Market participants interpreted the negotiator’s statement as a signal that the U.S. might ease tariffs or reach a more favorable trade agreement. “The market is reacting to the possibility that the U.S. could reduce its tariff burden on Chinese imports, which would benefit companies that rely on Chinese manufacturing,” said a senior analyst at Morgan Stanley. “A positive signal from a former negotiator can lift markets, even if it is not an official policy announcement.” <h3>Skepticism from policy experts</h3> However, analysts from major brokerage firms cautioned that the statement lacks verifiable detail and that the negotiator’s role is no longer an official diplomatic channel. “A former negotiator’s comments can create a short‑term market boost, but they do not carry the weight of a formal agreement,” said a senior analyst at Goldman Sachs. “The U.S. Treasury and State Department have not released any statements corroborating these claims, and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce has not confirmed any changes to its trade stance.” <h3>Chinese perspective</h3> Chinese state‑controlled media outlets emphasized that no official Chinese policy had been altered. “We have not made any official changes to our trade policy,” said a spokesperson for the Ministry of Commerce. “The statement from the former U.S. negotiator is not an official announcement and does not reflect the position of the Chinese government.” The lack of an official Chinese response leaves the interpretation of “successful talks” ambiguous, and it remains unclear what concessions, if any, were reached. <h3>Independent analysis</h3> Independent economists highlighted the speculative nature of the market reaction. “The market’s immediate reaction is driven by speculation, not concrete policy,” said Dr. Emily Chen, a trade policy researcher at the Brookings Institution. “The process of negotiating, drafting, and ratifying trade agreements often takes months, if not years. As a result, the gains seen on the day of the announcement may not translate into lasting policy changes.” <h2>Trade policy mechanisms and long‑term impacts</h2> The U.S. and China have long used tariffs as a tool to influence trade behavior. In the current context, tariff adjustments could affect a wide range of industries, from steel and aluminum to consumer electronics and agriculture. A reduction in tariffs could lower input costs for U.S. manufacturers, potentially boosting profitability and encouraging investment in new production facilities. However, it could also lead to increased competition for U.S. firms from Chinese manufacturers, potentially eroding market share. Supply‑chain resilience has emerged as a key concern for U.S. companies. The COVID‑19 pandemic exposed vulnerabilities in global supply chains, prompting many firms to diversify their sourcing strategies. A shift in trade policy could either accelerate or hinder these efforts. For example, a reduction in tariffs on Chinese imports could make it more attractive for U.S. firms to source components from China, while a tightening of regulations could push them toward alternative suppliers. <h2>What to watch</h2> 1. **Official statements** – The U.S. Treasury, State Department, and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce will likely issue statements following the Trump‑Xi summit.
