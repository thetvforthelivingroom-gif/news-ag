Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-20
Global stocks surged sharply after an unverified claim that U.S. trade officials had a productive dialogue with China before President Trump’s meeting with President Xi. The rally, driven by optimism that tariff tensions might ease, shows how quickly markets can respond to tentative signals of diplomatic progress.

What happened

On the day of President Donald Trump’s summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite rose 0.7 % and 0.9 % respectively, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 0.6 % [13]. Technology and consumer‑discretionary stocks led the gains, with shares of Apple, Microsoft, and Amazon increasing between 1.2 % and 1.5 % in a single session. Energy and industrials also benefited; crude‑oil futures moved up 0.4 % after the announcement of a “successful” pre‑meeting discussion between senior U.S. Treasury and Commerce officials and their Chinese counterparts [13].

The market reaction was swift. Within minutes of the statement, the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) fell from 20.3 to 18.9, reflecting a drop in perceived risk. Analysts noted that the spike in indices was largely driven by investors’ expectation that the Trump‑Xi talks might lead to a formal tariff‑relief package or at least a pause in further tariff escalations [13].

Background and context

The U.S.–China trade war began in 2018 when President Trump imposed 25 % tariffs on $34 billion of Chinese goods, citing intellectual‑property abuses and unfair trade practices. China retaliated with 25 % tariffs on $36 billion of U.S. goods, sparking a cycle of escalating duties that strained global supply chains [11]. In January 2019, the two sides signed a “Phase‑One” trade agreement that lifted tariffs on $200 billion of U.S. goods, but the deal left many issues unresolved, including technology transfer and market access for U.S. companies [11].

The senior official who made the claim—often referred to as “Bessent” in market circles—holds a key position in the U.S. Treasury and Commerce departments, overseeing trade‑policy implementation. His remarks, made on the morning of the summit, were not accompanied by a formal statement from either administration, leaving the precise content of the discussion unclear [13]. The lack of an official communiqué has led to speculation about whether the talk resulted in concrete policy shifts or merely a brief exchange of perspectives.

The bigger picture (diverse perspectives)

Market optimism vs. caution

While the immediate market response was positive, some analysts caution that the rally may be premature. “The markets are reacting to a rumor, not a policy change,” said a senior economist at Goldman Sachs, noting that the U.S. government has yet to announce any tariff adjustments following the meeting [13]. The VIX’s decline suggests short‑term risk aversion, but it could rebound if the expected policy shift fails to materialize.

Technological and manufacturing implications

Technology firms, which have faced supply‑chain disruptions and increased costs due to tariffs on components such as semiconductors, saw the largest gains. Investors hope that a de‑tariffing of critical components could reduce production costs and improve margins. However, the U.S. government has maintained that any tariff relief would be contingent on China meeting certain intellectual‑property and market‑access conditions, which remain unverified [13].

Manufacturing stocks, particularly those in the automotive and electronics sectors, also benefited. The potential easing of tariffs on steel, aluminum, and electronic components could lower input costs for U.S. manufacturers that rely on Chinese suppliers. Yet, some industry leaders warn that the current tariff framework still imposes significant uncertainty on long‑term supply‑chain planning [13].

Commodity exposure

Energy companies experienced a modest uptick as oil prices rose in response to the perceived reduction in trade friction. Analysts noted that a more stable trade environment could improve global economic growth prospects, thereby supporting commodity demand. However, the commodity market remains sensitive to geopolitical developments, and any misinterpretation of the “successful” talks could trigger a reversal [13].

Historical precedent

Similar market rallies have occurred after unverified statements about U.S.–China trade talks. In 2018, a rumor of a “deal” between the two administrations caused the S&P 500 to rise 0.5 % before falling back once the rumor was debunked. These episodes illustrate the market’s tendency to overreact to ambiguous signals, only to correct when the underlying policy remains unchanged [14].

Domestic pressures

Within the United States, lawmakers from both parties have expressed divergent views. Republicans have called for a firm stance against China, arguing that any concessions could undermine U.S. competitive advantage. Democrats emphasize the need to protect U.S. workers and businesses from unfair trade practices. The Chinese government has reiterated its commitment to opening its markets, but has also warned against “unilateral” tariff reductions that could harm its domestic industries [12].

What to watch (future implications)

Official confirmation

Investors will closely monitor any formal statements from the U.S. Treasury, Commerce, or the White House regarding tariff adjustments. The absence of a clear policy announcement could lead to renewed volatility, especially if market expectations diverge from the actual outcomes of the Trump‑Xi meeting.

Trade‑policy trajectory

If the U.S. government follows through on tariff relief, it could signal a shift toward a more collaborative trade relationship, potentially easing tensions in other areas such as technology transfer and intellectual‑property enforcement. Conversely, a failure to deliver on the “successful” talks could reinforce the perception of a stalled trade relationship, prompting a return to higher tariffs or new trade barriers.

Impact on global supply chains

A genuine easing of tariffs would likely reduce costs for U.S. manufacturers and technology firms, potentially accelerating the shift toward more resilient supply chains. However, any abrupt policy reversal could disrupt existing agreements and increase uncertainty for multinational corporations.

Market sentiment and risk appetite

The VIX’s current decline suggests a short‑term improvement in risk sentiment. Yet, the market remains sensitive to any new information that could alter the trade trajectory. Analysts advise maintaining a cautious stance, especially given the complex interplay between domestic politics and international diplomacy.

In the days and weeks ahead, global markets will be watching for concrete policy signals that either confirm or refute the preliminary optimism sparked by the “successful” pre‑meeting talks. Until such clarity emerges, the rally’s longevity will hinge on the ability of U.S. and Chinese officials to translate tentative dialogue into tangible policy moves that address the core issues of the trade war.
