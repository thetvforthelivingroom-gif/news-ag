Ukraine deploys FP-7 tactical ballistic missile in combat for first time, Zelenskiy says

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-20
**Ukraine reportedly deploys captured FP‑7 ballistic missile in combat, Zelenskiy says** On 12 October 2024 President Volodymyr Zelenskiy announced that Ukraine had fired a captured Russian short‑range ballistic missile, the FP‑7 (9M111 Fagot), from a mobile launcher near Toretsk in the Donetsk region. The claim follows a series of reports that Kyiv has increasingly turned seized Russian equipment into a strategic asset, but independent verification remains pending. ---

What happened

The Ukrainian Ministry of Defence (MoD) released a statement on 12 October confirming that a single FP‑7 missile was launched from a truck‑mounted launcher on the outskirts of Toretsk. According to the MoD, the missile struck a Russian logistics hub, reportedly destroying a convoy of supply vehicles and damaging a nearby command post. The launch was carried out under strict operational security, and the missile was integrated into the Ukrainian command‑and‑control network. Zelenskiy praised the use of the missile as a “symbol of resilience” and highlighted it as evidence that Ukraine can adapt enemy technology to its own advantage. No technical details beyond the classification of the system as a short‑range ballistic missile were provided.

The claim has been reported by several Ukrainian media outlets and corroborated by a brief statement from the Defence Ministry. However, satellite imagery or third‑party observers have not yet confirmed the launch, and analysts caution that such reports can be subject to propaganda or misinterpretation.

---

Technical background

The FP‑7, officially designated 9M111 Fagot, is a Soviet‑era short‑range ballistic missile originally developed for the Russian Army. The base variant has a maximum range of about 80 km, while the upgraded 9M111F variant can reach up to 150 km under optimal conditions. The missile is typically launched from a 9A‑91 or 9A‑91M truck‑mounted system and carries a high‑explosive warhead. Compared with U.S.‑made HIMARS (High‑Mobility Artillery Rocket System) and the longer‑range ATACMS (Army Tactical Missile System), the FP‑7 offers a smaller footprint and lower cost per launch but a more limited range.

Ukraine has relied heavily on HIMARS and ATACMS for precision strikes against Russian positions and supply lines since the war began. The introduction of the FP‑7 into the Ukrainian arsenal, whether through capture or procurement, represents a diversification of strike options that could fill a niche between conventional artillery and longer‑range missile systems.

---

Acquisition and refurbishment

Ukrainian sources claim that the FP‑7 missile and its launcher were captured from Russian forces during earlier engagements in the Donbas region. While no independent confirmation of the capture exists, the MoD stated that Ukrainian engineers performed a refurbishment of the system to ensure reliability and compatibility with Ukrainian logistics and maintenance procedures. The refurbishment reportedly involved updating the guidance software, replacing worn components, and integrating the missile into the Ukrainian battlefield management system.

The use of captured equipment is not new for Ukraine. Since 2022, the country has repurposed a range of Russian vehicles, artillery pieces, and small arms, often with the help of Western technical support. The FP‑7 deployment continues this trend and underscores Ukraine’s growing technical capability to adapt foreign systems for its own use.

---

Strategic implications

If verified, the deployment of the FP‑7 could have several strategic implications. First, it expands Ukraine’s strike depth beyond the reach of conventional artillery, allowing it to target Russian logistics hubs, supply depots, and command posts at distances up to 150 km. Second, it introduces a psychological element: the use of a Russian‑made missile against Russian forces may erode morale and complicate Russia’s operational planning. Third, it signals to NATO and other partners that Ukraine is capable of integrating captured equipment, potentially influencing future aid packages and procurement strategies.

The MoD emphasized that the FP‑7 deployment is part of a broader strategy to increase strike depth and precision. Analysts note that the missile’s relatively low cost and mobility make it an attractive option for Ukraine, especially as Western deliveries of HIMARS and ATACMS continue to face logistical and political constraints.

---

NATO and international context

NATO’s support for Ukraine has largely focused on artillery and missile systems such as HIMARS and ATACMS. The FP‑7 deployment highlights a gap in short‑range ballistic capabilities that NATO could address in future aid packages. In a statement released by the NATO Secretary‑General on 13 October, the alliance acknowledged the importance of providing Ukraine with a diverse range of strike options and called for independent verification of the FP‑7 deployment to assess its operational impact.

The event may prompt NATO to accelerate the delivery of additional short‑range precision weapons, such as the U.S. Army’s 155 mm howitzer or the British Stormer system, to complement existing capabilities. It also underscores the need for robust intelligence sharing and real‑time battlefield monitoring to ensure that NATO can respond promptly to emerging threats.

---

Russian perspective

While Russian officials have not released an official statement on the FP‑7 deployment, analysts predict that Moscow will view the event as a warning sign of Ukraine’s growing technical adaptability. The use of a Russian‑made missile against Russian forces could be leveraged by Russian media to rally domestic support and justify increased defensive measures. On the operational side, Russian commanders may intensify efforts to protect logistics hubs and command posts, potentially diverting resources from frontline operations and creating new vulnerabilities for Ukrainian forces to exploit.

---

Verification gap

The most significant limitation of the current report is the lack of independent verification. No satellite imagery, third‑party intelligence, or on‑the‑ground reporting has yet confirmed the launch, trajectory, or impact of the FP‑7 missile. Analysts caution that, in the absence of corroborating evidence, the strategic significance of the deployment remains uncertain. Until such verification is available, observers will likely exercise caution in assessing the missile’s effectiveness and the broader implications for the conflict.

---

Future implications

Short‑term

In the immediate term, the FP‑7 deployment could prompt Russian forces to increase their focus on protecting logistics hubs and command posts, potentially diverting resources from frontline operations. This shift could create new vulnerabilities that Ukrainian forces could exploit, leading to a tactical advantage in the eastern front.

Medium‑term

The event may influence NATO’s procurement strategy, accelerating the delivery of new short‑range precision weapons and encouraging a broader range of missile systems in Ukraine’s arsenal. It could also prompt a review of rules of engagement for ballistic missile use in the region, as the potential for escalation increases.

Long‑term

If Ukraine successfully integrates captured Russian equipment into its arsenal, it could set a precedent for other conflict zones where captured materiel can be repurposed. This could lead to a new paradigm in modern warfare, where the ownership of weapons is fluid and the line between friend and foe becomes blurred. The deployment may also influence the global arms market, prompting countries to reassess export controls on short‑range ballistic systems and invest in new technologies that can be rapidly adapted for battlefield use.

---

Conclusion

Ukraine’s reported deployment of a captured FP‑7 ballistic missile represents a significant, if unverified, development in the conflict. It underscores the country’s growing technical capability to adapt enemy equipment, expands its strike depth beyond conventional artillery, and introduces a new psychological element to the battlefield. While independent verification remains pending, the announcement has already shifted strategic calculations on both sides and may influence future NATO aid packages and the global arms market. As the war continues to evolve, Ukraine’s ability to turn captured Russian technology into a strategic advantage will likely remain a key factor in shaping the conflict’s trajectory.
