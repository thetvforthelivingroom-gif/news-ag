Poland has warned that Russia is preparing drone or rocket strikes against NATO territory, a claim that could heighten tensions in a region already on edge after the Ukraine war. The warning, issued by the Polish Ministry of Defence on 20 September 2024, was reported by several news outlets and has prompted discussions across the alliance about deterrence and the balance of power in Eastern Europe. While the intelligence behind the claim has not been fully disclosed, the statement underscores the heightened tensions that have persisted since Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine and the broader strategic contest between NATO and Moscow.

What Happened

On 20 September 2024, the Polish Ministry of Defence released a statement that Russian intelligence assessments indicated preparations for drone or missile attacks targeting NATO member states. The announcement came amid a series of Russian military exercises near the Polish border, notably the “Anakonda 2024” exercise, which included live‑fire drills involving air‑defence systems and missile launchers (Polish Ministry of Defence, 2024‑09‑20). Polish officials emphasised that the threat is not yet a confirmed attack but a credible risk that warrants vigilance and preparedness from both national and alliance forces. The statement was picked up by international media, sparking immediate reactions from NATO allies who are reassessing their air and missile defence postures in the area. Polish officials also called for a coordinated NATO response to deter any potential aggression and reassure neighbouring countries (Reuters, 2024‑09‑20).

The warning is part of a broader pattern of Russian strategic posturing. Analysts note that Moscow has used hybrid warfare tactics, including cyber operations and asymmetric attacks, to exert influence and pressure on neighbouring states (BBC, 2024‑09‑22). The alert comes at a time when NATO has been bolstering its presence in Eastern Europe, deploying additional troops and equipment to the Baltic states and Poland itself (NATO, 2024‑09‑21). Poland’s current coalition government, led by Prime Minister Donald Tusk’s Civic Coalition, has previously highlighted the importance of maintaining a robust defence posture and has increased defence spending in recent years, reaching 3.5 % of GDP in 2023 (European Defence Agency, 2024).

Background and Context

Poland’s position as a frontline state in the NATO alliance has made it particularly sensitive to any perceived threats from Russia. Historically, the country was a member of the Warsaw Pact and has since transitioned to a democratic system, joining both the European Union in 2004 and NATO in 1999 (Polish Ministry of Foreign Affairs, 2024). The annexation of Crimea in 2014 and the ongoing conflict in eastern Ukraine have heightened Poland’s security concerns, prompting the government to invest heavily in modernising its armed forces (European Defence Agency, 2024). While the “Polish Shield” programme refers to the U.S. missile‑defence system planned for Poland, the annual “Anakonda” exercise is a large‑scale joint drill that tests the country’s readiness against potential Russian aggression (Polish Ministry of Defence, 2024).

The intelligence that prompted Poland’s warning is said to be based on a combination of open‑source information, signals intelligence, and reports from allied partners. While the specifics of the threat remain classified, the Polish government has stated that the possibility of drone or rocket attacks is a realistic scenario that could be executed with relative ease by Russian forces (Polish Ministry of Defence, 2024‑09‑20). The warning also reflects Russia’s broader strategy of demonstrating its military capabilities and signalling deterrence to NATO members (Reuters, 2024‑09‑20).

The Bigger Picture – Diverse Perspectives

From the perspective of NATO, the Polish warning has prompted a review of the alliance’s deterrence posture in the region. Article 5 of the Washington Treaty, which obligates members to respond collectively to an armed attack, has been invoked in discussions about how best to protect member states that are geographically closer to Russia (NATO, 2024‑09‑21). The alliance has increased its air‑policing missions over the Baltic Sea and has deployed additional Patriot missile batteries to Poland and neighbouring countries (NATO, 2024‑09‑21). Some NATO officials have emphasised that the alliance’s rapid‑response capabilities are designed to deter any hostile actions and reassure member states (NATO, 2024‑09‑21).

Poland’s domestic politics have also come into focus as the government’s hard‑line stance on security has been both praised and criticised. Supporters argue that the increased defence spending and readiness measures are essential to safeguard national sovereignty, while critics raise concerns about the financial burden and the potential for escalating tensions with Russia (The Guardian, 2024‑09‑23). Public sentiment in Poland has largely favoured a strong defence posture, especially in light of the ongoing conflict in Ukraine (European Commission, 2024). The Polish government has used the warning as a rallying point to justify further investment in military capabilities and to strengthen its position within NATO (Polish Ministry of Defence, 2024‑09‑20).

Ukraine’s leadership has welcomed Poland’s warning, viewing it as a sign that NATO allies are taking the threat from Russia seriously. Kyiv’s foreign ministry said that the warning “reinforces the need for continued support for Ukraine’s defence and for a robust deterrence posture in the region” (Ukrainian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, 2024‑09‑20). However, Ukrainian officials also cautioned that any escalation could spill over into the broader region, potentially drawing in more states into a conflict that already strains international security.

Russia’s Response

Russia’s response to the Polish warning has not been publicly detailed. The Kremlin has historically used diplomatic channels and military demonstrations to convey its strategic intentions (Reuters, 2024‑09‑20). In a brief statement, the Russian Ministry of Defence reiterated that it will continue to develop and modernise its deterrent forces, including hypersonic missiles and drone platforms (Russian Ministry of Defence, 2024‑09‑20). Moscow has also increased its own air‑defence drills near the Polish border, citing the need to protect its national security interests (Russian Ministry of Defence, 2024‑09‑20). Russian officials have dismissed the Polish warning as “political posturing” aimed at provoking a response from NATO (Russian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, 2024‑09‑21).

Implications for the Alliance and the Region

The Polish warning underscores the fragility of the security environment in Eastern Europe and the need for heightened vigilance by NATO and its partners. While the threat of drone or rocket strikes remains unverified, the intelligence assessment points to a realistic risk that could be exploited by Russian forces in a conflict scenario. NATO’s rapid‑response capabilities, reinforced by increased air policing and missile‑defence deployments, are designed to deter such aggression, but the situation remains fluid. For Poland, the warning is both a call to action and a political statement that reinforces its commitment to national security and alliance solidarity.

In the coming weeks, NATO is likely to convene a high‑level meeting to assess the threat and to coordinate a possible collective response. The alliance may also consider expanding its presence in the Baltic Sea and strengthening its cyber‑defence posture to counter hybrid threats. Meanwhile, Russia may continue to conduct large‑scale exercises and to showcase its new missile and drone technologies, signalling its willingness to use asymmetric tactics if it perceives a strategic advantage.

Conclusion

Poland’s warning about potential drone or rocket strikes by Russia has amplified concerns about the security of NATO territory and the broader European landscape. The intelligence assessment, while not yet confirmed, highlights a credible threat that could be exploited in a crisis. NATO’s deterrence posture, built on collective defence, rapid response, and advanced missile‑defence systems, remains a cornerstone of regional security. However, the situation remains fluid, and the balance between deterrence and diplomacy will be crucial in preventing a new escalation that could destabilise the broader European security architecture. The warning also serves as a reminder that the alliance’s cohesion and readiness are essential in the face of evolving threats from Russia and other state‑actors.
