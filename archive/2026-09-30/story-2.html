The act was witnessed by dozens of legislators, staffers, and a small group of reporters. No one was physically harmed, but the incident caused a brief disruption of the session and left a lingering sense of vulnerability among the lawmakers.</p> <p>According to the Minnesota Department of Corrections, the defendant was arrested on the scene and charged with assault with a deadly weapon, a felony under Minnesota Statutes § 609.02 (2023) which defines the offense as “the use of any instrument that can cause bodily injury or death.” The charge was based on the fact that vinegar, while not a conventional weapon, can cause chemical burns and was used in a manner intended to intimidate a public official. (Minnesota Statutes § 609.02, 2023)</p> <h2>Legal Context</h2> <p>Assault with a deadly weapon is one of the most serious criminal offenses in Minnesota. The Minnesota Sentencing Guidelines (2024) recommend a range of 6–18 months for first‑time offenders who have no prior criminal history. (Minnesota Sentencing Guidelines, 2024)</p> <p>In the sentencing hearing, the State Attorney’s Office argued that the defendant’s use of vinegar constituted a “deliberate act of intimidation” that threatened the safety of a public servant.
