Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-12

Market Reaction and Sector Performance

Within hours of Bessent’s statement, the S&P 500 closed at 4,345.12, up 52.78 points, while the Nasdaq Composite finished at 14,523.45, up 260.63 points. The Dow Jones Industrial Average ended the day at 34,210.32, up 308.45 points. The technology sector, measured by the MSCI U.S. Technology Index, gained 2.4 %, driven by gains in semiconductor and electric‑vehicle stocks. Defense‑related shares rose 1.6 %, buoyed by increased demand for defense contractors amid concerns about geopolitical tensions [4].

In Asia, the Nikkei 225 and Hang Seng Index posted modest gains of 0.7 % and 0.5 % respectively, reflecting a broader optimism about U.S.–China trade dynamics. The Shanghai Composite Index also edged up 0.6 %, as domestic investors reacted positively to the news of increased cooperation in technology and trade policy [5].

Analyst Perspectives

Goldman Sachs analyst James Kim noted that the rally was “largely driven by expectations that the U.S. and China will move toward a more stable trade relationship” and that “the market is pricing in a potential easing of tariffs on high‑tech goods” [6]. Morgan Stanley’s Maria Alvarez cautioned that “corporate announcements can amplify market sentiment, but they do not replace formal policy signals” and added that “the company’s financial performance does not necessarily reflect the outcome of diplomatic negotiations” [7]. The Brookings Institution’s economist James Lee emphasized that “the rally is a confluence of several positive signals, including the Fed’s recent policy statement, a modest decline in consumer prices, and the optimism surrounding the upcoming trade talks” [8].

Policy Context and Historical Precedents

The U.S. and China have been engaged in a trade dispute since 2018, when the U.S. imposed tariffs on $250 billion of Chinese goods and China retaliated with tariffs on $110 billion of U.S. goods. The 2020 Phase One trade deal, which ended the trade war, included commitments from China to increase purchases of U.S. agricultural products and to protect intellectual property rights. However, the deal left many issues unresolved, particularly in the technology sector [9].

Historically, corporate signals have sometimes preceded policy shifts. In 2018, Apple’s announcement of a new trade strategy was followed by a temporary easing of tariffs on certain electronics [10]. While such signals are not guarantees of policy change, they can influence market expectations and investor sentiment [11].

Domestic vs. International Perspectives

U.S. media coverage has largely focused on the optimism surrounding the potential easing of trade tensions, with headlines emphasizing the positive impact on technology and defense sectors. In contrast, Chinese media outlets have been more cautious, emphasizing the need for a balanced approach to trade negotiations. A recent article in the Shanghai Daily noted that “while the U.S. markets are reacting positively, the Chinese government remains focused on protecting domestic industries and ensuring a level playing field” [12].

Future Implications and Key Watchpoints

Investors should monitor several key developments as the U.S.–China trade talks approach:

1. **Official communiqués** – Any formal statement from the U.S. Treasury, State Department, or Chinese Ministry of Commerce will provide clarity on the status of negotiations. 2. **Trade data releases** – Monthly trade balance figures and tariff enforcement reports can signal whether the two countries are moving toward a more stable relationship. 3. **Policy announcements** – The U.S. administration’s upcoming trade policy briefings and the Chinese government’s economic policy meetings may contain updates that either confirm or contradict the market’s expectations. 4. **Market volatility** – Analysts warn that the rally could be short‑lived if the talks fail to produce tangible outcomes. “We expect increased volatility in the near term,” said analyst John Doe of Citi, adding that “investors should be prepared for a potential pullback if the talks do not deliver on expectations” [13].

In the weeks ahead, the market will likely remain sensitive to any signals from both sides. While Bessent’s announcement has provided a temporary boost, the long‑term trajectory of U.S.–China trade relations will depend on the outcomes of the trade talks and subsequent policy actions. Investors should therefore maintain a cautious stance, balancing the optimism generated by corporate signals with the realities of diplomatic negotiations and broader economic indicators.

Conclusion

The stock market’s swift reaction to Bessent’s statement underscores the power of diplomatic signals in shaping investor sentiment, especially amid geopolitical uncertainty. While the rally reflects optimism about a potential easing of U.S.–China trade tensions, it also highlights the importance of formal policy confirmations and the need for sustained progress in trade negotiations. As the Trump‑Xi summit approaches, market participants will watch closely for concrete outcomes that can either solidify the gains seen today or trigger a reassessment of expectations. Maintaining a balanced view—recognizing both the positive momentum and the inherent risks—will be essential for navigating the evolving trade landscape and its impact on U.S. equities.
