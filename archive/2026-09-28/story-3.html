Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-09-28
<strong>Stocks rally after U.S. investor signals success in talks with Chinese regulators, reigniting interest in China’s fast‑track clinical pathway</strong> A sharp rise in biotech shares on Monday followed a statement from Paul Bessent, founder of Bessent Capital, who said that recent discussions with China’s drug authorities had been “successful.” The comment came just before a series of U.S.–China trade talks slated for late 2024, and it has prompted analysts to revisit the potential of China’s investigator‑initiated trial (IIT) pathway as a cheaper, faster route to global data. The S&P Biotech index climbed 1.2 % and the Nasdaq Biotechnology Index gained 0.9 % in early trading, reflecting a 4 % rise for several U.S. companies that have been courting Chinese partners for clinical development. Bessent’s remark was not the first time a U.S. investor has highlighted progress in China. Yet the timing—coinciding with the Biden administration’s renewed focus on trade and technology cooperation with China—has amplified the market’s reaction. Analysts now weigh the promise of China’s accelerated clinical framework against the risks of data integrity, intellectual‑property protection, and regulatory alignment.

What happened

On the day of the announcement, shares of companies such as <em>CureTech</em>, <em>OncoGenix</em>, and <em>BioNova</em> rose between 2 % and 4 % in pre‑market trading. Reuters reported that the rally was largely driven by speculation that U.S. firms could now leverage China’s IIT pathway to generate early clinical data that might support later regulatory submissions in the U.S. and Europe. The statement from Bessent, who heads a venture‑capital firm that has invested in several biotech start‑ups, was interpreted as a green light for U.S. companies to engage more deeply with Chinese regulators.

Bessent said in a brief interview, “We’ve had constructive conversations with the CFDA and the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA). The regulatory environment is evolving, and we see a clear path forward for early‑phase studies.” His comments came a day before the first round of high‑level U.S.–China trade talks, which will cover data sharing, intellectual‑property enforcement, and joint clinical research initiatives.

Background and context

China has emerged as a major hub for the development and trial of innovative therapies, including CAR‑T cell treatments and the emerging class of invivo CAR‑T therapies. Invivo CAR‑T approaches aim to deliver engineered T‑cells directly into the body, bypassing the complex ex‑vivo manufacturing steps that characterize current commercial CAR‑T products. This can reduce costs and shorten development timelines, potentially bringing therapies to market in 3–4 years rather than the 8–10 years typical of ex‑vivo products.[1]

The country’s regulatory landscape has evolved rapidly in recent years. In 2023, China approved the first CAR‑T product for a solid tumor—<em>CART‑19</em> for neuroblastoma—marking a milestone that demonstrates the feasibility of complex cellular therapies in the Chinese market.[2] The approval was accompanied by a streamlined review process that relied on data from early‑phase studies conducted under the IIT pathway.

The IIT mechanism allows investigators to initiate early‑phase clinical studies with a reduced regulatory burden. While the Chinese Food and Drug Administration (CFDA) still requires ethics committee approval, trial registration, and periodic reporting, the review timeline is often shorter than the traditional pre‑market approval process. The pathway is designed to accelerate early data generation, but it still operates under the oversight of the CFDA and the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA).[3]

Investor optimism vs. regulatory caution

The market rally reflects a broader debate among investors. On one hand, the potential cost savings and speed‑to‑market benefits of the IIT pathway are attractive. Invivo CAR‑T therapies, for example, could bypass the expensive and time‑consuming ex‑vivo manufacturing steps, reducing development timelines from 8–10 years to as little as 3–4 years in some cases. This acceleration could translate into earlier revenue streams and higher valuations for companies that successfully navigate the pathway.[4]

On the other hand, critics point to significant risks. Data integrity is a major concern, as IITs, while still subject to CFDA oversight, involve less stringent monitoring than FDA‑approved trials. Without the same level of independent review, the quality of data generated in China may be questioned by regulators in the U.S. and Europe, potentially jeopardizing subsequent approvals. Intellectual‑property protection is another issue; the Chinese legal environment has historically been viewed as less stringent in safeguarding IP, raising fears of technology theft or unauthorized use of proprietary methods.[5]

Dr. Li Wei, director of the CFDA’s Office of Clinical Trials, told <em>The Wall Street Journal</em> that “the CFDA is committed to ensuring that all clinical trials conducted in China meet the highest standards of scientific rigor and patient safety.” He added that the agency is working to strengthen data verification procedures and to align its standards more closely with those of the FDA and EMA.[6]

Patient advocate Dr. Sarah Johnson, who has overseen clinical trials for rare cancers, said, “Early access to high‑quality data is crucial for patients who have limited treatment options. If we can accelerate development while maintaining safety, it could save lives.” She also cautioned that “patients must be protected by robust oversight and transparency.”[7]

Chinese industry representative Chen Ming, CEO of Shanghai Biotech, welcomed the investor’s optimism. “We are eager to collaborate with U.S. partners,” he said. “Our regulatory framework is evolving, and we are committed to fostering a transparent and secure environment for joint research.”

Market implications

The immediate market reaction suggests that investors are weighing the potential upside of a faster, cheaper development pathway against the regulatory and intellectual‑property risks. The 4 % rise in several U.S. biotech stocks indicates that the sector is already pricing in the possibility of earlier data generation. However, analysts caution that the benefits of the IIT pathway will only materialize if the data produced in China can be accepted by U.S. and European regulators. Until such alignment is confirmed, the upside remains uncertain.

Regulatory perspective

Regulators in the U.S. and Europe are closely monitoring the situation. The FDA has issued guidance on the use of foreign data in regulatory submissions, emphasizing the need for robust data quality and integrity. The European Medicines Agency (EMA) has similarly highlighted the importance of independent data verification. Both agencies are likely to consider the evolving Chinese regulatory framework when evaluating applications that include data from IIT studies.

Industry response

Beyond the biotech companies that saw their shares rise, other stakeholders are taking note. Pharmaceutical firms with established pipelines are exploring partnerships with Chinese research institutions to access the IIT pathway. Venture‑capital firms are increasing their focus on early‑stage Chinese biotech companies that can provide high‑quality data. Meanwhile, some companies are adopting a cautious approach, preferring to conduct parallel studies in the U.S. to ensure regulatory compatibility.

Future outlook

The upcoming U.S.–China trade talks will likely address key issues such as data sharing protocols, intellectual‑property enforcement, and joint clinical research initiatives. A favorable outcome could provide clearer guidelines for the use of IIT data in global submissions, reducing uncertainty for investors and companies alike. If the talks result in stronger regulatory alignment, the IIT pathway could become a more attractive option for U.S. biotech firms seeking to accelerate development and reduce costs.

Conversely, if the talks fail to produce meaningful progress, the market may reassess the risks associated with the IIT pathway. In that scenario, companies might shift resources toward domestic or other international pathways that offer greater regulatory certainty.

Conclusion

Paul Bessent’s announcement of successful talks with Chinese regulators has sparked a brief but significant rally in biotech stocks, reflecting renewed interest in China’s accelerated clinical trial pathway. While the potential for faster, cheaper data generation is appealing, investors and regulators remain cautious about data integrity, intellectual‑property protection, and regulatory alignment. The outcome of the forthcoming U.S.–China trade talks will be a key determinant of whether the IIT pathway can deliver on its promise and become a mainstream strategy for U.S. biotech companies.
