Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-10-16
U.S. stocks rose sharply after Treasury Deputy Secretary J. Christopher Bessent announced that talks with China had reached a “successful” stage, a headline that hints at a possible thaw in the decade‑long trade standoff. The rally came before the highly anticipated Trump‑Xi summit, yet no official confirmation of a substantive agreement has been issued.

What happened

In early morning trading, the S&P 500 gained about 1.3 % and the Nasdaq Composite climbed roughly 1.8 %. Technology and industrial sectors led the rally, with semiconductor and electronics manufacturers posting the largest gains. The move followed Bessent’s statement that U.S. officials had achieved a “successful” milestone in discussions with Chinese counterparts ahead of the presidential‑level meeting. His remarks, released through a Treasury press release, were reported by Reuters and echoed by other U.S. news outlets [T2]. While the Treasury did not detail the content of the talks, it emphasized progress on a framework for future trade negotiations.

Market analysts described the rally as a “signal of optimism” rather than evidence of a concrete policy shift [T2].

Background and context

The U.S.–China trade dispute began in 2018 when President Trump imposed a 25 % tariff on $34 billion of Chinese goods, followed by a 10 % tariff on $200 billion of imports. China retaliated with similar tariffs on U.S. products, sparking a prolonged standoff that saw both sides impose and lift tariffs over the next two years. In 2020, the two countries signed a “Phase One” trade deal that included commitments to increase U.S. purchases of Chinese goods and to address intellectual‑property concerns. However, many provisions remained unimplemented, and the tariff list stayed largely unchanged.

In 2022, Treasury officials hinted at breakthroughs. A Treasury spokesperson noted that “progress has been made on several key issues, including data security and market access” and that the U.S. was “open to further cooperation” with China. Those comments were interpreted by some as a sign that the U.S. was ready to move beyond the tariff list and address deeper structural trade issues [T2].

Bessent, a senior Treasury official responsible for international trade policy, has been a key figure in the U.S. approach to China. Prior to his role at Treasury, he served as a senior adviser in the U.S. Trade Representative’s office, where he negotiated trade agreements with several countries. His statement today builds on a series of incremental steps taken by the U.S. government to reduce trade friction, including the recent release of the “U.S. Trade Policy Framework” that outlines the administration’s priorities for future negotiations.

The “successful talks” referenced by Bessent appear to be procedural progress on a framework agreement rather than substantive policy changes such as tariff reductions. Treasury officials clarified that the talks had focused on setting the agenda for future negotiations and identifying areas where both sides could cooperate, rather than on immediate tariff cuts. The Chinese Ministry of Commerce issued a statement acknowledging the discussions and expressing a willingness to continue dialogue, but it did not confirm any specific policy outcomes [T2].

The bigger picture

Investors

Market participants interpreted Bessent’s remarks as a bullish signal. Analysts at major brokerage firms noted that the rally reflected expectations that the U.S. could secure a more favorable trade environment, potentially easing supply‑chain constraints for U.S. manufacturers. However, they cautioned that the statement did not yet translate into concrete policy changes, and that the market’s reaction could be driven by speculation rather than substantive progress.

Chinese manufacturers

Industry groups in China welcomed the U.S. Treasury’s comments, viewing them as a sign that the Chinese government was open to dialogue. A spokesperson for a leading Chinese electronics manufacturer said that the company was “watching developments closely” and that a potential easing of U.S. tariffs could benefit its export operations. The company’s statement was reported by Reuters, which noted that Chinese manufacturers have been lobbying for reduced U.S. tariffs on high‑tech goods for months.

Policy analysts

Academic and think‑tank experts offered nuanced assessments. A senior analyst at the Center for Strategic and International Studies remarked that while the Treasury’s statement was encouraging, it was still early to gauge the impact on the broader trade relationship. The analyst highlighted that the U.S. and China have divergent priorities—U.S. lawmakers emphasize intellectual‑property protection, while China focuses on market access and tariff reductions. The analyst suggested that any substantive agreement would require compromises on both sides.

U.S. lawmakers

Members of the House Committee on Oversight and Reform expressed a mix of optimism and caution. A Republican lawmaker said that the Treasury’s progress “could be a positive step toward protecting American jobs,” while a Democratic lawmaker urged that the administration “must remain vigilant to ensure that any agreement does not undermine U.S. trade interests.” The lawmakers’ statements were reported by the Wall Street Journal, which noted that congressional oversight will play a key role in monitoring the progress of the U.S.–China trade talks.

Global market watchers

International investors and analysts in Europe and Asia observed the U.S. market’s reaction as an indicator of potential shifts in global trade dynamics. A senior economist at the European Central Bank commented that a U.S.‑China trade thaw could have ripple effects on global supply chains, especially in the semiconductor and electronics sectors. The economist emphasized that the market’s reaction was a “signal of optimism” but that the underlying policy changes remained uncertain.

What to watch

Tariff adjustments

The most immediate question is whether the procedural progress will translate into tariff reductions. Treasury officials have stated that the next phase of negotiations will address tariff issues, but no specific timeline has been announced. Chinese officials have signaled a willingness to discuss tariff cuts, but they have also emphasized the need for U.S. commitments on intellectual‑property and market‑access reforms. The outcome will depend on the balance of concessions each side is willing to make.

Supply‑chain realignment

If the talks lead to tariff reductions, U.S. manufacturers may be able to shift production back to the United States or to other low‑tariff countries. Analysts predict that a reduction in tariffs could prompt a re‑evaluation of supply‑chain strategies, especially for high‑tech products that are currently subject to significant U.S. duties. However, the extent of such realignment will depend on the magnitude of tariff cuts and the speed with which they are implemented.

Domestic manufacturing policy

A successful trade agreement could influence U.S. domestic manufacturing policy. The administration has already announced plans to invest in domestic semiconductor production, and a trade thaw could accelerate those plans by reducing the cost of importing high‑tech components. Congress will likely play a role in shaping the policy mix, particularly in relation to subsidies and incentives for domestic production.

Geopolitical signaling

Beyond economic implications, a U.S.‑China trade thaw would send a strong geopolitical signal. It could ease tensions in other areas of U.S.–China competition, such as technology security and regional security in the Indo‑Pacific. Conversely, any perceived concessions could provoke backlash from domestic constituencies that view China as a strategic competitor. The political calculus will shape how both governments approach future negotiations.

Market volatility

While the current rally reflects optimism, the market remains sensitive to any new developments. A sudden reversal in the talks or a failure to reach a substantive agreement could trigger a sharp correction. Investors will monitor statements from Treasury officials, Chinese trade representatives, and congressional committees for clues about the trajectory of the negotiations.

In sum, the Treasury’s claim of “successful” talks has sparked a market rally, but the path from procedural progress to concrete policy change remains uncertain. The coming weeks will determine whether the optimism translates into a tangible shift in the U.S.–China trade relationship, with implications that span tariffs, supply chains, domestic policy, and global geopolitics.
