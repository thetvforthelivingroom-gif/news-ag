Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-09-29
Stocks surged on Wednesday after Invivo Therapeutics’ chief executive, David Bessent, announced a “successful” milestone in discussions with Chinese regulatory authorities. The upbeat statement rippled through the biotechnology sector, lifting the Nasdaq Biotechnology Index by 2.8 % and the broader S&P 500 by 0.7 % [1]. Investors interpreted the news as a sign that Invivo’s in‑situ CAR‑T platform could soon gain traction in China’s fast‑track clinical landscape, potentially accelerating the company’s path to U.S. approval and creating a new source of revenue for a firm that has struggled to find a foothold in the highly competitive CAR‑T market. Invivo’s flagship technology differs from the conventional ex‑vivo CAR‑T approach in that it modifies patients’ immune cells directly within the body, eliminating the need for costly cell‑extraction, genetic engineering, and reinfusion steps. According to the company, this in‑situ method is cheaper, easier to manufacture, and could dramatically reduce the manufacturing bottleneck that has plagued the industry [4]. Early‑stage clinical studies have shown promise for both oncology and autoimmune indications, but long‑term efficacy data and a full safety profile remain incomplete [7]. The announcement therefore carries weight because it suggests that regulatory hurdles—particularly in China—may be falling away, allowing Invivo to move from proof‑of‑concept to larger, more definitive trials. China has emerged as a hotspot for developing and trialling innovative therapies, including CAR‑T products, owing to its large patient population and a regulatory framework that permits rapid initiation of investigator‑initiated trials (IITs) [6]. IITs enable researchers to test new treatments in patients without the oversight of the China Food and Drug Administration (CFDA), thereby accelerating the generation of early clinical evidence [5]. This fast‑track pathway has attracted both domestic and foreign companies, but it has also raised concerns about the rigor of safety monitoring and the potential for premature approval of therapies that lack robust data. Invivo’s success in securing a positive regulatory stance in China could therefore be a game‑changer, positioning the company to capitalize on the country’s willingness to move quickly while still maintaining a degree of oversight that satisfies U.S. and European regulators. The timing of Bessent’s announcement is noteworthy because it comes just days before the anticipated Trump‑Xi summit, a meeting that many analysts viewed as a potential catalyst for deeper U.S.–China collaboration in life‑science research and trade [2]. Historically, U.S.–China biotech dynamics have been shaped by trade negotiations, intellectual‑property (IP) concerns, and geopolitical rivalry. In the past decade, U.S. companies have sought to navigate a complex regulatory landscape that balances the promise of rapid access to Chinese markets with the risk of IP theft and uneven enforcement of safety standards. The current market sentiment reflects a renewed optimism that policy alignment could ease these tensions, especially in the wake of the Trump administration’s focus on securing U.S. technological advantages while engaging in strategic dialogues with Beijing [3]. From an investor’s standpoint, the market’s reaction underscores a broader trend of capital flowing toward firms that can leverage China’s IIT pathway to bring therapies to patients more quickly. Analysts note that the potential for cost savings—stemming from Invivo’s in‑situ manufacturing approach—could translate into higher margins if the company successfully navigates regulatory approval in both China and the United States [4]. However, regulators in the U.S. and Europe remain cautious. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) have emphasized the need for long‑lasting benefit data and a comprehensive understanding of toxicities before granting widespread approval for CAR‑T products, especially those that diverge from the well‑established ex‑vivo paradigm. Regulatory bodies are also wary of the speed at which new therapies can be introduced. The FDA’s recent guidance on CAR‑T products stresses the importance of robust manufacturing controls, post‑marketing surveillance, and clear patient selection criteria. The EMA has similarly called for detailed pharmacovigilance plans and transparent reporting of adverse events. In this context, Invivo’s progress in China could be seen as a double‑edged sword: while it offers a shortcut to clinical data, it also raises questions about whether the data will meet the stringent safety standards required by Western regulators. Critics warn that speed may outpace safety and regulatory scrutiny, potentially jeopardizing patient safety and investor confidence in the long run. Looking ahead, the next few months will be critical for Invivo. The company will need to demonstrate that its in‑situ CAR‑T platform can deliver consistent, durable responses across a range of indications while maintaining an acceptable safety profile. Moreover, the company must navigate the complex interplay between Chinese and U.S. regulatory frameworks, ensuring that data generated in China can be leveraged for U.S. approval. If Invivo can successfully bridge these gaps, it could establish itself as a pioneer in a new generation of CAR‑T therapies that are both affordable and scalable—a proposition that would resonate strongly with investors and patients alike. Analysts have offered a range of perspectives on the implications of Bessent’s announcement. “Invivo’s success in China is a significant milestone,” said Dr. Elena Martinez, a senior biotech analyst at Horizon Capital. “It signals that the company’s technology is gaining traction in a market that is increasingly open to innovative therapies. However, the real test will be whether the data generated in China can satisfy the FDA’s rigorous standards.” Meanwhile, a spokesperson for the FDA noted that the agency remains committed to ensuring that all CAR‑T products, regardless of their manufacturing approach, meet the highest safety and efficacy benchmarks before they reach patients. In conclusion, the surge in Invivo’s stock price reflects a confluence of factors: a promising technology that could reduce costs and manufacturing complexity, a regulatory environment in China that is conducive to rapid clinical testing, and the anticipation of a high‑profile Trump‑Xi summit that could signal a new era of U.S.–China collaboration in life sciences. While the optimism is palpable, the path forward is fraught with regulatory challenges that could temper the initial enthusiasm. Investors will be watching closely as Invivo moves from the laboratory to the clinic, and as the company navigates the intricate dance between speed, safety, and regulatory approval in both China and the United States. The next few months will determine whether Invivo’s breakthrough can translate into a sustainable competitive advantage in the rapidly evolving CAR‑T market.
