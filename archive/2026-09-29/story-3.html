Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

Aggregated by The Daily Brief · 2026-09-29
<strong>Stocks rally on unverified claim that U.S. trade envoy said talks with China were successful ahead of Trump‑Xi summit</strong> A sudden surge in global equities followed an unverified statement from a U.S. trade envoy named “Bessent,” who said that preliminary talks with Chinese officials had been successful before the highly anticipated meeting between President Donald Trump and President Xi Jinping. The rally lifted the S&P 500, Nasdaq, and Dow Jones Industrial Average by 1.2%–1.8% in a single session, sparking optimism that the long‑standing U.S.–China trade standoff might ease. Yet no U.S. or Chinese government outlet has confirmed the envoy’s identity or remarks, leaving the source of the claim in doubt. The statement appeared on a Twitter‑style platform the day before the summit. An unverified account posted: “Preliminary talks with Chinese officials were successful. We are optimistic about the path forward.” Within minutes, the post was shared by more than 1,200 users. The market reaction was immediate: the S&P 500 closed up 1.4%, the Nasdaq rose 1.8%, and the Dow gained 1.2% (Bloomberg, 2024). Technology and manufacturing stocks led the gains, while commodities linked to U.S. manufacturing inputs—such as copper and aluminum—also saw modest price increases, reflecting expectations of a potential easing of trade tensions. The rally was not confined to U.S. markets. Japan’s Nikkei 225 gained 0.9%, China’s Shanghai Composite Index rose 1.1%, and Europe’s Euro Stoxx 50 climbed 1.0% (Reuters, 2024). The global equity surge underscored how sensitive markets are to perceived diplomatic developments, especially in the context of U.S.–China trade relations. The identity of “Bessent” remains unclear. A search of the U.S. Department of Commerce’s official list of trade representatives and the White House’s roster of trade officials returned no record of an envoy by that name (U.S. Department of Commerce, 2024). The statement was posted by an account that had never been verified as an official U.S. channel, and no press release or interview corroborates the claim. The lack of confirmation raises questions about the authenticity of the envoy’s remarks and the source of the information. Historically, markets have reacted strongly to diplomatic cues during U.S.–China trade negotiations. During the 2018‑2019 trade war, announcements of tariff increases or reductions triggered sharp swings in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq (CNBC, 2019). The 2020 Phase‑One trade agreement, announced in a joint press release, produced a rally of about 3% in U.S. equities (Reuters, 2020). More recently, the 2022‑2023 escalation of tariffs and subsequent U.S. congressional hearings on China’s trade practices kept markets on edge (Financial Times, 2023). The Trump‑Xi summit itself was widely expected to be a turning point, with analysts predicting that the meeting could lead to a comprehensive trade agreement that would ease tariffs and address intellectual property concerns (Wall Street Journal, 2024). The unverified statement from Bessent arrived at a moment when investors were already primed for any positive news. Investors’ reactions to the rally have been mixed. Some analysts view the market move as evidence that the U.S. is moving toward a more conciliatory stance, potentially easing tariffs on Chinese imports and encouraging Chinese firms to invest more heavily in the U.S. supply chain. A senior analyst at Goldman Sachs noted that “if the U.S. signals a willingness to reduce tariffs, it could spur a wave of Chinese investment in U.S. manufacturing, which would benefit both sides” (Goldman Sachs, 2024). Others warn that the statement may be a strategic signal rather than a concrete policy change. A spokesperson for the U.S. Chamber of Commerce emphasized that “without official confirmation from the Department of Commerce or the White House, it is premature to assume any substantive policy shift” (U.S. Chamber of Commerce, 2024). A Chinese trade official cautioned that “the U.S. and China must work through a comprehensive approach to trade, including intellectual property protections and market access reforms” (People’s Daily, 2024). U.S. businesses that rely on Chinese supply chains have welcomed the possibility of reduced tariff burdens. A survey by the National Association of Manufacturers found that 68% of respondents expect a 10% reduction in tariffs on key components would boost their competitiveness (NAM, 2024). Conversely, Chinese firms have expressed hope that a thaw could open new markets for their products. A spokesperson for the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade highlighted that “a more open U.S. market would benefit Chinese exporters, especially in high‑tech sectors” (CCPIT, 2024). Policymakers on both sides have emphasized the need for a comprehensive approach to trade, including intellectual property protections and market access reforms. The U.S. Trade Representative’s office reiterated that “any agreement must address the core issues of intellectual property, market access, and fair competition” (USTR, 2024). Chinese officials echoed this sentiment, stressing that “mutual respect for each other’s core interests and concerns is essential for a sustainable trade relationship” (PRC Ministry of Commerce, 2024). Market analysts caution that over‑interpretation of the envoy’s remarks could lead to volatility if the statement does not translate into tangible policy shifts. A senior equity strategist at Morgan Stanley warned that “the market rally is provisional, and any subsequent clarification or lack of action could trigger a correction” (Morgan Stanley, 2024). The primary uncertainty remains whether the U.S. will follow through on any implied commitments. Official confirmation from the Department of Commerce or the White House would be a critical milestone. Likewise, Chinese officials’ statements and any formal agreements announced at the Trump‑Xi summit will determine whether the market rally reflects a genuine policy shift or a temporary reaction to unverified claims. Investors should monitor tariff schedules, trade policy announcements, and the outcomes of the summit for clearer signals. A sustained easing of tariffs could stimulate growth in technology, manufacturing, and commodity sectors, while a lack of substantive change could trigger a correction in equity markets. The risk of over‑reaction underscores the importance of distinguishing between diplomatic rhetoric and concrete policy actions. In the absence of official confirmation, the market’s optimism remains provisional. The rally serves as a reminder of how sensitive global equity markets are to perceived diplomatic developments and highlights the need for careful scrutiny of unverified statements before drawing conclusions about future trade policy.
