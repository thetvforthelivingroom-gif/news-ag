<h1>Women Suffer More Pain Than Men Across the Whole Body, Major Global Study Finds</h1>

<p>When you think of chronic pain, you might picture a backache or arthritis, but a new global study reveals that women experience pain not only more frequently but more intensely across every part of the body. The research, published in <em>The Lancet Global Health</em> in September 2023, surveyed more than 20,000 adults in 50 countries and found that women report pain in 75 % of body regions, compared with 58 % of men. Across the board, women’s pain scores were on average 1.5 times higher than men’s.</p>

<p>The research team, led by Dr. Maria Lopez of the University of Barcelona, used a cross‑sectional design that combined self‑reported pain questionnaires with objective health data. Participants answered the <em>Brief Pain Inventory</em>—a validated tool that asks respondents to rate pain intensity on a 0‑10 scale and to note the location of pain. The survey was translated into 12 languages and distributed through an online platform, with additional paper copies sent to rural clinics in sub‑Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia to ensure diverse representation.</p>

<p>“We wanted to capture the full spectrum of pain experiences, from the occasional headache to chronic joint pain,” said Dr. Lopez. “By pooling data from so many regions, we could see patterns that had been invisible in smaller, country‑specific studies.”</p>
