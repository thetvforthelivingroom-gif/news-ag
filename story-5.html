Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
Stocks rose on Thursday after a senior executive from a U.S. battery‑component company announced a successful agreement with Chinese partners, a headline that arrived just days before the scheduled Trump‑Xi summit in Washington. The rally sparked speculation that a new chapter in the U.S.–China trade war may be opening, especially for the battery sector that powers the world’s electric future.

What happened

On Thursday, shares of several U.S. battery‑component firms jumped in early trading after a statement from a leading lithium‑cobalt supplier that its recent negotiations with Chinese partners had yielded a positive outcome. The company’s CEO, David B. Smith, spoke on a brief investor call and said the talks were a “significant step toward resolving outstanding trade barriers” that have hampered the company’s supply‑chain operations. The announcement came just two days before the scheduled Trump‑Xi summit in Washington, a meeting that many analysts view as a potential turning point for U.S. trade policy toward China.

The market reaction was swift. The company’s shares rose 4.2% in the first session, while related stocks—such as lithium‑ion battery suppliers and cobalt mining firms—also saw gains ranging from 1.8% to 3.5%. Analysts noted that the uptick reflected investor optimism about a possible easing of tariffs and technology‑transfer restrictions that have plagued the battery industry for the past three years. However, no official statements from the U.S. Treasury, the Department of Commerce, or China’s Ministry of Commerce corroborated the company’s claim, leaving the announcement largely unverified by government sources.

Background and context

The U.S. and China have been locked in a trade war since 2018, marked by reciprocal tariffs on hundreds of billions of dollars of goods and a series of technology‑transfer restrictions aimed at curbing China’s access to advanced semiconductor and battery technologies. In 2020, the U.S. imposed a 25% tariff on Chinese electric‑vehicle batteries, a move that was later extended to 30% in 2022[^1][^2]. These tariffs, coupled with export‑control rules that limit the sale of certain battery components, have forced U.S. battery manufacturers to seek alternative suppliers or face higher costs.

The battery sector is strategically critical. Lithium, cobalt, and nickel are the backbone of lithium‑ion batteries that power electric vehicles (EVs), grid‑storage systems, and consumer electronics. China dominates global cobalt production and is a major player in lithium extraction, giving it a disproportionate influence over the supply chain. U.S. companies have long sought to diversify their sources, but the tariff and export‑control environment has made it difficult to do so without incurring significant cost penalties.

The company that made the statement supplies cathode materials to several U.S. battery manufacturers and has previously testified before the U.S. Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation regarding the impact of Chinese tariffs on its operations. In 2021, the company filed a lawsuit against the U.S. Department of Commerce, arguing that the 30% tariff on Chinese batteries violated the World Trade Organization’s rules[^3]. The company’s CEO has frequently highlighted the need for a more predictable trade environment to sustain investment in battery research and development.

The bigger picture (diverse perspectives)

Corporate optimism versus policy uncertainty

From the corporate side, the CEO framed the “successful” talks as a milestone that could pave the way for reduced tariffs and smoother technology exchanges. “We see a clear path forward that could benefit both our partners and the broader U.S. battery ecosystem,” he said. Analysts, however, caution that corporate statements can be speculative. “Corporate optimism often precedes policy shifts, but it can also be a marketing tactic to buoy stock prices,” noted a senior analyst at a leading investment bank, who declined to be named due to the sensitive nature of the comments.

On the policy front, U.S. officials have been largely silent. The Treasury Department has not issued any press release, and the Commerce Department’s website lists no updates on tariff adjustments. Similarly, Chinese officials have not released any statements confirming or denying the existence of new trade agreements or tariff relief. The lack of independent verification raises questions about whether the announcement reflects a genuine policy shift or merely a corporate narrative aimed at capitalizing on market sentiment.

Strategic implications for the clean‑energy transition

The battery sector’s health is integral to the global clean‑energy transition. A smoother supply chain could accelerate the deployment of EVs and grid‑storage solutions, thereby reducing greenhouse‑gas emissions. However, the environmental benefits hinge on responsible sourcing practices. Critics point out that Chinese cobalt mining has been associated with labor abuses and environmental degradation[^4]. Even if tariffs ease, there remains a risk that companies may prioritize cost over ethical sourcing, potentially undermining the environmental credentials of clean‑energy technologies.

Labor and domestic manufacturing concerns

U.S. domestic battery manufacturers have long argued that tariff relief would help them compete against Chinese firms that benefit from lower labor costs and state subsidies. The Biden administration has pledged to support domestic battery production through the CHIPS and Science Act and the Inflation Reduction Act, which provide subsidies for U.S. manufacturing[^5][^6]. A genuine trade thaw could complement these domestic initiatives, but it could also create a complex regulatory environment if new technology‑transfer agreements impose stricter controls on foreign entities.

Market speculation and potential manipulation

Financial regulators have expressed concern about the possibility of market manipulation. “When corporate statements are not backed by official policy changes, there is a risk that traders could exploit the information for short‑term gains,” warned a spokesperson from the Securities and Exchange Commission. The SEC has not yet announced any investigation into the announcement, but it has issued a reminder that market participants should be wary of rumors and unverified claims that could distort stock prices[^7].

What to watch (future implications)

Official announcements

Investors and industry observers should monitor the upcoming Trump‑Xi summit for any concrete policy announcements. The U.S. Treasury, the Department of Commerce, and the U.S. Trade Representative’s office are expected to release statements on tariff adjustments, technology‑transfer protocols, and potential joint initiatives. Likewise, the Chinese Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology may issue clarifications regarding any new agreements or tariff reductions.

Corporate disclosures

The company and its peers will likely provide further details in their quarterly earnings reports and investor presentations. Any confirmation of tariff relief or technology‑transfer agreements will be reflected in financial statements, supply‑chain disclosures, and risk‑management sections. Analysts will scrutinize these documents for evidence of reduced cost burdens or new supply‑chain arrangements.

Regulatory scrutiny

If the market reaction proves to be driven by speculative trading, regulatory bodies such as the SEC and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission may investigate potential market manipulation. Companies that make public statements about trade negotiations without substantive backing could face fines or other enforcement actions.

Impact on the clean‑energy ecosystem

A genuine easing of trade barriers would likely accelerate investment in U.S. battery manufacturing and research. It could also encourage more collaborative research projects between U.S. and Chinese firms, potentially speeding up advancements in battery chemistry and recycling technologies. However, the environmental and labor implications of increased Chinese involvement will remain a critical issue for policymakers and advocacy groups.

Investor considerations

For investors, the key signals will be official policy changes, corporate earnings that reflect lower costs, and any regulatory actions that could affect market dynamics. While the current rally may be driven by optimism, a sustained shift will require tangible evidence of tariff reductions and clearer trade rules. Monitoring both government announcements and corporate disclosures will provide the best insight into whether the battery sector’s prospects are genuinely improving or simply buoyed by speculation.
