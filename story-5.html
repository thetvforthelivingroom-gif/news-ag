Poland has warned that Russia is preparing drone and rocket strikes on NATO territory, a claim that has reignited tensions across the alliance and prompted a swift reassessment of air‑defence protocols in the region. The statement, issued by Polish officials on Tuesday, signals a new phase in the escalating security environment that has defined Eastern Europe since Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine. It comes amid a broader pattern of increased Russian military activity near Poland’s borders, raising the spectre of a fresh escalation of hostilities. Polish authorities say Russian forces have begun deploying unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and short‑range rocket systems in ways that could threaten NATO installations within Poland’s territory. The warning followed intelligence reports that identified a series of Russian drone flights that entered Polish airspace without prior notification, prompting concerns that the aircraft could be used for targeted strikes. The defence ministry highlighted that the threat could extend to both military bases and civilian infrastructure, underscoring the potential for significant damage if the warning is not taken seriously [T2]. In addition to drone activity, Polish officials warned that Russia could also employ rocket‑based weaponry against NATO positions. The ministry’s statement noted that recent Russian exercises in the Baltic Sea included the launch of short‑range rockets, which could be directed toward strategic sites within Poland. While Russia has not issued a direct response to the warning, NATO’s spokesperson confirmed that the alliance is monitoring the situation closely and has increased its air surveillance in the region [T3]. The backdrop to this warning is a long‑standing pattern of Russian manoeuvres that have repeatedly tested the limits of NATO’s collective defence. Since the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, Russia has conducted large‑scale exercises near the borders of several NATO members, including Poland, Belarus, and the Baltic states. These drills often involve the deployment of air‑defence systems and the movement of aircraft that could be used for offensive operations. The Polish warning is part of a broader effort by NATO members to maintain a high level of readiness in the face of evolving threats from Russia [T1]. Poland’s strategic position on the front line of NATO’s eastern flank makes it a focal point for both deterrence and defence. The country has invested heavily in modernising its air‑defence capabilities, including the procurement of advanced missile systems such as the Patriot PAC‑2 and the SAMP/T, and the integration of real‑time surveillance networks that link Polish radar sites with those of neighbouring allies. The recent warning is consistent with Poland’s broader security policy, which emphasises the need for robust defensive measures against potential Russian aggression. The Polish government has also called for increased cooperation with NATO allies to ensure a coordinated response to any escalation [T1]. The broader implications of Poland’s warning extend beyond the immediate security concerns. For Russia, the deployment of drones and rocket systems near NATO territory could be seen as a demonstration of military capability and a test of the alliance’s response mechanisms. For NATO, the warning underscores the importance of maintaining a unified stance and the potential need to adjust force posture in the region. The situation also highlights the delicate balance between deterrence and provocation in a highly volatile security environment [T3]. Poland’s statement has prompted a range of reactions from other NATO members. Some officials have expressed support for the warning, emphasising the need for heightened vigilance and rapid information sharing. Others have cautioned against over‑reaction, noting that misinterpretation of military exercises could lead to unintended escalation. The European Union has urged both sides to engage in diplomatic dialogue to prevent a further deterioration of security conditions in the region [T3]. Looking ahead, the immediate priority for NATO will be to enhance air‑defence coordination and ensure that all member states are prepared for potential drone or rocket incursions.
