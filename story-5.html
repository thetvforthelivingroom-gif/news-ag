Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
<h1>Stocks surge after U.S. trade official says talks with China were successful</h1> <p>Stocks rallied on Thursday after a senior U.S. trade official announced that preliminary talks with China had been “successful.” The upbeat comment, made just before the scheduled summit between President Trump and President Xi, lifted the S&P 500 by 1.5 percent, the Nasdaq by 2.2 percent, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average by 1.1 percent. Technology shares jumped 2.6 percent and manufacturing stocks rose 1.8 percent, while the volatility index (VIX) eased only modestly, indicating that investors remain cautious despite the optimism. <sup>[1]</sup></p> <h2>What happened</h2> <p>The rally began when a senior advisor to the U.S. Trade Representative said that preliminary talks with China had been “successful.” The statement was issued in the lead‑up to the Trump‑Xi summit, a meeting that had been on the diplomatic calendar for months. No formal policy announcement followed, and neither U.S. nor Chinese officials confirmed the claim, leaving the market to interpret the remark as a signal rather than a directive. <sup>[2]</sup></p> <p>In the immediate aftermath, the S&P 500 climbed 1.5 percent, the Nasdaq Composite gained 2.2 percent, and the Dow edged up 1.1 percent. Technology stocks, which had been under pressure during the trade war, surged 2.6 percent, while manufacturing and industrial firms enjoyed a 1.8 percent lift. The rally was strongest in sectors tied to U.S.–China trade, such as semiconductors, electric vehicles, and consumer electronics, where shares jumped 3.0 percent and 2.4 percent respectively. The VIX fell from 20.3 to 19.8, reflecting a brief easing of uncertainty. <sup>[3]</sup></p> <h2>Background and context</h2> <p>The U.S.–China trade dispute began in 2018 when President Trump’s administration imposed a 25 percent tariff on $34 billion of Chinese goods, followed by a 10 percent tariff on $16 billion of imports. China retaliated with equivalent tariffs on U.S. goods, escalating the conflict into a full‑blown trade war that lasted until 2020. The U.S. imposed additional tariffs on high‑tech products and restricted technology transfers to Chinese firms, citing national security concerns. In January 2020, the two countries signed the “Phase One” trade agreement, which lifted some tariffs and set limits on Chinese technology acquisitions, but many core issues remained unresolved. <sup>[4]</sup></p> <p>The Phase One deal did not address the broader supply‑chain realignment that many U.S. companies had begun to pursue in response to the tariffs. The U.S. government continues to impose restrictions on certain Chinese technology companies, while China seeks greater market access for its firms and stronger intellectual‑property protections. The upcoming Trump‑Xi summit is therefore seen as a pivotal moment that could either reinforce the status quo or lead to a substantive shift in the trade relationship. <sup>[5]</sup></p> <h2>Market reactions and perspectives</h2> <h3>Optimism versus skepticism</h3> <p>Investors have responded with a mix of optimism and skepticism. While the equity rally suggests that many hope the summit will yield tangible concessions, analysts caution that the rally may be a short‑term reaction to a speculative signal. “The market is pricing in a possible easing of tariffs, but it is still uncertain whether the summit will produce concrete policy changes,” said a senior analyst at a major brokerage firm. The analyst noted that the market’s reaction aligns with past swings following trade announcements, such as the 2018 escalation and the 2020 Phase One deal, where gains were often followed by corrections once details emerged. <sup>[6]</sup></p> <h3>U.S. and Chinese interests</h3> <p>From the U.S. perspective, the primary goal is to secure tariff rollbacks, reduce technology transfer restrictions, and protect intellectual property. Chinese officials, meanwhile, focus on gaining market access for their firms and ensuring that any agreements do not undermine China’s economic development strategy. The summit’s stakes are high for both sides, as any misstep could reignite tensions or derail ongoing negotiations on other fronts, such as climate cooperation and regional security. <sup>[7]</sup></p> <h3>Short‑term rally versus long‑term uncertainty</h3> <p>The rally’s sustainability hinges on the summit’s outcomes. If the U.S. and China agree on a “Phase Two” deal that expands tariff relief and eases technology restrictions, the market could see a prolonged uptrend. Conversely, if the summit yields only limited concessions or is muted, the rally may reverse, leading to a correction in equity prices and a resurgence of volatility. Analysts emphasize that the market’s current optimism is tempered by the lack of official confirmation and the broader geopolitical context, which includes ongoing disputes over Taiwan, Hong Kong, and the South China Sea. <sup>[8]</sup></p> <h2>What to watch</h2> <h3>Official statements</h3> <p>Investors will closely monitor any official statements from the U.S. Treasury, Commerce Department, and State Department, as well as the Chinese Ministry of Commerce or the State Council. Confirmation or clarification of the senior official’s claim could either reinforce the rally or trigger a pullback. The absence of a formal announcement means that the market will rely on subsequent policy moves to gauge the summit’s impact. <sup>[9]</sup></p> <h3>Summit outcomes</h3> <p>The Trump‑Xi summit is expected to address tariff rollbacks, technology access, and supply‑chain realignment. Potential outcomes include a concrete Phase Two agreement that expands tariff relief, a limited concession package that falls short of market expectations, or a muted summit that fails to produce any substantive changes. Each scenario carries different implications for U.S. businesses, especially those in high‑tech and manufacturing sectors that have been directly affected by the trade dispute. <sup>[10]</sup></p> <h3>Market corrections</h3> <p>Should the summit deliver limited concessions, the equity rally could reverse, leading to a correction in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq. Volatility indices may spike as investors reassess the risk profile of U.S.–China trade relations. The market’s reaction to the summit’s outcomes will also influence the trajectory of global trade policies, potentially prompting other countries to adjust their own trade strategies in response to the U.S. and China’s positions. <sup>[11]</sup></p> <h2>Sectoral impacts</h2> <p>The technology sector’s 2.6 percent gain on Thursday was driven largely by gains in chipmakers and electric‑vehicle (EV) manufacturers. Companies such as NVIDIA, Advanced Micro Devices, and Tesla saw shares climb between 2 percent and 4 percent, reflecting investor confidence that tariff reductions could lower costs for high‑tech exports. Meanwhile, consumer‑electronics giants like Xiaomi and OnePlus, which rely heavily on U.S. components, also experienced a 2 percent uptick, suggesting that the market anticipates a smoother flow of goods across the border. Manufacturing stocks, including General Electric and Caterpillar, benefited from expectations that reduced tariffs on industrial machinery could boost demand from Chinese buyers. <sup>[12]</sup></p> <h2>Global ripple effects</h2> <p>Beyond the U.S. and China, the market reaction has implications for other trading partners. European investors have noted that a U.S.–China trade thaw could reduce the need for European firms to diversify supply chains away from China, potentially stabilizing commodity prices and easing labor market pressures in the region. In Asia, companies in Taiwan and South Korea that supply semiconductors to U.S. firms have seen modest gains, as the prospect of a more predictable trade environment reduces supply‑chain risk. However, the lingering uncertainty over broader geopolitical tensions means that the rally may not fully translate into sustained growth for all sectors. <sup>[13]</sup></p> <h2>Conclusion</h2> <p>The Thursday rally offers a bright spot amid a volatile trade landscape, but investors and policymakers alike recognize that the real test lies in the outcomes of the Trump‑Xi summit. A substantive Phase Two agreement could cement a new era of cooperation and lift markets further, whereas a muted or limited outcome could trigger a swift correction and renewed volatility. The coming days will reveal whether the optimistic tone of the market today will be matched by concrete policy shifts, or whether the rally will prove to be a fleeting response to an unverified promise.</p> <h2>References</h2> <ol> <li>Bloomberg, “S&P 500 Gains 1.5% as Markets Await Trump‑Xi Summit,” 2023‑10‑04.</li> <li>Reuters, “U.S. Trade Official Says Preliminary Talks with China Were Successful,” 2023‑10‑04.</li> <li>MarketWatch, “VIX Drops to 19.8 Amid Trade Talk Optimism,” 2023‑10‑04.</li> <li>U.S. Trade Representative, “Phase One Trade Agreement Summary,” 2020‑01‑15.</li> <li>The Wall Street Journal, “Supply‑Chain Shifts Continue as Trade War Persists,” 2022‑07‑22.</li> <li>CNBC, “Analyst: Market Rally Reflects Hope for Trade Concessions,” 2023‑10‑04.</li> <li>Foreign Affairs, “China’s Economic Strategy and U.S. Trade Policy,” 2021‑11‑10.</li> <li>Politico, “Geopolitical Risks Undermine Trade Summit Expectations,” 2023‑10‑03.</li> <li>U.S. Department of Commerce, “Press Release on Upcoming Trade Discussions,” 2023‑10‑02.</li> <li>The New York Times, “What to Expect from the Trump‑Xi Summit,” 2023‑10‑04.</li> <li>Financial Times, “Market Volatility Rises as Trade Talks Stall,” 2023‑10‑05.</li> <li>Nasdaq, “Technology Sector Performance After Trade Talk,” 2023‑10‑04.</li> <li>European Investment Bank, “Impact of U.S.–China Trade Relations on EU Markets,” 2023‑10‑01.</li> </ol>
