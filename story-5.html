Policy Context and Historical Precedents

The U.S. and China have been engaged in a trade dispute since 2018, when the U.S. imposed tariffs on $250 billion of Chinese goods and China retaliated with tariffs on $110 billion of U.S. goods. The 2020 Phase One trade deal, which ended the trade war, included commitments from China to increase purchases of U.S. agricultural products and to protect intellectual property rights. However, the deal left many issues unresolved, particularly in the technology sector [9].

Historically, corporate signals have sometimes preceded policy shifts. In 2018, Apple’s announcement of a new trade strategy was followed by a temporary easing of tariffs on certain electronics [10]. While such signals are not guarantees of policy change, they can influence market expectations and investor sentiment [11].
