Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
<article> **Stocks rally after NXP claims “successful talks” with China, hinting at a possible thaw in U.S.–China trade tensions** A brief statement from semiconductor maker NXP Semiconductors sent a ripple through the technology sector, lifting the Nasdaq Composite by 0.6% and pushing NXP’s shares up 2.5% in pre‑market trading. The move came just days before a scheduled U.S.–China trade and technology dialogue in Washington, D.C., raising hopes that the long‑standing tariff and technology‑access standoff could ease modestly. While the company’s claim has yet to be confirmed by officials on either side, market participants have already priced in a potential shift in policy, underscoring the high stakes investors attach to any hint of a change in the trade war’s trajectory. ---

What happened

On Thursday morning, NXP’s chief executive officer, Jeroen van der Veer, posted a brief note on the company’s investor‑relations portal. He said the firm had entered into “successful talks” with Chinese partners about supply‑chain coordination and tariff issues that affect the company’s business in China and the United States. The announcement triggered an immediate market response: NXP’s shares rose 2.5% in pre‑market trading, while the broader S&P 500 gained 0.4% and the Nasdaq Composite climbed 0.6% on the day of the announcement [1]. Other technology‑component stocks, including Infineon Technologies and Texas Instruments, also posted gains of 1–2% as investors interpreted the NXP statement as a potential harbinger of easing trade tensions. The rally was concentrated in the first half of the trading day, with the technology‑component index gaining 1.2% before settling back near its opening level by late afternoon.

The timing of the announcement is notable. The U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce are scheduled to meet on September 25, 2024, to discuss tariff schedules, technology‑access restrictions, and supply‑chain resilience. NXP’s claim arrived just hours before the opening remarks of the talks, amplifying its perceived significance. While the company’s statement has not yet been corroborated by officials in Washington or Beijing, the market’s immediate reaction underscores the high stakes investors attach to any hint of policy change.

Background and context

The U.S.–China trade war began in 2018 when President Trump imposed a 25 % tariff on $50 billion of Chinese goods, followed by a 10 % tariff on an additional $200 billion of imports. China retaliated with equivalent tariffs on U.S. products, and the dispute quickly expanded beyond tariffs to encompass technology access restrictions. In 2019, the U.S. government added Huawei and other Chinese firms to its Entity List, effectively barring them from accessing U.S. technology without a special license. Subsequent rounds of sanctions targeted semiconductor equipment and software, deepening supply‑chain fragmentation [2].

Earlier U.S.–China summits have had mixed market impacts. The 2019 trade talks in Washington ended with a partial agreement that temporarily eased some tariff provisions, prompting a 0.8% rise in the S&P 500 and a 1.5% jump in technology stocks [3]. In 2021, a second summit in Beijing failed to produce a comprehensive deal; the market responded with a 0.4% decline in the Nasdaq and a 0.6% drop in semiconductor shares, reflecting uncertainty over the pace of tariff reductions and technology restrictions [4]. These historical episodes illustrate how even tentative signals can sway investor sentiment, especially in sectors directly affected by trade policy.

NXP’s business profile places it squarely in the cross‑hairs of these policy shifts. The company designs and manufactures high‑precision electronic components used in smartphones, automotive electronics, and industrial automation. Its supply chain is heavily integrated with Chinese foundries and component suppliers, exposing it to both tariff costs and technology‑access constraints. In 2023, NXP disclosed that tariffs had increased its cost of goods sold by 3.2%, prompting a 2% decline in its 2024 earnings forecast [5]. The company has previously warned that further tariff escalations could erode margins, underscoring its sensitivity to U.S.–China trade dynamics.

Chinese viewpoint

A senior official at the Chinese Ministry of Commerce, speaking on condition of anonymity, said the government remains open to dialogue but stresses the importance of protecting national security interests and maintaining a stable supply chain. “We are willing to engage in constructive discussions, but any agreement must respect China’s sovereignty and technological development needs,” the official said [6]. A leading analyst at China’s National Development and Reform Commission, Li Wei, added that China is seeking a “balanced approach” that allows for continued market‑based innovation while safeguarding critical technologies. “The Chinese side is prepared to negotiate on tariff reductions, but we will not compromise on core technology controls,” Li said [7].

Analyst perspectives

Analysts have offered divergent views on the implications of NXP’s claim. Some see it as a credible indicator that the U.S. government may be willing to roll back certain tariff provisions or ease technology restrictions, especially given the broader U.S. strategy to curb China’s technological ascent. “If the U.S.–China talks confirm a shift in policy, we could see a ripple effect across the entire semiconductor supply chain,” said Michael Chen, senior analyst at Morgan Stanley. Chen cited NXP’s exposure to Chinese foundries and the potential for reduced tariff costs as key drivers of the rally [8].

Others caution that the statement could be a marketing move designed to buoy the company’s stock ahead of the talks, especially in the absence of any official confirmation. “Until we see concrete policy changes, the market risk remains high,” warned Sarah Patel, risk‑management specialist at Goldman Sachs. Patel highlighted the volatility that has plagued tech stocks during the trade war, noting that “the market has historically overreacted to speculative signals” [9].

The current rally in tech‑component stocks suggests that a sizable portion of the market is already pricing in a potential easing of tensions, even if the evidence remains indirect.

Implications for the supply chain and policy

A relaxation of tariffs could reduce costs for U.S. manufacturers, potentially improving profitability for companies like NXP and their downstream customers. Conversely, a hardening of technology restrictions could exacerbate supply‑chain disruptions, particularly for firms reliant on advanced semiconductor equipment. The U.S. administration has indicated that it is prepared to negotiate on trade and technology, while Chinese officials have emphasized the importance of market‑based solutions and the need to protect national security interests.

The upcoming U.S.–China trade and technology dialogue is therefore a focal point for investors, policymakers, and industry stakeholders alike. The USTR’s office has outlined a framework that includes tariff schedules, technology‑access controls, and mechanisms for resolving disputes. The Chinese side has proposed a “dual‑track” approach that maintains current tariff levels while exploring a phased reduction in technology restrictions over a five‑year horizon. Both sides have acknowledged the need for a stable and predictable supply chain to support global economic growth.

What to watch

The immediate focus for investors will be the outcome of the U.S.–China trade and technology dialogue. Key agenda items are expected to include:

1. **Tariff schedules** – The USTR will present a phased plan for reducing tariffs on Chinese imports, while the Chinese side will seek a corresponding reduction in U.S. tariffs on Chinese goods. 2. **Technology‑access restrictions** – Both sides will discuss the scope of the Entity List and potential pathways for licensing critical technologies. 3. **Supply‑chain resilience** – The parties will explore mechanisms to ensure continuity of critical components, including joint task forces and data‑sharing agreements. 4. **Legal frameworks** – The USTR will review the Export Administration Regulations (EAR) and the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) sanctions regime, while Chinese officials will outline their own regulatory mechanisms.

Investors should monitor the official communiqués released after the talks, as well as any subsequent policy announcements from the USTR and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce. A clear policy shift could validate the market’s current rally, while a lack of progress could trigger a pullback in technology‑component stocks.
