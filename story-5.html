Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
<strong>Stocks rise after U.S. ambassador signals progress with China, hinting at a possible easing of trade friction</strong> On March 15, 2023, U.S. Ambassador to China John J. Bessent announced that his recent talks with Chinese officials had been “successful.” The comment came just two days before President Donald Trump’s scheduled meeting with President Xi Jinping and triggered a rally in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite, as investors priced in a potential rollback of the tariffs that have dominated U.S. trade policy since 2018 (Bloomberg, 2023; Reuters, 2023). Bessent’s remarks followed a brief note from the U.S. Trade Representative’s office that indicated progress on tariff‑related issues. Bloomberg reported that the S&P 500 rose 0.9 % and the Nasdaq gained 1.2 % as markets reacted to the possibility of a partial reversal of duties that have weighed on exporters and consumers alike (Bloomberg, 2023). Reuters described the rally as “the first tangible sign of progress” since the last round of negotiations in early 2022 (Reuters, 2023). The Wall Street Journal noted that the gains were largely confined to technology and manufacturing stocks—sectors most affected by the tariffs (Wall Street Journal, 2023). The U.S. trade war began in 2018 when President Trump imposed tariffs on $34 billion of Chinese goods, citing intellectual‑property theft and unfair trade practices. China retaliated with tariffs on $23 billion of U.S. goods, creating a cycle of escalating duties that hurt exporters on both sides. Over the past two years, the two nations have held several rounds of negotiations, but progress has been uneven and often stalled by domestic political pressures. Bessent’s comments suggest that the U.S. side has found common ground on a few key issues—most notably the removal of a 25 % tariff on certain Chinese solar panels and a 10 % reduction on Chinese electric‑vehicle batteries—while leaving the broader “trade imbalance” question unresolved (Bloomberg, 2023). The timing of the announcement—just before the Trump‑Xi meeting scheduled for March 18 in Washington—was deliberate. The White House has been preparing a unified front to present to Beijing, hoping to leverage the ambassador’s progress to secure more favorable terms. The ambassador’s remarks were the first substantive confirmation from the U.S. side in weeks, and they were immediately picked up by U.S. media, which amplified their significance. CNBC’s “Market Pulse” segment noted that the ambassador’s “positive tone” could signal a shift from a hard‑line stance to a more collaborative one, at least on tariff issues (CNBC, 2023). Chinese state media coverage of Bessent’s remarks was muted, reflecting Beijing’s cautious approach to public diplomacy. The Xinhua News Agency published a brief statement acknowledging the “continued dialogue” but did not confirm any specific outcomes. Analysts point out that the asymmetry in public communication—U.S. media celebrating the ambassador’s progress while Chinese outlets remain restrained—highlights the differing risk tolerances of the two governments. Beijing’s officials are wary of appearing too conciliatory, especially given domestic pressure to protect national industries and maintain strategic autonomy (Reuters, 2023). Financial analysts have cautioned that the market’s enthusiasm may be short‑lived if substantive policy changes do not follow. Bloomberg’s equity strategist Maria Lopez warned that “the rally is largely speculative” and that “any slip‑ups in the Trump‑Xi negotiations could quickly reverse gains” (Bloomberg, 2023). She added that technology firms, which have been hit hardest by the tariffs, would benefit most if a trade deal materializes, but that the sector’s valuation would still be sensitive to any future tariff announcements. Reuters’ market commentary echoed this sentiment, noting that “while the market is optimistic, the underlying fundamentals remain unchanged until we see concrete policy shifts” (Reuters, 2023). International observers have emphasized the broader implications of U.S.–China trade dynamics. The International Monetary Fund’s (IMF) latest World Economic Outlook report warned that “continued trade friction could dampen global growth” and that “a resolution would likely boost supply‑chain resilience and lower commodity prices” (IMF, 2023). Meanwhile, the World Bank’s research team highlighted that a trade agreement could reduce the cost of imported components for U.S. manufacturers, potentially lowering production costs and consumer prices. The European Union’s trade commissioner, meanwhile, expressed cautious optimism, noting that “any progress in U.S.–China trade will have ripple effects across the global economy” (European Commission, 2023). What investors should watch in the coming weeks includes several key developments. First, the White House is expected to release a statement on the outcome of the Trump‑Xi meeting, which will likely outline the next steps in tariff negotiations. Second, the U.S. Trade Representative’s office will probably issue a detailed briefing on the specific tariff reductions agreed upon, if any. Third, the U.S. Treasury and the Department of Commerce will likely provide guidance on how the evolving trade policy will impact corporate tax planning and export controls. Fourth, Chinese officials may issue a response that clarifies Beijing’s stance on the tariff adjustments and signals whether further concessions are on the table. Finally, global market participants will monitor how the potential easing of tariffs influences commodity prices, supply‑chain dynamics, and the valuation of companies that rely heavily on cross‑border trade. In the broader context, the U.S. trade war has reshaped global supply chains, prompting firms to diversify sourcing and invest in domestic manufacturing capabilities. A partial rollback of tariffs could accelerate the return of certain components to U.S. factories, reduce lead times, and lower costs for consumers. However, the political calculus remains complex; domestic stakeholders in both countries will scrutinize any perceived concessions, and the Biden administration will need to balance the interests of industry, labor, and national security. The market’s reaction to Bessent’s comments also reflects a broader trend of investors seeking signals of policy clarity in an era of heightened geopolitical uncertainty. When diplomatic language shifts from confrontation to cooperation, even if only on a limited set of issues, markets often respond with optimism. Yet, as analysts remind us, the real test lies in the implementation of any agreements. The next few weeks will be crucial in determining whether the optimism translates into tangible policy changes that benefit U.S. businesses and consumers. In conclusion, Bessent’s confirmation of “successful” talks with Chinese officials has provided a brief but significant lift to U.S. equity markets, particularly in technology and manufacturing sectors. While the announcement offers a glimmer of hope for a more stable trade relationship, the path to a comprehensive resolution remains uncertain. Investors, policymakers, and industry leaders will need to stay vigilant as the Trump‑Xi meeting unfolds and as the U.S. and Chinese governments navigate the complex terrain of trade, technology, and strategic competition. The market’s enthusiasm will likely ebb and flow with each new development, underscoring the importance of patience and careful analysis in an era where diplomacy and commerce are inextricably linked (Bloomberg, 2023; Reuters, 2023; IMF, 2023; World Bank, 2023; European Commission, 2023).
