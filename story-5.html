Stocks advance after Bessent says talks with China were successful before Trump

By Yahoo Finance · Sep 19, 2026
<article> **Stocks rally as Bessent announces successful talks with China ahead of Trump‑Xi summit** The U.S. equity market opened higher on Tuesday, with the S&P 500 posting a 0.9 % gain and the Nasdaq up 1.2 % after U.S. Trade Representative‑designate Bessent announced that preliminary talks with Chinese officials had reached a “breakthrough” before the scheduled Trump‑Xi summit. The announcement lifted investor sentiment on a day when trade tensions between the two largest economies had lingered since the 2018 tariff escalation. The rally was strongest in technology, consumer staples, and industrials, where shares of companies with significant U.S.‑China exposure jumped between 1.5 % and 2.3 % in early trade.

A quiet but decisive statement

Bessent, who was confirmed by the Senate in a 2023 hearing, delivered the news in a brief statement released by the U.S. Department of Commerce on Tuesday morning. “We have made significant progress on key issues, including intellectual‑property protections, market access, and the removal of non‑tariff barriers,” Bessent said. “These developments lay the groundwork for a constructive dialogue at the upcoming Trump‑Xi meeting.” The statement was issued just hours before the first U.S.–China trade conference call between senior officials, which was scheduled for 10:30 a.m. ET.

The comment came after a series of back‑to‑back meetings between the U.S. Office of the Trade Representative (OTR) and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce, which began in late May. According to a Reuters report, the talks focused on the enforcement of the U.S. Phase‑One trade agreement, the status of U.S. investment in China, and the protection of U.S. intellectual property. The U.S. has been seeking to address a range of concerns that it says remain unresolved under the 2020 Phase‑One deal, including the enforcement of the “China‑specific” provisions and the removal of certain subsidies that benefit Chinese technology firms.

Market reaction and sectoral gains

The market’s reaction was swift. The S&P 500 closed at 4,210.12, up 37.56 points, while the Nasdaq Composite finished at 13,145.67, up 158.23 points. The Dow Jones Industrial Average also gained, closing at 33,112.45, up 210.12 points. The gains were driven largely by technology and consumer staples. Shares of Apple, Microsoft, and Nvidia rose 1.6 %, 1.8 % and 2.3 % respectively, while Procter & Gamble and Coca‑Cola gained 1.5 % and 1.2 %. The industrials sector saw a 1.3 % rise, led by companies such as Caterpillar and 3M, which have significant U.S.‑China supply chains.

The U.S. dollar weakened slightly, falling 0.6 % against the euro and 0.4 % against the yen, reflecting a shift in risk sentiment. The Chinese yuan strengthened 0.2 % against the dollar, suggesting that Chinese investors were also optimistic about the upcoming summit.

Analyst perspectives

“Bessent’s statement is a clear signal that the U.S. is making headway in its trade negotiations with China,” said Jane Liu, senior equity strategist at Goldman Sachs. “If the summit confirms these gains, we expect a sustained rally in sectors that are most exposed to U.S.‑China trade dynamics.” Liu added that the market was “picking up the tailwind” for companies that have been hit hard by tariffs and supply‑chain disruptions.

Morgan Stanley’s research team noted that the market’s reaction was “largely driven by expectations of a de‑tariffing wave.” “We see the possibility of a partial rollback of tariffs on Chinese goods, especially in the consumer electronics and automotive sectors,” said analyst Thomas Chen. Chen cautioned that the market should remain wary of “political risk” and the potential for a “back‑sliding” of China’s policy stance.

Chinese reaction

The Chinese Ministry of Commerce issued a statement later that day, saying it welcomed the “positive signals” from the U.S. side and reaffirmed its commitment to “mutual benefit and win‑win” in trade relations. The statement highlighted the importance of the upcoming Trump‑Xi meeting for “deepening cooperation and resolving outstanding issues.” A spokesperson for the ministry said that China was “prepared to engage in constructive dialogue” and that it would “continue to work with the U.S. to promote a fair and balanced trade environment.”

China’s state‑owned media, Xinhua, reported that the Chinese government had been “preparing for a constructive outcome” and that it had “identified key areas where it can offer concessions.” The report noted that China had already taken steps to reduce tariffs on U.S. agricultural products and had begun to address some intellectual‑property concerns raised by U.S. firms.

Historical context

The U.S.–China trade war began in 2018 when President Trump announced a 25 % tariff on $50 billion worth of Chinese goods, followed by a 10 % tariff on an additional $200 billion. China responded with tariffs on U.S. goods, leading to a tit‑for‑tat escalation that lasted until the signing of the Phase‑One trade agreement in January 2020. The agreement, which included a $200 billion purchase commitment by China and a commitment to address intellectual‑property concerns, was seen as a partial success but left many U.S. concerns unresolved.

Since then, the U.S. has continued to impose tariffs on a range of Chinese goods, including steel, aluminum, and certain electronics. China has also been accused of engaging in unfair subsidies and state‑owned enterprises that give Chinese firms an advantage. The trade war has had a significant impact on global supply chains, with companies relocating production and diversifying sourcing.
